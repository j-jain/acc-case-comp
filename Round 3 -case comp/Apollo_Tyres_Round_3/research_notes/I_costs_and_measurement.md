# Apollo Tyres x ACC Round 3: costs and measurement reference (research notes, 6 Nov 2026)

Scope: research only, no programme design. 37 of 38 allowed searches used. Source numbers [S#] refer to the Source register at the end. Tags: [Published rate card], [Quoted in press], [Vendor / blog estimate - low reliability], [Academic / official], [Not found]. Finding tags: [Verified fact], [Single source], [Interpretation], [Unverified secondary claim]. Any "Data unavailable" line means exactly that: requires primary research or company confirmation.

## 1. Rural activation unit costs

### Takeaway
Only vendor and agency pages price rural activation; the one published rate card is the PAU Kisan Mela stall list (Rs 34,000-75,000 per stall). Van, haat, wall and nukkad figures are asking prices with unclear inclusions and should be carried as wide ranges.

### Cited Findings
- LED van rental listings on IndiaMART run about Rs 5,000-14,500 per day (Lucknow Rs 5,500 and Rs 6,000; a 12x8 ft three-side van Rs 13,500; Ludhiana hydraulic van Rs 14,500). Inclusion of fuel, driver, generator not stated. [Vendor / blog estimate - low reliability] [S1]
- A mobile-van agency estimates LED van advertising at Rs 8,000-18,000 per day in metros, cheaper in tier 2. [Vendor - low] [S2]. Media Ant lists Rs 10,500 per van per day (Hyderabad LED screen), printing and mounting extra. [Vendor - low] [S3]
- Pune rural-activation agency FAQ: a 30-day rural roadshow covering 100 villages with branded van, 4-person team and daily reporting "typically costs Rs 8-15 lakh". Dividing gives roughly Rs 8,000-15,000 per village (my calculation, not on the page). [Vendor - low] [S4]
- Same agency: nukkad natak, 3 weeks, 50 villages, Rs 5-10 lakh (about Rs 10,000-20,000 per village, my calculation); inclusions not stated. Haat and mandi activation Rs 15,000-30,000 per market day per location. [Vendor - low] [S4]
- Wall painting advertising: IndiaMART vendor lists Rs 6-25 per sq ft (outdoor Rs 6, commercial Rs 15, digital Rs 25); SmartAds state pages show about Rs 15-16 per sq ft with a 10,000 sq ft minimum, digital Rs 36 (Karnataka). A blog quotes Rs 5-15 per sq ft per month (unclear whether a per-month or one-off rate). Rates are asking prices; wall rental/permission cost to the owner is not separated. [Vendor - low] [S5, S6]
- PAU Ludhiana Kisan Mela, 18-19 Sep 2026: general stall Rs 48,000, strategic-point stall Rs 58,000, combo stall with hoarding Rs 75,000, stall behind Kairon Kisan Ghar Rs 34,000, route-side strategic stall Rs 50,000; includes two tables and four chairs, electricity extra. - GBPUAT Pantnagar Kisan Mela 2026: 30 ft x 30 ft ground Rs 26,000 including 18% GST. [Published rate card] [S8]
- A Hindustan Times report cited one Punjab farmer-camp function at a marriage palace costing Rs 1.5-2 lakh per day; ICAR farmer-meet reports show 120-150 attendees. These give a rough per-attendee figure of about Rs 1,000-1,700 for a hired-venue event (my calculation, a single anecdote). [Quoted in press] [S9, S10]

### Inferences
- The agency roadshow total divided by 100 villages implies a rural van-plus-team cost of roughly Rs 8,000-15,000 per village; this is the only cost-per-village anchor and it is one vendor's.

Village-tournament costs can only be built bottom-up from small components (umpires, balls, equipment) and entry-fee norms; no organiser budget, state-league sponsorship amount, or ex-cricketer appearance fee was found as a published figure. ## 6. Measurement method references

### Takeaway
The standard citations are Google's geo-experiment papers (Vaver and Koehler 2011, 2012 GBR; Kerman, Wang and Vaver 2017 time-based regression) and Meta's open-source GeoLift. With only a handful of districts, synthetic-control or synthetic-DiD style estimators with placebo inference are the documented route, but pre-period length, donor pool and spillover limit them.

### Cited Findings
- Google TBR paper ("Estimating Ad Effectiveness using Geo Experiments in a Time-Based Regression Framework", 2017; Google pages credit Wang and Vaver, other listings add Kerman) builds on Vaver and Koehler's 2011 and 2012 geo-based regression (GBR), which needs many geographic units; TBR was built for few geos, such as one treatment and one control region. I read only the abstract and secondary summaries. [Academic / official] [S56]
- Google Meridian GeoX documentation: fit an OLS pretest model of treatment-geo average on control-geo average (non-negative slope), predict the counterfactual, sum daily lifts, placebo-based inference; the page gives no minimum geos, test length or power rules. [Academic / official] [S57]
- Meta GeoLift: augmented synthetic control; GeoLiftMarketSelection runs power simulations to choose test number, duration and markets; N defaults to deciles of locations; default effect sizes 0-25% in 5% steps; alpha default 0.1; lookback window setting; superseded functions exist. [Academic / official] [S58]
- Synthetic DiD (Arkhangelsky et al. 2021) is described as suited to a few treated markets; plain DiD relies on parallel trends; synthetic control needs many pre-period observations and a sizable donor pool, and with few donors placebo standard errors can be optimistic; geo tests suffer spillover and media leakage. These points come from vendor and library documentation, not the primary paper. [Vendor / blog estimate - low reliability] [S59, S60]
- Cluster-randomised village trial in India: an AEA-registered four-arm digital advisory trial in Eastern Uttar Pradesh randomised 160 villages (40 per arm); power calculation assumed rice-productivity ICC 0.15 (India) and maize 0.18 (Uganda), minimum detectable effect 0.3 SD, with awareness and knowledge among primary outcomes. [Academic / official] [S61]
- A picture-based advisory trial in Punjab and Haryana randomised 203 villages in three arms and found knowledge gains but no short-run adoption change (reported by CGIAR; paper title not verified). Digital Green involvement in these trials was not verified. [Single source] [S62]
- Sample size for a proportion: n = z^2 p(1-p) / E^2 under simple random sampling; with clustering multiply by design effect Deff = 1 + (m-1) x ICC (m = average cluster size); a Deff of 1.5-2 is "reasonable" but larger is common; more clusters beat bigger clusters. No brand-awareness ICC benchmark was found. [Verified fact (standard statistics, two methodological pages)] [S63, S64]
- Attribution tools in rural India (coupons, missed-call numbers, QR codes, dealer sell-out data): no published source with costs or response rates found. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

### Inferences
- Worked illustration (my arithmetic, not a source figure): to detect awareness moving from 20% to 30% with 5% alpha (two-sided) and 80% power needs roughly 290 respondents per arm under simple random sampling; with 25 respondents per village and ICC 0.05, Deff is about 2.2, so about 640 per arm or roughly 26 villages per arm. The ICC is an assumption, and must be replaced by a pilot value.
- Sales outcomes need dealer sell-out data by district; Apollo's warranty registration by call centre is a possible partial channel but registers only a share of sales.

### Gaps
- Minimum geos/length guidance from the Google papers (full text not read); an ICC for brand awareness; any rural-India marketing geo-experiment case.

## 7. Farmer incentive rules and norms

### Takeaway
The Prize Chits and Money Circulation Schemes (Banning) Act, 1978 targets prize chits and money-circulation schemes, not ordinary free-entry promotions, but lucky draws are state-sensitive (Tamil Nadu bans them) and consumer-protection rules on disclosure apply. No tyre-maker camp or exchange offer with published cost or result was found.

### Cited Findings
- The 1978 Act bans promotion or conduct of prize chits and money-circulation schemes; s.2(e) defines prize chit inclusively; s.11 exempts state-promoted schemes; the Supreme Court upheld the Act's constitutionality and held the Peerless endowment scheme outside it. (I could not open the statute text directly: indiacode returned 403.) [Academic / official (case summaries)] [S65, S66]
- A law commentary says: Tamil Nadu bans lucky-draw contests under the Tamil Nadu Prize Schemes (Prohibition) Act, 1979; lottery under the Lotteries (Regulation) Act, 1998 needs chance, consideration and prize, so a free-entry draw arguably falls outside; the Prize Competitions Act 1955 covers puzzle contests; consumer-protection law can treat undisclosed or misleading draws as unfair trade practice; the Supreme Court Horlicks decision found no lottery where no draw of lots and no price charged. A forum claim that 13 states ban lotteries is unverified. [Quoted in press / law commentary] [S67, S68]
- The usual "no purchase necessary" practice and any ASCI guideline on contests: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation (no source addressed it directly).
- Tyre-maker precedents: CEAT organised 15 "Aayushmaan Tractor Meets" (farmer awareness camps) in Uttar Pradesh till December 2016, no cost or result published; JK Tyre 2012 farmer tyre-care campaign said to benefit about 20,000 farmers across seven states, no cost; CEAT has a Maharashtra tyre exchange offer with a legal terms PDF (not read). No Apollo or BKT camp or exchange-offer data; no cost or sales result for any. [Quoted in press] [S69, S70, S71]

### Inferences
- A free-entry, skill-or-quiz or guaranteed-benefit structure with disclosed terms and Tamil Nadu excluded is the conventional low-risk shape, but this is an interpretation; a legal opinion is needed before any prize draw is designed.

### Gaps
- Statute text and state-by-state list; ASCI contest guidance; any measured result of a tyre camp or exchange offer.
