## Q1. Market size and structure (units, value, OEM / replacement / export, rear / front, bias / radial, shares)

### Takeaway
No credible public source gives a current (FY25/FY26) size of the Indian farm-tyre market in units or rupees, or its OEM / replacement, rear / front or bias / radial split. ATMA does not publish this openly, and ICRA, CRISIL and CARE do not break out farm tyres in their public releases. Company shares are not published by any neutral body. The one specific figure is about 30% for Goodyear India (HDFC Securities, 2022). BKT claims "over 20%" for itself (2026). The best proxy for OEM demand is a record year for tractors: about 11.6 lakh domestic wholesale units in FY26.

### Cited findings
- **Whole-industry structure (not farm-specific).** CRISIL Ratings, 24 Aug 2026: the aftermarket (replacement) is about 50% of tyre volumes, OEM about 25%, exports about 25%. The top six tyre makers make up about 85% of sector revenue of about Rs 1.36 lakh crore (FY26). FY27 volume growth is expected at 4-5% [Forecast]. Margins are expected to dip from about 14.2% to about 12% because input costs are up 35-40% — [S7]. An earlier CRISIL release (Aug 2023) gave replacement 60%, OEM 30%, exports 10% — [S6]. The two splits differ by year and basis, so cite each with its date. [Single source each]
- **Concentration.** The CCI found that five makers (Apollo, MRF, CEAT, JK, Birla) controlled "over 90%" of Indian tyre production. The finding is in the order dated 31 Aug 2018, which was made public in Feb 2022, and covers conduct in 2011-12. This is all tyres, not farm — [S4]. [Verified fact: CCI order reported by several outlets]
- **Tractor OEM demand proxy.** FY26 domestic tractor wholesale (TMA) was about 11,60,231 units, up 23.47%. FADA retail crossed 10 lakh for the first time (10,50,077 against 8,82,825) — [S29]. [Single source: snippet; corroborated by several outlets in search results] [Interpretation] Every new tractor takes two front and two rear tyres, so OEM farm-tyre demand rose roughly in line in FY26.
- **Tax change.** GST on tractor tyres was cut from 18% to 5% in Sept 2025, against 28% to 18% for most other tyres — [S5]. CEAT expected this to lift tractor-tyre sales. [Single source]
- **Market-research vendor sizes (LOW reliability, flagged).** The 2024 India agri-tyre market is put at USD 270 mn (IMARC, 4.2% CAGR to 2033), USD 521 mn (Credence, "agri OTR"), or USD 1.2 bn (DRI/TechSci, 5.75% CAGR to 2030) — [S30]. A 4x spread means the definitions are not comparable. Do not use any of these as a headline number. [Unverified secondary claim]
- **Old ATMA unit data (historic only).** One ATMA production period (reported Mar 2006): tractor front tyres 121,150 units, rear 96,040. Radialisation rates were "not contained" in ATMA data — [S40]. A TechSci report (c. 2009-14) said farm tyres are about 80% of India's OTR market, and that trailer tyres are sold only in replacement — [S41]. [Single source, dated]
- **Company shares (all are claims, not neutral data):**
  - Goodyear India: "~30% market share" in farm tyres; "market leader in tractor tyre segment" (HDFC Securities initiating coverage, 14 Dec 2022) — [S8]. "Over 30% market share in tractor tyres" (Leveraged Growth report, Dec 2022, low reliability) — [S19]. Goodyear's own FY26 MD&A says it "sustained its leadership position in the Farm business" but gives no number — [S11]. [Single source for the ~30% figure: two broker documents of the same date, probably not independent]
  - BKT: Rajiv Poddar said "in the agricultural market of India, we have over 20 per cent" (EVO India, 25 Feb 2026) — [S1]. In 2020 he gave 7-8% of Indian *off-highway* tyres — [S2]. The two conflict: different years and different denominators. [Single source, company claim]
  - MRF: no share figure. MRF's FY26 MD&A cites an "increasing share of business with tractor manufacturers" — [S26]. A 2019 MRF press release calls MRF the "most preferred Farm Tyre Brand amongst Tractor owners", based on J.D. Power's 2018 Tractor Tyre Satisfaction Index — [S17].
  - Apollo: retailer blogs claim "over 25%". These are unsourced [Unverified secondary claim] (seen in a search snippet from tractorforeveryone.com; not used).
  - CEAT, JK, TVS Eurogrip, Birla: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- **BKT's own mix (global, standalone volumes).** Agriculture was 58.8% of FY26 volumes of 317,356 MT (60.5% FY24, 59.9% FY25, 59.5% Q1FY27). Replacement was 70.1% in FY26 and 71.6% in Q1FY27 — [S3]. [Verified fact: primary investor deck]

### Inferences
- [Interpretation] The figures on record point to two leaders in Indian farm tyres: Goodyear (OEM-anchored) and Apollo (replacement-strong), with MRF a close contender. BKT's "over 20%" claim is hard to reconcile with its 2020 figure of 7-8% of all off-highway tyres. Treat the "number one" claims from Goodyear, MRF and BKT as marketing, not measurement.
- [Interpretation] The farm-tyre share of total company revenue gives a rough guide to how much each rival depends on farm. Goodyear: about 50% (analyst estimate via search snippet, Trendlyne). CEAT: about 10% (S5). BKT India: small (S3). MRF and JK: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

### Gaps
- Market units and value (FY25/FY26); OEM / replacement / export split for farm tyres; rear / front / trailer split; bias / radial share. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. Possible routes: the ATMA members' statistical bulletin (paid / members only), the TMA, or paid ICRA / CRISIL sector reports.
- A neutral, dated share table by company: none found.

## Q2-3. Competitor set and profiles

### Takeaway
On the evidence found, Goodyear India is the strongest farm specialist in OEM fitment and in its own claims. MRF is the strongest on durability perception (J.D. Power 2018) and on replacement brand pull. CEAT and JK are using farm launches and rural retail formats to push. BKT is an export giant whose Indian farm push is mostly brand-led, and it now uses cricket (IPL teams, R. Ashwin). Radials are still a niche in India: CEAT, TVS Eurogrip and BKT have the radial capability, mostly aimed at export.

### Cited findings: by company

**Goodyear India (farm specialist)**
- Product: Vajra Super and Sampurna (rear). Ultra Grip launched 1 Jul 2026 in 14.9-28 only, with "deeper tread", "puncture-resistant", "cut- and chip-resistant compound" — [S9]. Prices on TractorGyan, 6 Nov 2026: Vajra Super 13.6x28 Rs 17,440 — [S14]. TractorJunction, accessed 6 Nov 2026: Vajra Super 6.00x16 front Rs 3,600, 12.4x28 Rs 15,500 — [S15]. [Single source: portal prices, asterisked "may vary by state"]
- Warranty: "6 years from the date they are made" or until 85% of the tread is worn (TractorGyan Q&A, 14 May 2025) — [S37]. [Unverified secondary claim: no Goodyear document found]
- OEM: original equipment supplier to M&M, TAFE, Escorts, ITL (Sonalika), John Deere and Eicher (HDFC Sec, Dec 2022) — [S8]. Its FY26 MD&A says Farm OE will focus on "deepening key account relationships" — [S11]. [Single source]
- Distribution: Ultra Grip was shown to about 100 dealers and channel partners in Chandigarh — [S9]. The farm replacement priority is "channel expansion, enhanced channel engagement" — [S11]. Its total or farm dealer count: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

On 5 Dec 2025 the parent concluded the review and kept the business — [S10]. [Verified fact: company disclosure reported by Tyre Trends and Autocar Pro]

## Q4. How the leaders acquire farmers without relying on cricket (documented initiatives)

### Takeaway
The documented non-cricket farmer playbook is thin and mostly old. It consists of: tractor-OEM fitment (Goodyear); farmer awareness meets with local tyre and tractor dealers (CEAT, 2016); agri-expo launches with live demonstrations (JK 2026, TVS 2024 at Krishi Darshan Hisar); rural retail formats (JK Steel Wheels 2025, CEAT's FMCG-style outlets); rural sport (BKT kabaddi from 2018); and durability claims backed by third-party satisfaction rankings (MRF and J.D. Power 2018). BKT is the clearest case of a rival that began with rural non-cricket sport and later added heavy cricket.

### Cited findings
- **OEM fitment as acquisition.** J.D. Power found satisfaction higher when buyers chose the OE tyre brand on a new tractor — [S17]. Goodyear supplies "almost all leading tractor manufacturers" — [S8]. Sales of new tractors: about 11.6 lakh in FY26 — [S29]. [Interpretation] OE fitment puts the brand on the farm, and that drives later replacement choice.
- **Farmer meets.** CEAT ran 15 Aayushmaan Tractor Meets in UP (Oct-Dec 2016), with tyre-care education, product showcases and local dealers present — [S32].
- **Expo demonstrations.** JK: Shresth Plus launch, stall and live tractor stunts at Krishi Darshan Expo, Hisar, Feb 2026 — [S12]. TVS Eurogrip: Tigertrac radial display at the same expo, Feb 2024 — [S20].
- **Dealer launch events.** Goodyear: Ultra Grip shown to about 100 dealers in Chandigarh, Jul 2026 — [S9].
- **Rural retail formats.** JK Steel Wheels (May 2025, towns of 1 lakh people or fewer, entrepreneur-run) — [S36]. CEAT targets outlets in villages of 5,000-10,000 people through kiranas and puncture shops (2022, two-wheeler context) — [S33].
- **Rural sport.** BKT has backed Pro Kabaddi since 2018, with farmers taken to matches and the tagline "Desh Ki Dharti Se Juda" — [S18]. It also sponsored the Kila Raipur "Rural Olympics" (2019) — search snippet, [Single source].
- **Durability proof.** MRF publicises its J.D. Power No.1 ranking and its lead on durability — [S17].

### Inferences
- [Interpretation] No rival was found publishing results (sales or share) from a farmer-marketing programme. The case therefore has no outcome benchmark beyond OEM fitment and the claimed satisfaction rankings.

### Gaps
- Mechanic or retailer loyalty apps for farm tyres, WhatsApp or YouTube farmer content, influencer use, and kisan-mela calendars for MRF, Goodyear and BKT. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

## Q5. Trade economics (dealer margins, incentives, credit, multi-brand dealers, steering)

### Takeaway
No authoritative, current figure for tyre-dealer margins, turnover discounts or credit days in India was found. What exists is anecdotal or about manufacturers. The evidence does show three things: dealer margins have long been contested (the AITDF complaint led to the CCI case); manufacturers run complex scheme-based incentives; and at least one maker has tilted margins toward exclusive stores over multi-brand dealers.

### Cited findings
- **Dealer margins.** 8-20% depending on volume and category, 8-15% for MRF (franchise blog, undated, LOW reliability) — [S23]. [Unverified secondary claim]
- **Dealer federation.** In 2010 the All India Tyre Dealers' Federation alleged that tyre majors' practices "controlling the profit margins of over 11,000 tyre dealers". The dealer complaint triggered the CCI investigation (June 2010) — [S16][S4]. [Single source for the 11,000 figure]
- **CCI facts.** The order found that companies exchanged price-sensitive data through ATMA, which "collected and compiled company-wise and segment-wise data on production, domestic sales and exports". Penalties: MRF Rs 622.09 crore, Apollo Rs 425.53 crore, JK Rs 309.95 crore, CEAT Rs 252.16 crore, Birla Rs 178.33 crore. NCLAT remanded the matter in Dec 2022 to re-examine the penalties — [S4]. [Verified fact] [Interpretation] Segment-wise data, farm included, exists at ATMA but is not public.
- **Exclusive vs multi-brand.** An XLRI case (2023, unnamed maker) describes margins changed "to favour exclusive stores at the cost of multi-brand dealers", then a recommendation to rebalance for economy tyres — [S21]. CEAT says 50% of its sales go through its exclusive channel — [S24]. JK has about 900 exclusive brand shops against 6,000+ channel partners — [S36].
- **Schemes.** A JK Tyre / Vistex case study: manual administration of schemes caused errors and payout delays for distributors; automation standardised incentives across regions — [S22]. [Single source: vendor case study]
- **Credit and street pricing (anecdotal).** Credit periods are common; tyres sell below MRP; margins are better in smaller towns with less competition (Team-BHP forum) — [S42]. [Unverified secondary claim]
- **Manufacturer margins (context, not dealer).** CRISIL expects FY27 operating margin of about 12%, down from about 14.2%, with "calibrated" price hikes — [S7].

### Inferences
- [Interpretation] Rural farm-tyre retail appears to be largely multi-brand, given the AITDF's 11,000 dealers against company exclusive-store counts in the hundreds (JK about 900). The share of rural multi-brand outlets is not quantified.
- [Interpretation] How far dealers steer brand choice is not measured in any public source found.

### Gaps
- Percentage margins on farm tyres, turnover-discount slabs, credit days, prevalence of multi-brand dealers in rural markets, dealer influence on brand choice. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. A dealer survey is the realistic route.

## Q6. Competitive weaknesses, barriers and likely responses

### Cited findings
- **Goodyear.** Heavily dependent on farm (about 50% of revenue, analyst estimate); "limited presence in the Commercial tyre category", which restricts portfolio selling (FY26 MD&A) — [S11]. Two years of ownership uncertainty (sale review Apr-Dec 2025) — [S10]. Ultra Grip is in one size only so far — [S9].
- **MRF.** Left the ICC deal in May 2024 — [S35]. No disclosed farm-specific distribution or farmer-marketing programme found — [S26].
- **CEAT.** Farm is only about 10% of revenue — [S5]. Farm OEM growth was weaker (mid-single-digit) in Q1 FY26 — [S24]. Its cricket spend is aimed at 4-wheeler and premium buyers — [S24].
- **BKT.** India has historically been 5-10% of sales — [S18]. It relies on distributors, not dealers — [S1]. Its 2026 IPL and Ranveer Singh push is for consumer (non-farm) tyres — [S27]. Management attention is split by the on-highway entry — [S3].
- **JK.** No farm-share disclosure. Steel Wheels rollout numbers after May 2025 are unknown — [S36].
- **Pricing response.** CRISIL says margins are compressing and price hikes are staggered — [S7]. [Interpretation] Rivals have limited room for deep farm price cuts in FY27, but historical parallel pricing (the CCI finding, [S4]) suggests prices tend to move together.
- **Barriers to copying.** OEM key-account ties (Goodyear's six named OEMs, [S8]); BKT's multi-year sports portfolio ([S18][S27]); CEAT's 5-year IPL contract to about 2028 ([S34]); JK's entrepreneur-run rural centres ([S36]). [Interpretation]

### Gaps
- Regional strength by state for each rival (out of scope here; covered by another researcher). Service infrastructure for farm tyres. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
