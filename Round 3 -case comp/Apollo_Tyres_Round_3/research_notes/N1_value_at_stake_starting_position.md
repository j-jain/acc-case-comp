## Q1. Market size and structure (units, tonnes, channel split)

### Takeaway
The only usable segment data is CRISIL MI&A's industry chapter in the Tolins Tyres RHP (Aug 2024 report): **domestic tractor tyre demand was 7.7 million tyres in FY24, split almost evenly between OEM (3.8 m) and replacement (3.9 m)**, plus **5.6 million farm/agri tyres exported** (front, rear and trailer). Imports are negligible. No tonnage split, no front/rear/trailer split and no FY25/FY26 update was found.

### Inferences
- Domestic farm tyre demand is roughly half replacement, half OEM in units - not the 25/75 split in the Motilal note (see Q3).
- Demand is cyclical with tractor sales (FY20 5.2 m to FY23 8.0 m), but replacement is steadier than OEM (FY24 replacement rose while OEM fell).
- Exports (5.6 m units, Rs ~9,800 cr) are as large as the domestic market in value but are a different product (large agri sizes for Europe/US), so they belong outside the "rural India" prize.

- No FY25 or FY26 tractor tyre unit data; no tonnage; no front/rear/trailer split; JK Tyre QIP document's industry section not retrieved (filing not found in search). ATMA and Rubber Board category tables not accessible.

## Q3. Apollo's farm mix and starting position

### Takeaway
The "25% replacement-driven" line sits in a list of Apollo management's own segment comments and most likely describes **Apollo's** farm business, not the industry (the industry is ~50% replacement domestically). If so, Apollo is OEM-heavy and under-represented in farm replacement. Apollo says farm replacement grew strongly in H2 FY26 and stayed "surprisingly strong" in Q1 FY27, while plants run above 90% and Apollo is considering outsourcing agri tyres rather than adding capacity.

## Q7. Rural tyre prize across categories

### Takeaway
Through the same rural dealers, the two-wheeler and car replacement pools are larger than farm: on an illustrative retail-value basis, rural 2W replacement is about Rs 8,000 cr and rural PV about Rs 5,900 cr, against about Rs 3,000-3,400 cr for rural tractor replacement. But Apollo already holds ~20% of PV replacement nationally and buys in its 2W replacement tyres, so the size of each pool and Apollo's ability to supply it point in different directions.

## Q6. Replacement demand

### Takeaway
CRISIL puts farm replacement at 3.9 m tyres in FY24, now slightly above OEM (ratio 1.03, up from 0.59 in FY21). No company states a replacement cycle; blogs say 4-6 years, but the park-based arithmetic implies 9-11 years per tyre on average.

### Cited Findings
- [Computed from CRISIL Figure 20] Replacement/OEM ratio: FY19 0.80; FY20 0.71; FY21 0.59; FY22 0.92; FY23 0.86; FY24 1.03.
- [Unverified secondary claim] Tractor tyres "may last 4-6 years on average" (TractorGyan blog, https://tractorgyan.com/tractor-industry-news-blogs/2444/when-to-replace-tractor-tyres-signs-farmers-should-not-ignore); Birla Tyres blog: replace a tyre "in use for more than 5 years and has gone through several repairs" (https://birlatyre.com/blogs/agricultural-bias-tyres-when-you-can-repair-and-when-to-replace).
- [Computed] Implied average interval = (park x 4 tyres) / 3.9 m: 9 m tractors gives 9.2 years; 11 m gives 11.3 years.
- Company or industry statement on replacement frequency for farm tyres: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

### Inferences
- [Interpretation] The gap between 4-6 years and the implied 9-11 years means one or more of: a smaller working park; rear tyres replaced much more often than fronts; long use of worn or retreaded tyres; unorganised volume missing from CRISIL. This is a key primary-research question (dealer and farmer interviews).

## Triangulation

All values are rupees per year for the **domestic** farm (tractor) tyre market; exports excluded. Retail-price values include dealer margin; manufacturer-level values would be lower by an unknown dealer margin and OEM discount (Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation).

**Demand side** (units x price) [Computed]
1. Base year FY24, fully sourced units: 7.7 m tyres (CRISIL). Price per tyre = average of one front and one rear at portal prices: low (3,000 + 16,000)/2 = Rs 9,500; high (3,600 + 17,700)/2 = Rs 10,650.
   - Total: 7.7 m x 9,500 = Rs 7,315 cr; x 10,650 = Rs 8,201 cr. Ex-GST at 5%: Rs 6,967-7,810 cr.
   - Replacement 3.9 m: Rs 3,705-4,154 cr retail (Rs 3,529-3,956 cr ex-GST). OEM 3.8 m: Rs 3,610-4,047 cr at retail prices (real OEM prices lower).
2. Rolled forward to FY26 (labelled assumptions):
   - OEM: FY26 domestic wholesale 11,60,231 x 4.0 tyres = 4.64 m (low) to x 4.55 tyres (FY23 CRISIL OEM tyres per domestic tractor) = 5.28 m (high).
   - Replacement: 3.9 m grown for two years at the CRISIL-implied FY24-FY29P CAGR range, (4/3.9)^(1/5)-1 = 0.5% to (6/3.9)^(1/5)-1 = 9.0%: 3.94-4.63 m.
   - Total FY26: 8.58-9.91 m tyres; at Rs 9,500-10,650 = **Rs 8,151-10,554 cr retail; Rs 7,763-10,051 cr ex-GST**. Replacement alone: Rs 3,743-4,931 cr retail.
- Weakest link: price per tyre (one SKU per position, retail not manufacturer price, replacement mix may be rear-heavy, which would raise value) and the FY26 roll-forward (CRISIL has no FY25-FY26 actuals). Also, CRISIL's count may exclude trailer/implement tyres.

**Supply side** (company revenues) [Computed]
- Inputs: Apollo farm Rs 1,189 cr (6% x 19,816; includes exports); Goodyear farm ~Rs 1,176 cr (50% x 2,351; share from 2016); CEAT ~Rs 1,568 cr (10% x 15,678; includes export agri radials); Goodyear share ~30% (2023 broker); BKT >20% (company claim 2026).
- Low: Goodyear anchor: 1,176 / 0.30 = **Rs ~3,900 cr**.
- High (if Apollo's and CEAT's farm revenues were all domestic): M ≥ 1,189 + 1,176 + 1,568 + 0.20 M (BKT) gives M ≥ 3,933 / 0.8 = **Rs ~4,900 cr**, before MRF, JK, Birla, TVS and others, so the true high end is open.
- Weakest link: Goodyear's farm share of revenue (2016 figure) and the unknown export share of Apollo's and CEAT's farm revenue. The supply side does not close: Goodyear's ~30% cannot be reconciled with Apollo + CEAT at face value.

**Side by side**
| Basis | Low | High | Weakest link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand, FY24 units, retail ex-GST | Rs 6,967 cr | Rs 7,810 cr | price per tyre; retail vs manufacturer price |
| Demand, FY26 roll-forward, retail ex-GST | Rs 7,763 cr | Rs 10,051 cr | roll-forward assumptions + price |
| Supply, manufacturer revenue | Rs ~3,900 cr | Rs ~4,900 cr + unlisted players | Goodyear farm revenue; export content of Apollo/CEAT |

- [Interpretation] The two sides meet only if manufacturer revenue is roughly 35-50% below retail ex-GST value (dealer margin + OEM discount), or if the supply side is missing Rs 2,000-4,000 cr of revenue from MRF, JK, Birla and others. A defensible working range for the domestic market at manufacturer prices is **Rs ~4,000-7,000 cr (FY26)**, with replacement about half of units and probably more than half of value.
- [Computed] Apollo's implied share of domestic value: Rs 1,189 cr / Rs 7,763-10,051 cr (FY26 retail ex-GST) = 11.8-15.3%; / Rs 3,900-4,900 cr (supply side) = 24-30%. The low end fits the competitor claims (Goodyear ~30%, BKT >20%) better. Two biases offset each other: Apollo's figure includes exports (overstates) but is at manufacturer price (understates against a retail denominator).
- [Computed, if Motilal's 25% is Apollo's mix] Apollo farm replacement Rs ~297 cr / replacement market Rs 3,565-4,696 cr (FY26 retail ex-GST) = **6.3-8.3%**, well below its estimated overall farm share and below its ~20% PV replacement share.
