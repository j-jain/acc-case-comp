# U1. What drives the replacement tyre choice, and who makes it: the published evidence (verified 8 Nov 2026)
Research note for the replacement-decision analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case. Feeds `..\Replacement_Decision\`.

**Scope.** Published evidence on what drives a tyre replacement and who makes the choice: tractor tyres first, then Indian tyres generally, original-equipment (OE) pull-through, farm tyres abroad, replacement tyres abroad, and a short note on theory. Not repeated here: farmer advice sources and the lubricant study (see 04 sections 1, 5 and 6), the decision journey and J.D. Power's 2018 tractor-tyre findings (see M04 sections 3 to 5), and input-dealer and contractor evidence (see Q5 section 2).

**How to read.** Each row gives the source as printed, the sample, a verbatim quote, a label and the day seen. Where a page refused a direct fetch (HTTP 403), the row says so and the quote is from the search engine's extract of that page. Labels: [Verified fact] primary, read; [Fact - single source]; [Reported by press]; [Benchmark - external]; [Outside-in; not a survey]; [Computed]; [Interpretation].

**What matters most** [Interpretation]

1. No published study, in India or abroad, measures who chooses a tractor tyre at replacement. Section 7's split is an analogue-based estimate.
2. The owner's experience of the outgoing tyre is the best-evidenced driver of rebuying. Uneven wear cuts repurchase intent for a tractor-tyre brand to 18%.
3. Shops say they set the brand for most buyers: 46% to 52.5% name no brand and a further 26% are switched (US trade surveys, trucks 2003 and cars 2011; the 2025 poll gives dealers' "most often" answers). Owners say only 17% (US) to 36% (Japan) follow the salesperson.
4. OE pull-through is under half in mature car markets: 37% (US, 2017) to 42% (US, 2026) of owners replacing two or more tyres. No farm-tyre figure exists anywhere.
5. CEAT's marketing head calls a tyre purchase unplanned and the garage mechanic "the influencer" (2020); a trade writer calls decisions "often dealer-led" (2026). Nobody has published a share. (Corrected 8 November 2026 after the round-8 fact-check: the "still very influential" line is an interviewer's question, see rows 2.8 and 2.9.)

**Application** [Interpretation]. A tractor tyre meets the conditions under which these papers expect the adviser to decide: it is bought every few years, so the owner's knowledge goes stale; its quality shows only after hundreds of hours; the mechanic who says it must go also steers the replacement; and brands look alike on the shelf. Two forces pull the other way: a branded rear pair costs Rs 32,000 to 35,000 (see M04 section 5.3), which raises involvement, and tyre life is visible to neighbours. Farmers follow advice only when the adviser seems disinterested (see 04 section 5), and Japanese buyers who took the salesperson's brand were less satisfied (row 5.5). The channel's sway is large but conditional on trust.

### 7(b) Who decides: best estimate

**Owner-led**: the owner arrives with a brand and buys it. **Channel-led**: he arrives without one, or names one but buys what the dealer or mechanic proposes.

| Purchase | Mainly owner | Mainly dealer or mechanic | Basis | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Answer** [Interpretation]: the replacement is consumer-led in its reasons and channel-led in its execution. The owner's experience decides whether he leaves a brand; the shop and mechanic mostly decide which brand he moves to, and almost always do when the purchase is urgent.

### 7(c) What OE pull-through implies for Apollo

1. **Benchmarks.** US owners replacing two or more tyres chose the factory brand 37% (2017) to 42% (2026) of the time; mass-market car owners 31%. Indian car owners state 46% to 65%. No farm figure exists. [Benchmark - external]
2. **Apollo's implied ceiling** [Computed]. If Apollo tyres are on a share S of tractors reaching first replacement and Apollo wins 9% of replacement, its pull-through cannot exceed 9% divided by S, since part of the 9% is won from rivals' fitments. S of 20% gives a ceiling of 45%; 30% gives 30%; 40% gives 23%. At S of 30% or more, Apollo keeps fewer of its own fitments than the weakest US car segment. Apollo's OE share of the tractor parc: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
3. **Why a tractor's OE tyre pulls through weakly** [Interpretation]. The owner mostly did not choose it (1.1), so fitment gives familiarity, not commitment. The first replacement comes at wear-out, when US loyalty falls (3.3). Many first replacements are bought by a second owner (64% of Punjab-belt tractors were bought used, see M04 section 2.1). And in an urgent purchase the shop's stock decides.
4. **For the diagnosis.** The evidence supports "fitted at the factory, lost at the first replacement" and places the loss in two spots: the verdict on the outgoing tyre's life, and the shop and mechanic at purchase. Neither is fixed by awareness alone, consistent with the jersey not moving search share (see M04 section 4).
5. **To measure.** Ask owners of Apollo-fitted tractors three to eight years old which brand they bought at first replacement, whether it was planned or urgent, and who named the brand.

## 9. Cautions and discrepancies

1. **India's OE-preference series does not reconcile:** 57% (2012) to 65% (2014) per MRF; 54% (2013) to 46% (2017) per Tyrepress; 48% (2017) to 54% (2018) per J.D. Power. The 2017 value is 46% or 48% by source, and "retention" and "preference" may be different measures. All are stated by owners of one- to two-year-old cars. The 2016 figure of 94% includes "probably would".
2. **US measures differ.** In 2026, 54% is overall loyalty; 42% applies to two or more tyres. Compare 42% with 2017's 37%.
3. **Who answers changes the answer.** Dealers report setting the brand for 72% to 78% of buyers (2003, 2011); owners credit the salesperson 17% to 36% of the time. The 2025 MTD figure is a straw poll of its readers.
4. **AutoPacific is internally consistent** (about half know the brand) but conflicts with MTD's "70/30 rule".
5. **Most analogues are cars in rich markets.** An Indian tractor owner spends far more relative to income, buys in pairs, faces a rural shop with few brands and often buys on credit. Analogues were adjusted by judgement, not data.
6. **Row 1.1's 38% concerns the new tractor, not a replacement;** the 18% applies only to owners reporting uneven wear.
7. **Search-extract figures** (4.4's 32% and 53%, 4.5's 75%, 5.10's shares) were not read in full.
8. **Interested sources.** CEAT and BKT run dealer and mechanic programmes; MRF's releases promote MRF; Autobei sells its reports and discloses no samples.
9. **Consumer Reports' reasons overlap,** so failure-led purchases in the US lie somewhere between 14% and 32%; this is not a tractor figure.
