# M05. How to win: the offer, the trade and the brand

Prepared 6 November 2026. Part of the McKinsey-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis. It treats the problem as a commercial system (offer, price, channel, brand), which is how a strategy consulting team would treat it, and not only as a marketing programme. It is not McKinsey's work.

**Purpose.** To state Apollo's offer in the tractor owner's own economics, to work out what would move a dealer and a mechanic, and to fund the offer, which the first analysis did not.

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **Sell hours, not tyres.** Owners judge tyres by how long they last. The offer should be a number of hours, guaranteed. [Strategic hypothesis]
2. **Back it with a guarantee that is paid at the counter.** Refused claims are the commonest complaint across brands. One maker, Trelleborg, launched an hours-based guarantee on tractor tyres in 2026. [Outside-in; Verified fact]
3. **Hold the price.** Apollo already lists 6 to 10% below MRF and Goodyear. A cut is unaffordable and unnecessary. [Portal price; Interpretation]
4. **Make the dealer and the mechanic better off for recommending Apollo.** A dealer earns roughly Rs 1,100 to 1,900 on a rear tyre; the first analysis offered the mechanic about Rs 200 a purchase, which is too thin. [Computed; Planning assumption]
5. **Start with the owners Apollo already has:** tractors that left the factory on Apollo tyres. [Strategic hypothesis]
6. **Say "Apollo" first.** The jersey builds one name; the tyre should carry it. [Interpretation]

## 2. The value map

### 2.1 What can and cannot be compared

Indian tyre makers do not publish tread depth, tyre weight or lug count for the common sizes (only Apollo's Virat 23 states its 23 lugs). So "price per millimetre of tread" cannot be computed for any Indian tractor tyre. Every 13.6-28 rear tyre seen is a 12-ply bias tyre with the same load rating. ### 2.2 Cost per hour

Tyre cost per hour = price divided by hours of life. Life figures are makers' and portals' claims (1,500 to 4,000 hours); no measured Indian figure exists. [Computed on unverified inputs]

| Tyre | Price | At 1,500 h | At 2,000 h | At 2,500 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market-channel tyre | Rs 8,000 | Rs 5.33 | Rs 4.00 | Rs 3.20 |
| Apollo Krishak Gold | Rs 16,000 | Rs 10.67 | Rs 8.00 | Rs 6.40 |
| MRF Shakti Super | Rs 17,670 | Rs 11.78 | Rs 8.84 | Rs 7.07 |

What the arithmetic says:

- **Against MRF:** at equal life Apollo is about 9% cheaper per hour. MRF has to last about 2,200 hours where Apollo lasts 2,000 to justify its price. Apollo does not need to claim longer life than MRF; it needs owners to believe its life is equal. [Computed]
- **Against the cheap tier:** a tyre at half the price wins unless it lasts less than half as long. Apollo cannot win that argument on price; it can win it with heavy users for whom a failure in season costs working days. [Interpretation]
- **Each 100 hours is worth about Rs 800** to the owner of a Rs 16,000 tyre that lasts 2,000 hours. That is the value of proof. [Computed]
- **The radial:** Farm King must last 1.41 times as long as a bias tyre to break even on tyre cost. Apollo claims 1.5 times. The margin is thin, and no Indian field trial of radial against bias tractor tyres was found. Foreign trials show fuel savings of about 3 to 7%. The radial is an offer for the highest-hour users, not the lead. [Computed; Fact - single source each]

A field trial with an agricultural university would be cheap and would give Apollo the first Indian evidence on tyre life and fuel. [Strategic hypothesis]

## 3. The guarantee

### 3.1 Why a guarantee

| Reason | Evidence | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Tyre life is what owners argue about | Coded comments under 30 videos | [Outside-in; not a survey] |
| Refused claims are a standing grievance, across brands | Same | [Outside-in; not a survey] |
| Warranty length does not separate brands today | Apollo 7 years, CEAT 7, MRF 6, all on manufacturing defects only, pro-rata | [Verified fact] |
| Short warranties depress satisfaction | 42% of owners got a one-year tyre warranty on a new tractor; satisfaction was highest at three years or more | [Verified fact: J.D. Power 2018] |
| Uneven wear destroys repurchase | Only 18% would buy the brand again | [Verified fact: J.D. Power 2018] |
| A guarantee needs registration | So it builds the record of buyers Apollo lacks | [Interpretation] |
| It costs money only when a tyre is sold | Unlike an event | [Interpretation] |

### 3.2 The precedent

| Maker | What it offers | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Trelleborg (large rear radial tractor tyres, Europe) | Up to 5,000 hours of tread wear, announced 25 February 2026. Register within 30 days. If the tyre wears to 20% of its tread early, the owner gets a credit for the unused hours toward a new tyre of the same size and model: 200 hours unused earns 4% of the original price, 500 hours 10%. Proof is a photo of the hour meter. The eligible sizes are for large tractors, well above Indian 30 to 50 horsepower sizes | [Verified fact: terms document re-read 6 November 2026] |
| Continental (farm radials, Europe) | Defects up to 10 years; stubble damage up to 3 years; punctures from foreign objects up to 2 years | [Fact - single source] |
| Apollo itself | Unconditional warranties on car tyres in Thailand (2015), Malaysia (2017) and on one car tyre line in India | [Fact - single source each; pages not all opened] |
| Indian farm tyres | No hours-based or damage guarantee found from any maker | [Verified fact that none was found] |

No maker has published a claim rate or a result.

### 3.3 A design to test

Working title: the Lambi Paari guarantee ("the long innings"). [Planning assumption throughout]

| Element | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Tyres covered | Rear Virat and Virat 23 first: the mixed-use tyre owners ask for |
| The promise | A stated number of hours of tread life. Apollo sets the figure from its own wear data; 2,000 hours is used here only to show the arithmetic |
| If it falls short | A credit toward a new Apollo tyre for the unused hours, as a share of the price paid |
| Settlement | At the home-ground dealer, within seven days, on his own authority up to a limit |
| Conditions | Registered at purchase with a photo of the hour meter; tyre pressure checked at a health camp once a season |
| Tractors without a working hour meter | A season-based version: a stated number of crop seasons |
| Excluded | Cuts and impact damage, as now; quarry and mining haulage |

Cost [Planning assumption]: if 5% of guaranteed tyres claim, at an average credit of 25%, the cost is 1.25% of the sales covered. We reserve 2%. The credit can be spent only on another Apollo tyre, so its cash cost is lower than its face value and it keeps the customer.

Risks: tampered hour meters; claims concentrated among the heaviest hauliers; dealers settling loosely. Each is a reason to start in the test districts, with a cap per dealer, before going national.

What only Apollo can supply: its tread-wear data by tyre and use. Without it the hours figure cannot be set. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

## 4. The trade

### 4.1 What a dealer earns

| Item | Figure | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Operating margin of the one rated tyre distributor found (multi-brand, not farm) | 6.99% in FY20, the last year it disclosed | [Fact - single source: CARE Ratings] |
| Dealer margins quoted on franchise blogs | 8 to 12% for Apollo; 10 to 15% for MRF | [Vendor estimate] |
| Deposits Apollo holds from dealers | Rs 1,315 crore across about 6,500 dealerships: about Rs 20 lakh each on average | [Fact - single source: CRISIL]; average [Computed] |
| Cost of holding one Rs 16,000 tyre in stock for 60 days | Rs 420 at 16% interest; Rs 790 at 30% | [Computed] |

An illustrative dealer account for one rear tyre [Planning assumption]:

| Line | Rs |
|---|---|
| Retail price, with 5% GST | 16,400 |
| GST | 780 |
| Dealer's cost (assuming he marks up 10%) | 14,200 |
| Gross margin | 1,420 |
| Cost of 60 days' stock | 420 to 790 |
| What is left before rent and wages | 630 to 1,000 |

What this shows:

- A dealer's gain on a farm tyre is small in rupees and is eaten by slow stock. Farm tyres are bulky, seasonal and tie up money.
- A bonus of Rs 400 on each rear tyre above his own baseline raises what he keeps on that tyre by 40 to 60%. [Computed]
- Stock on extended credit in the two buying seasons may matter to him as much as a bonus. Sixty days' credit on a rear tyre costs Apollo about Rs 230 at a 10% cost of money. [Computed; Planning assumption]

Real dealer margins, credit terms and rivals' schemes are not public. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. A survey of 40 dealers comes before any scheme is fixed.

### 4.2 What a mechanic earns

No survey of rural mechanics' or puncture-shop owners' income was found. Job advertisements for puncture-repair workers offer Rs 8,000 to 27,000 a month. [Vendor estimate] Castrol's programme for mechanics and retailers had 1.5 lakh users by September 2019 and was reimbursing about Rs 6 crore a month: about Rs 400 a user a month if all were active. [Fact - single source: Castrol; average Computed]

The first analysis budgeted about Rs 200 for each registered purchase. Against a monthly wage of Rs 8,000 to 27,000 that is slight.

Proposal [Planning assumption]: Rs 300 for each rear tyre sold on the mechanic's referral, paid to his phone when the buyer registers. A pair is Rs 600: between 2% and 8% of a month's pay for one conversation.

### 4.3 What the trade programme costs per tyre

For each extra rear tyre sold through the programme [Planning assumption]:

| Item | Rs per rear tyre |
|---|---|
| Mechanic referral | 300 |
| Dealer growth bonus | 400 |
| Harvest-timed credit (Rs 750 of interest absorbed, taken up on 3 purchases in 10) | 225 |
| Guarantee reserve (2% of Rs 14,200) | 280 |
| Registration and follow-up | 50 |
| **Total** | **1,255** |
| As a share of Apollo's realisation | **About 9%** |

Front tyres carry registration only. The effect on the business case is in `M07_Economics.md`.

### 4.4 From list price to pocket

Consultants call the path from list price to what the maker keeps a pocket-price waterfall. For a rear tyre sold through the programme [Computed on planning assumptions]:

| Step | Rs |
|---|---|
| Retail price with GST | 16,400 |
| Less GST | 15,620 |
| Less dealer margin: Apollo's invoice | 14,200 |
| Less programme costs | 12,945 |
| Less materials and other variable cost (70% of invoice) | 3,005 |

So an extra programme tyre leaves about Rs 3,000 of contribution, against about Rs 4,260 for a tyre sold without the programme. Trade schemes that Apollo already runs are not known and are left out.

## 5. The offer, funded

The first analysis listed exchange value, credit and a gift, left their cost out of the budget, and still claimed no price cut. Corrected:

| Element | What it is | Who pays | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exchange | The dealer takes the old tyres and credits their value as retread casings | The retreader, through the dealer | None to Apollo, if casings have a market. Casing values are not published. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. |
| Harvest-timed credit | Six months to pay on a rear pair, through a finance partner | Apollo absorbs the interest | About Rs 750 a tyre at 16%; lenders' rates run from 16% to 30% [Published rate; Computed] |
| Guarantee | Section 3 | Apollo | 2% reserve |
| Gift | Dropped | – | – |

Minimum loan sizes matter: one lender's tyre product starts at Rs 50,000, above the price of a rear pair. The finance partner has to be chosen for small tickets. [Published rate]

Credit is a price concession in substance. It is disclosed here as one, and it is narrower than a cut: it goes only to registered buyers of rear pairs in two seasons.

## 6. Start with the owners Apollo already has

About three-quarters of Apollo's farm sales appear to go to tractor makers and export (`M02_Value_at_Stake.md`). Those tractors are in villages now, and their owners will replace tyres over the next decade, the heavy users within three or four years.

| Step | What it needs |
|---|---|
| List the tractor models and years fitted with Apollo tyres at the factory | Apollo's supply records |
| Find those tractors in the priority districts | Tractor dealers' service records; health camps; mechanics |
| Offer the first replacement on Apollo with the guarantee and a returning-owner credit | The home-ground dealer |
| Record why owners who left did so | Two questions at the health camp |

Why first: these owners have used the tyre, the dealer of their tractor knows them, and holding a customer costs less than winning one from MRF. If the reason they leave is uneven wear, as J.D. Power's study suggests is common, Apollo learns that too. [Strategic hypothesis]

This depends on tractor makers and their dealers sharing records, which is not assured.

## 7. Brand architecture

| Today | Problem | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| The jersey says Apollo Tyres | – | Keep |
| The tractor tyre is sold as Virat, Virat 23, Krishak Gold, Farm King, FX 222, Dhruv | Six names, none of them on the jersey | "Apollo" leads in every rural use; the product name follows: Apollo Virat |
| The cricket film has no tractor in it | The owner cannot connect the two | One farm line, about tyre life, used everywhere tyres are sold |
| "Virat" is also a cricketer's name | Risk of implying a personal endorsement | No word-play on the name; no player's image without clearance |

Cricket already belongs to other tyre brands in this category: MRF through the bats of India's best-known batters and a pace academy founded in 1987, CEAT through its ratings since 1995 and the IPL time-out since 2015 (`M06_Sponsorship.md`). That is a further reason to carry the Apollo name on the tyre and at the shop, where the jersey cannot be mistaken for someone else's. [Interpretation]

## 9. What has to be true

| Belief | How the test checks it |
|---|---|
| Owners will pay Apollo's price for a guaranteed number of hours | Sales of guaranteed tyres against the same tyres without, in matched areas |
| Claims stay under 2% of sales | Claim records, by dealer and use |
| A Rs 300 referral moves a mechanic | Mechanic survey before and after; referral counts |
| A Rs 400 growth bonus and seasonal credit move a dealer | Mystery visits; share of Apollo in his farm tyre sales |
| Owners of Apollo-fitted tractors can be found and held | Share of health-camp tractors on Apollo; their repurchase rate |
