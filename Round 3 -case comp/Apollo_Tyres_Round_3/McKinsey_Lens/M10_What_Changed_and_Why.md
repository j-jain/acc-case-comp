# M10. What changed and why

Prepared 6 November 2026. Part of the McKinsey-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** A plain account of how the answer changed when the case was re-worked from a strategy consultant's angle, which faults in the first answer were fixed, and what is still not known. Our own analysis; not McKinsey's work.

**Purpose.** So that nobody uses a superseded number, and so that the team can defend the changes.

---

## 2. Twelve faults in the first answer, and the fix

An independent review, done as a senior partner would before a client meeting, found these. Each was checked against the files.

| # | Fault | Where it was | Fix | Where fixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The gates approved scale-up below break-even: 2.3 points for the engine against a full-cost break-even of 4.0 | File 14 section 6; file 13 sections 7.4 and 7.5 | Each gate is the higher of full-cost break-even and what the test can detect: 2, 5 and 3 points | M08 section 3.4 |
| 2 | Targets were smaller than the pilot could detect: an 8-point recall target against 10 to 13 detectable | File 14 sections 3.5 and 5.3 | More tehsils and owners; sales, not recall, is the gate; detectable differences are stated | M08 section 3.3 |
| 3 | The pilot was richer than the roll-out: about Rs 16 lakh a tehsil against Rs 5.4 lakh | File 13 sections 4 and 6 | The test runs the roll-out version at roll-out cost | M08 section 3.1 |
| 4 | Cricket was tested when India was not playing | File 16 section 12 | Launch with the home Tests on 21 January 2027; second window at the World Cup | M06 section 8; M08 section 4 |
| 5 | Tyre life cannot be shown in one season | File 04 section 5 | A guarantee replaces the demonstration; a two-year university trial supplies evidence | M05 section 3 |
| 6 | The offer was unfunded: exchange, credit and a gift sat outside the budget | File 13 section 4 | Each element costed; the gift dropped; Rs 1,255 a rear tyre in every calculation | M05 sections 4 and 5; M07 |
| 7 | A run-rate was presented as an outcome | File 13 sections 6 and 7.5; file 16 section 14 | Results shown year by year and cumulatively; no pay-back claimed by March 2028 | M07 section 6 |
| 8 | Rewards too thin to move a mechanic: about Rs 200 a purchase | File 13 section 6 | Rs 300 a rear tyre to the mechanic; Rs 400 to the dealer on tyres above his baseline | M05 section 4 |
| 9 | Nearly every tehsil in the twelve districts was in the experiment, beside its own control | File 14 section 3.2 | Thirty districts; one tehsil per arm; market towns 25 km apart; cross-buying counted from registrations | M08 section 3.1 |
| 10 | Rivals' cricket heritage was not researched, yet the jersey was called an asset no rival could match | File 07 | Researched: MRF since 1987, CEAT since 1995, BKT since 2020. The jersey's distinct value is restated | M06 section 5 |
| 11 | The scoring matrix was partly circular (one criterion rewarded using the jersey) and 52 against 50 is noise | File 12 | The choice now rests on economics: each layer has a cost, a break-even and a gate | M07 section 4 |
| 12 | The 30% contribution margin was used without showing where it comes from | File 13 section 7.2 | Bridge shown: raw materials at 55% of revenue plus 12 to 15% other variable cost | M02 section 4 |

Two further corrections came from the new research:

| Fault | Fix | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The diagnosis contradicted itself: rivals were said to arrive on new tractors, while Apollo's own farm business is mostly fitment | First link of the diagnosis restated: Apollo is on the tractor at the factory and loses the owner later | M02 section 3.3 |
| File 16 section 8 said some elements "cost nothing more" | Every element of the offer is costed | M05 section 5 |

One error was made and caught inside the re-analysis itself: a research note put the capital cost of new capacity at about Rs 46,500 per tonne of yearly output. The correct arithmetic on the same inputs is about Rs 4.65 lakh. The conclusion changed with it: supply should come from allocation and outsourcing, not from a new line (M02 section 5.1).

## 6. What did not change

- The evidence rules, the labels and the gap sentence.
- The five-link diagnosis, apart from the first link.
- The dealer with eleven mechanics, health camps and registration, now as the field engine.
- Difference-in-differences on matched areas with an independent review.
- No price cut; no television campaign for farm; no player endorsement.
- The eighteen original files, which remain the record of the first-pass research.

---

## 7. Limits of this re-analysis

- It is our own work in a consultant's manner. It uses three McKinsey publications that were read directly. Two ideas (growth at a fine-grained level; the path from list price to pocket price) are used in their general form and were not read at source.
- A consulting team would have Apollo's data and several weeks of interviews. This work had neither. Section 5 lists what that leaves open.
- The share gains in the three cases are assumptions. No published case gives a benchmark for a tyre maker.
- The market figure rests on one industry chart and portal prices. The range is wide (Rs 2,500 to 4,000 crore) and is carried through the sensitivities.
