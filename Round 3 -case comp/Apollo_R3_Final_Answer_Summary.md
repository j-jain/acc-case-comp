## 1. The answer in one paragraph

> Apollo's tractor tyres come fitted on new tractors, and Apollo loses the owner at the first replacement. Apollo should decide to invest in farm replacement, with a cash cap, in the two places it can lead: the **first replacement on tractors that left the factory on Apollo tyres**, and the **haulier** whose trolley damage every rival's warranty excludes. The owner decides whether to leave a brand, on how long his old tyre lasted; the **shop closes most sales** (about three in five*), through stock, price per pair and credit. To win the counter: get the tyre **stocked**; **guarantee tyre life in hours** at today's price; cover cuts, bursts and trolley wear **only for hauliers**; pay the **dealer and mechanic per extra tyre**; promise **two clocks** (tyre in the shop within 48 hours, claim settled in 7 days); and have the dealer **register every buyer**. The Team India jersey is the **trust mark at the counter** and, above all, **the reward for the dealers and mechanics who close the sale**: match seats and team shirts tied to stocking and to registered tyres, tested against cash. No farm-tyre maker anywhere has done this. Cricket experiences are tested against controls, and nothing in cricket is bought for reach. It costs about **Rs 15.5 crore to March 2028**, mostly moved from budgets Apollo already spends, and the national offer **breaks even at about 1.3 points of share** (about 1.7 if the trade rewards do not pay for themselves at scale). A 30-district test from **21 January 2027** decides what scales, before the sponsorship comes up for renewal.

**Five numbers to remember:** about **9%** replacement share today (against about 20% in car tyres); **Rs 33 crore** of revenue and **Rs 7.2 crore** of net contribution per point*; **about Rs 15.5 crore** to March 2028*; break-even at **1.3 points***; about **three in five** replacements closed by the shop*; **Rs 200 crore** a year if Apollo reaches 15%*.

## 3. What goes on each slide

### Slide 1. The answer
The section 1 paragraph in three lines, then **why** (Apollo is underweight in a real prize), **how** (an offer that works at every counter, with cricket paying the dealers and mechanics who close the sale) and **proof** (it pays at about 1.3 points, and a test sizes the rest), and the ask in one line.

### Slide 2. Diagnosis: why recall is weak
Weak recall is a symptom. The real problem is that Apollo loses the sale at the first replacement.
- **Apollo is on the new tractor and loses the owner afterwards.** About a quarter of Apollo's farm sales are replacement, against half for the industry. That puts Apollo near **9% of replacement**, against about 20% in car tyres and 29% in truck radials. This rests on one broker note, so say that on the slide; Apollo's books can confirm it in a week.
- **The tyre is often not in the shop.** A dealer portal lists about **170 Apollo tractor-tyre dealers against about 3,690 for MRF** (an outside-in signal, not a count).
- **Owners don't trust tyre-life claims.** Early failures and unanswered claims are the recurring complaints: in one sample, 23 of 39 tractor-tyre complaints were cuts, cracks and bursts, and 10 were claims refused, delayed or unanswered. Where owners name Apollo as the tyre their tractor came on, it is mostly in failure and claim stories (6 of 8 items), handled through the tractor dealer.
- **The jersey has not moved farm demand.** Apollo's share of tractor-tyre searches went from 14.0% to 14.8% in fourteen months; MRF stayed at 47%. Say "no effect visible in farm", not "failed".
- **Cricket is crowded, and TV reach is weakest where owners are most numerous.** MRF, CEAT and BKT already hold cricket assets. In Uttar Pradesh, 42% of men watch TV weekly and 45% have no regular media exposure (NFHS-5).

### Slide 3. The prize, and whether to invest
- About **7.7 million tractor tyres a year, half replacement**, worth about **Rs 3,300 crore** (range Rs 2,500 to 4,000 crore).
- Share ladder*: 9% → 12% → 15% → 20%. One point is about Rs 33 crore, so 9% to 15% is about Rs 200 crore a year.
- **Strip: should Apollo be in farm at all?** Farm replacement grew 6.9% a year in units (FY19 to FY24), but Apollo holds perhaps a quarter to a third of the leader's share. Management is harvesting farm without having decided to: farm is absent from its FY2026 targets, there is no farm capex, and the European farm line closed on 30 June 2026. The classic rule for a competitor this far behind is to concentrate where it can lead, or cash out. So: **invest, with a cash cap, in the two places Apollo can lead.**
- Scale line: farm's share of the jersey fee is about Rs 14 crore a year. Car and truck tyres must justify the jersey; farm borrows its standing.

### Slide 4. Target customer and the rural journey
| Target | Why Apollo can lead | Where to find them |
|---|---|---|
| **Returning Apollo owner:** tractors 3 to 5 years old that left the factory on Apollo tyres | The factory fitment. Mahindra (43.6% of tractor sales) has fitted Apollo for two decades | At the tractor dealer's service visit and at health camps. No tractor maker sells replacement tyres publicly |
| **Haulier:** owners who pull trolleys of crops or carry loads for money (31 to 34% of tractor hours in two district studies) | Rivals exclude trolley damage in writing (Goodyear) or reject it (MRF) | At the **sugar mill gate** (over two-thirds of cane moves by trolley; nearly 700 purchase centres in five states) and the **44,573 custom-hiring centres** |

- **Owners buy for a job.** Seven occasions: own field, hired out, road with crops, trolley for money, puddling, PTO work, all-rounder. Name the promise by the job, in the owner's words: "Mera khet", "Trolley se mill".
- **The owner decides whether to switch; the shop mostly decides what he switches to.** Tyre life is the reason in 42% of 261 public owner comments coded on replacement. But the shop closes most sales through stock, price per pair, credit and its own guarantee: about half when the purchase is planned, about four in five after a failure, about three in five overall*. Nobody has measured this for tractor tyres, so the test measures it (slide 9).
- **Heavy users matter most:** about four in ten owners do two-thirds of tractor hours (one Punjab survey, 2016-17; hours, not purchases).
- **Journey visual:** two paths. Planned: the owner brings one or two brands, and the shop settles it. Urgent (the day a tyre fails): the mechanic says replace, and the nearest shop with the size sells. Mark where Apollo drops out: the factory tyre's claim stalls at the tractor dealer, the tyre is not in stock, there is no trusted promise, the mechanic names a rival.

### Slide 5. Focus geographies
- **The prize is spread out.** The 20 largest districts hold only about 11.5% of new registrations. So the offer goes national, and field money goes only to dense tehsils (5,000+ working tractors).
- **First wave: 30 districts in three belts*:**
  - **Cotton belt:** Bathinda, Mansa, Sri Muktsar Sahib, Sangrur, Barnala, Fazilka, Sirsa, Fatehabad, Hisar, Hanumangarh, Sri Ganganagar.
  - **Rohilkhand and western UP:** Bareilly, Badaun, Pilibhit, Shahjahanpur, Rampur, Moradabad, Aligarh. UP has the most owners, and Bareilly is the brief's own example.
  - **Rajasthan and MP:** Bikaner, Nagaur, Jodhpur, Alwar, Chittorgarh, Shivpuri, Vidisha, Raisen. Hard soil, which Virat 23 is built for.
  - **Haryana, to reach 30:** Bhiwani, Jind, Karnal, Sonipat.
- Mark the sugar-mill gates on the map. Gujarat and Maharashtra join in the second wave.

### Slide 8. From recall to sales: the funnel
| Step | What moves the owner forward |
|---|---|
| Knows the name | Jersey mark at the counter, videos, match days |
| Believes it lasts | A **guaranteed number of hours** in writing at today's price (no Indian maker offers this), with "Team India's sponsor" on the card and a university tyre-life trial behind it. **Life numbers from registered owners** ("Virat, 3,200 hours, Sirsa, trolley and field") on boards and in videos, because owners judge a tyre by hours and years |
| Hears it from the adviser | Mechanic Rs 300 and dealer Rs 400 for each rear tyre above the dealer's baseline, plus the season slabs: a match seat and shirt for the dealer, a shirt for the mechanic |
| Finds it in the shop | **Clock 1: in stock within 48 hours.** The only clock owners know today is Mahindra's 48-hour parts promise |
| Buys | Harvest-timed credit; the returning-owner credit; **for hauliers, the cut-and-burst and trolley cover** |
| Is registered | Dealer paid Rs 100 per registration. Target: half of buyers in the test, three in five at scale |
| Gets a claim paid | **Clock 2: settled at the dealer in 7 days**, dealer authority up to Rs 5,000. MRF promises only "intimation" within 7 days |
| Returns and refers | SMS to the registered base; referral codes; repeat and referred sales tracked |

**Offer cost per rear tyre*:** Rs 300 mechanic + Rs 400 dealer + Rs 225 credit + Rs 280 reserve + Rs 100 registration = **Rs 1,305**, or **Rs 1,525** for hauliers with the cover. No price cut: Apollo is already 6 to 10% below MRF, and a cut gives away margin on every tyre, not just the extra ones.

A field team must add about 5 points in its tehsil, and cricket about 3 more, which is why both are tested.*

## 4. How we got to this answer

### 4.1 Check the brief's own numbers
Farm is about **5 to 6%** of Apollo's Indian revenue (about Rs 1,200 crore), not the 8 to 10% of 2019. Rs 579 crore is a **press figure**. The **jersey logo is the only confirmed right** (Muthoot FinCorp is the home title sponsor). Farm tyres are **short of capacity**: European farm production stopped in June 2026 and supply is partly off-take. So the plan cannot assume spare tyres or broad cricket rights.

### 4.2 First pass, then a reset
The first pass (files 00 to 17) assumed rivals win because they are on the new tractor, and proposed a 200-tehsil field programme at Rs 17.6 to 24.8 crore. Checking it found twelve faults (M10). The diagnosis was backwards, the scale-up gate sat below the programme's own break-even, and cricket was tested when India wasn't playing.

### 4.3 Three independent passes
We solved the case three times. Each time we wrote a fresh Day-1 answer before any research, then tried to break it using that firm's published frameworks.

| Lens | Starting question | What it added |
|---|---|---|
| **McKinsey-style** (issue tree, prize sizing) | How big is the prize, and where does Apollo lose? | "Fitted, then lost"; Rs 33 crore a point; the national hours guarantee; field teams only in dense tehsils; the four-arm test with full-cost gates. Rs 11 crore |
| **Bain-style** (profit pool, core and adjacency, NPS, sales plays) | Where is the profit, and is farm next door to the core? | **Get stocked first**; the **haulier** segment; the cut-and-burst cover and 7-day settlement; the **competition-law guardrail**; **experiences with controls**; a December start; **reallocation**, not new money. Rs 17 crore |
| **BCG-style** (portfolio matrix, Rule of Three and Four, demand spaces, time-based competition, TSR, DICE) | Should Apollo be in farm, and where can it lead? | **Invest or harvest**; occasions, so **cover only for hauliers** (Rs 0.6 crore a year vs Rs 1.9 crore); **two clocks**; **registration** as the jersey's farm asset; the **mill gate** and service visit; the **CFO case** and 25% margin case; funding from the **trade pool**; delivery-odds fixes. Rs 13.7 crore |

### 4.4 Keep what all three agree on
All three reached the same core from different starts, which is the strongest evidence we have: the diagnosis, the prize, the hours guarantee and per-tyre trade pay, the jersey as trust mark, the target owners, the 30 districts, the gated test, and no new line.

### 4.5 Pick the differences on evidence and cost
| Option | What it is | Cost | Break-even | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | McKinsey deck as built | Rs 11 cr | 1.0 pt | Misses stocking, legal risk, cover, clocks, registration and the CFO case |
| B | A plus Bain's six additions | ~Rs 14.5 cr | ~1.3 pts | Covers every tyre (costly); nothing on speed or registration |
| C | Full Bain deck | Rs 17 cr | 1.5 pts | Most practical; costs the most |
| **D** | **B plus BCG's eight additions** | **~Rs 15 to 15.5 cr** (about Rs 15.6 cr after 4.6) | **~1.3 pts** | **Chosen** |
| E | Full BCG deck | Rs 13.7 cr | 1.3 pts | Sharp, but a portfolio-matrix opening may confuse marketing judges |

**Why D:** each addition either fixes a gap a judge would spot (no stocking count, no legal check, no finance logic) or sharpens the answer for little or nothing (haulier-only cover, the two clocks, registration). It costs about Rs 1 crore more than B. **Present it as one story, not three frameworks:** framework names go in the notes and appendix. **If time is short:** B plus four BCG additions that cost nothing or save money (invest-or-harvest, the CFO case, haulier-only cover, delivery fixes) comes to about Rs 13.8 crore at the same break-even (about Rs 14 crore with the trade-reward swap in 4.6).

### 4.6 One more check: who decides the replacement
After choosing D we tested its biggest assumption, that dealers and mechanics decide the replacement. Nobody has measured this anywhere, so we triangulated: owner and shop surveys abroad, the trade's statements, farm-input shops, and 261 public Indian owner comments coded on replacement.

| Finding | What it changed in D |
|---|---|
| The **reason** is the owner's: tyre life decides whether he leaves a brand | **Life numbers** from registered owners as the message |
| The **close** is mostly the shop's: about three in five overall (an estimate) | **Cricket rewards for the trade** as the jersey's main farm use; a **counter intercept** to measure it |
| Factory-fitted Apollo shows up mostly in **claim stories** at the tractor dealer | A **fast lane for factory-Apollo claims** |
| No farm-tyre maker has used sport to reward its trade; no controlled result exists anywhere | Slabs instead of a top-300 ranking, tested against cash |

Net cost: about Rs 0.2 crore more than D (about Rs 15.6 crore), or about Rs 0.7 crore less if the village tournaments are dropped. Detail: `Apollo_Tyres_Round_3\Replacement_Decision\RD00` to `RD04`.
