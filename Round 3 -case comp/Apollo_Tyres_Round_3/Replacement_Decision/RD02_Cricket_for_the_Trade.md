# RD02. Cricket for the trade: has it been done, does it work, and how Apollo would do it

Prepared 8 November 2026. Part of the replacement-decision analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis. It tests the user's proposal: if the tractor-tyre replacement is decided by the trade, use cricket to give the trade benefits. It draws on `..\research_notes\U3_cricket_for_trade_precedents.md` (precedents and evidence of effect), `U4_trade_voice_and_input_dealer_analogue.md` (what moves a dealer or mechanic), `U1_replacement_choice_drivers.md` and `RD01_Who_Decides_the_Replacement.md` (who decides), and the cricket facts in `..\research_notes\Q7`. ## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **The proposal is right in direction and needs three conditions.** The close of a tractor-tyre replacement is mostly the trade's (RD01: about 60 to 65% of choices, analogue-based), so cricket rewards for the trade belong in the spine of the jersey's farm use, not in a side layer. But the reward works only where the tyre is in stock, only alongside money per tyre and credit, and only if the owner's verdict on the old tyre is also fixed. [Interpretation]
2. **In farm tyres it has not been done anywhere.** No tyre maker, Indian or foreign, has published a sports-linked reward for farm-tyre dealers, tractor mechanics or puncture shops. Apollo would be first. [Verified fact that none was found: U3 section 7]
3. **In Indian tyres it has been done only lightly, and never measured.** Apollo's own Champions Offer gave cricket prizes to car and two-wheeler buyers through the dealer's counter; BKT lists "dealer activations" with eight IPL teams; CEAT holds an IPL ticket pool and publishes no dealer use. In fourteen months Apollo has published no dealer-only, sales-linked cricket scheme. [Verified fact; Reported by press: U3 sections 1, 2]
4. **Elsewhere the format is common and the evidence thin.** Paint makers brought dealers and painters to meet IPL players; India Cements' consumer offers gave buyers of 50 bags (2018) and 25 bags (2023) an assured gift and a chance, by contest or lucky draw, of match tickets; Gulf Oil took 54 mechanics to an IPL match; Bridgestone's US dealers who reach "certain milestones" can use tickets, hospitality and, for a fee, athlete appearances. The only sales result published is Firestone's 2006 claim of 30 to 50% growth, with no control. [Reported by press; Verified fact for Bridgestone: U3 sections 2, 3]
5. **The controlled evidence that exists is indirect, and we infer a design from it.** In repeated ranked sales tournaments, tangible prizes beat cash because losers kept trying (a field experiment, 2017); a sponsorship tie-in moved store visits only among fans (a field experiment, 2018). Our inference: slabs every dealer can reach, not one ranking; cricket rewards on top of margin, not instead of it; and a test against cash. [Benchmark - external; Interpretation]
6. **One calendar fact shapes everything.** India plays no home international between 3 March 2027 and the reported England series in January 2028, which covers both 2027 buying seasons. Tickets earned in a season are redeemed months later. The home window of December 2026 to March 2027 should therefore reward stocking and registration before the first season, and the rewards for the two seasons themselves should be immediate (jerseys, screenings, recognition) with tickets for England in early 2028. [Verified fact for the calendar: Q7 section 2; Strategic hypothesis]

## 4. Which trade, and at which moment

From RD01, the trade decides at three moments, and each has a different person at the counter. [Interpretation on RD01's evidence]

| Moment | Who closes the sale | What he needs | Where the cricket reward fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| The planned purchase before a season | The multi-brand tyre dealer, settling between the one or two brands the owner brings | Stock of the hero pair, margin per tyre, credit to give the farmer, claim authority | A season slab on extra registered rear pairs, every dealer who reaches it rewarded |
| The urgent purchase after a cut or burst | The mechanic or puncture shop who says the tyre must go, and the nearest dealer with the size | A reason to name Apollo; stock within reach | A per-referral reward to the mechanic; a code on the tyre or card that pays the fitter; a team shirt at a small slab |
| The first replacement on an Apollo-fitted tractor | The tractor dealer's service desk, which sees the tractor at years three to five and sells no replacement tyre today | A reason to refer the owner to an Apollo dealer | Hospitality for the principals of the tractor dealers in the first-replacement play, tied to referred registrations |

## 5. The design

[Strategic hypothesis throughout; costs are planning assumptions]

### 5.1 Four audiences

| Audience | Entry condition | The money (already in the three lenses) | The cricket reward (new or reshaped here) | Tie to sales |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stocking tyre dealers** in the 30 districts, then nationally | The hero rear pair in stock and on display; registration on the one app | Rs 400 per extra registered rear tyre above his own baseline; harvest credit on stock; settlement authority (M05, B05, C05) | **Season slab:** 5 extra registered rear pairs in a buying season earns one seat at a home international with travel and a team shirt; 10 earns a second seat for a family member; a stocking reward before the first season (below) | Extra registered rear pairs against the dealer's own 12-month baseline, counted from the app |
| **Mechanics and puncture shops** enrolled in the adviser programme | KYC; a unique ID | Rs 300 per registered referral, paid within 24 hours (M05, B05) | **Shirt slab:** 5 referred registrations in a season earns an official team shirt; the district contest's top mechanics go to the final at a home international (B05) | Registrations carrying the mechanic's ID |
| **Fitters at the point of sale** | None | A small voucher per tyre fitted, through a code printed on the warranty card and texted at fitting (the mechanism in U4 row 3.1, if Apollo already runs it) | None beyond the voucher | The code |
| **Tractor dealers' principals** in the first-replacement play | Ten Mahindra dealers in the first wave (B05 Play 2) | Key-account terms | Hospitality at a home Test, with a matched set of tractor dealers not invited (B06's design) | Owners referred to Apollo dealers and registered |

### 5.2 Rules taken from the precedents

1. **Slabs every dealer can reach, not one ranking.** The 2017 field experiment tested ranked tournaments and found tangible prizes worked through the round-one losers who kept trying; our inference is that a slab, where no one loses, keeps everyone in; JK's points scheme engaged 35% of members (Q5). Every dealer who reaches the slab is rewarded. [Benchmark - external; Interpretation]
2. **On top of the money, never instead of it.** The per-tyre bonus, the credit and the settlement authority are the base (RD01: credit and stock decide the close); the cricket reward is the part rivals cannot match, because only Apollo holds the national team's rights. [Interpretation]
3. **No stock, no reward.** The entry condition is the hero pair on the shelf. A dealer without stock loses the urgent sale whatever he is offered (92.5% switch when the brand is missing, U4 row 5.3). [Interpretation]
4. **Skill, not chance; no draws in Tamil Nadu.** Rewards are assured at a slab; any contest is decided by a skill question, as the Champions Offer was; Tamil Nadu's 1979 Act bans prize schemes, "all kinds of prize draws which are determined by lot, draw or otherwise". [Fact - single source: law firm summary, U3 section 6]
5. **Open to every stocking dealer, never conditional on exclusivity.** The competition-law guardrail stands (B05 section 5): no minimum resale price, no tie-in, no "sell only Apollo farm" condition. A slab on Apollo's own registered sales is a volume reward, not an exclusivity term. [Interpretation; legal review required]
6. **Tax handled by Apollo.** Section 194R takes 10% at source on business benefits above Rs 20,000 a year per recipient, including trips; the reward is priced so that a single slab stays under the threshold, and Apollo deducts or grosses up above it. [Fact - single source: U3 section 6]
7. **Tested against cash.** In half the treated tehsils the slab pays cash of equal value instead of the seat and shirt (M06 section 10 and B06 already designed this split); the dealer's sell-out is compared across the two halves and against the control tehsils. [Strategic hypothesis]

### 5.3 The calendar

| Window | Home cricket | Farm calendar | What the trade reward does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 December 2026 to 3 March 2027 | Sri Lanka ODIs and T20Is; Zimbabwe ODIs; five Australia Tests (Q7) | Wheat standing; dealers order stock for April | **Stocking reward:** dealers in the ten prototype districts and then the 30 who take and display a hero rear pair on 90 days' credit and register their first buyers earn a seat at one of these matches; the hospitality group for tractor-dealer principals at Nagpur or Ahmedabad |
| April to June 2027 (first buying season) | None; IPL only | Wheat harvest; rear-tyre buying | **Season slab 1:** the team shirt immediately; the seat is a voucher for the next home series; screenings at dealers during away matches where licensed |
| October to December 2027 (second buying season) | ODI World Cup in southern Africa (team marks only; ICC marks belong to the ICC) | Paddy and cotton harvest; buying | **Season slab 2:** the shirt immediately; seats for the England series |
| January to March 2028 | England in India (reported, not announced) | Wheat standing | Seats redeemed for both seasons' slabs; the last window before the sponsorship ends |

A reward months after the sale motivates less than one paid at once, which is why the shirt and the per-tyre money are immediate and the seat is the deferred part. If the England series is not confirmed, the deferred reward needs a substitute the contract allows (a player appearance day at a district event, signed team merchandise). ### 6.3 Does a slab pay?

A season slab of 5 extra rear pairs (10 tyres) earns a reward costing about Rs 16,000. Those 10 tyres leave about Rs 29,550 of net contribution after the per-tyre offer cost (C07: Rs 2,955 a rear tyre in the field spaces). The slab pays if more than about half of the extra pairs are truly extra, which is what the cash arm and the control tehsils measure. At national scale (about 3,500 stocking dealers, a quarter reaching each slab, two seasons) the slabs cost about Rs 2.8 crore a year and need about 4,700 truly extra rear pairs a year to pay (Rs 2.8 crore over Rs 5,910 of net contribution a pair), about half a point of share at about 9,700 pairs a point. If they do not pay for themselves, they raise the national offer's break-even from about 1.3 points to about 1.7 (Rs 9.4 crore plus Rs 2.8 crore of fixed cost over Rs 7.2 crore a point). [Computed on planning assumptions]
