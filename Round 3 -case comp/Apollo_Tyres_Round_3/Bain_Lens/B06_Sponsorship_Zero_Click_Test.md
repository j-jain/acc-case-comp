# B06. The sponsorship: Bain's Zero-Click test applied to farm

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the Bain-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using Bain's most recent published work on sports sponsorship, "The Zero-Click Playbook: How Sports Sponsorships Build Preference Before Search" (Milligan, Beaudin, Dennehy and Beale, 9 June 2026), with Bain's marketing-measurement work (February 2018; July 2026). It is not Bain's work, and Bain has published nothing on cricket. Sources: `..\research_notes\Q7_rural_cricket_reach_and_sponsorship_tests.md`, `Q2`, `Q4`; the McKinsey-lens treatment in `..\McKinsey_Lens\M06_Sponsorship.md`, which applied McKinsey's 2014 measures and which this file does not repeat.

**Purpose.** To say what the jersey is for in farm, whether each cricket use passes Bain's three steps, how its effect is measured against a control, what else in cricket is worth backing, and what the farm business should ask for at the 2028 renewal.

**Labels.** [Verified fact], [Fact - single source], [Reported by press], [Outside-in], [Computed], [Planning assumption], [Interpretation], [Strategic hypothesis], [Bain-published], [Not found].

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **Bain's rule for a sponsor is three steps and a scorecard:** "Define the outcome, not the asset"; "Apply an authenticity test"; "Design for experiences, not impressions"; then measure brand impact, engagement and commercial outcomes against a matched control, because "without a rigorous test-and-control design, sponsorship ROI is merely an opinion." [Bain-published]
2. **For farm, the outcome is registered replacement sales, not recall.** Recall is measured as a brand-impact tier, and the brief's "first brand named" is a means to it. [Interpretation]
3. **The jersey passes the authenticity test at the counter and fails it on the screen.** Tyre brands have been in Indian cricket for thirty years through rivals; up to one in six IPL viewers credits another team's sponsor to the most-followed team; no rural split of cricket viewing has been published since 2019, so the cost of reaching a tractor owner by broadcast cannot even be computed. "Team India's sponsor" next to the word Apollo on a dealer's board cannot be misattributed. [Verified fact; Not found; Interpretation]
4. **So buy experiences, each with a control:** hospitality at home Tests for the farm dealers and tractor-dealer principals who move the most tyres, with a matched set not invited; a mechanics' contest whose final is at a Test; a Kisan XI at the village tournaments that already run in the cotton belt with no sponsor; and dealer match days only where the screening is licensed. [Strategic hypothesis]
5. **Measure it in three tiers and lock the metrics with finance before the budget is released:** first mention and NPS; attendance and registrations at events; sell-out in treated against control areas, registered owners and earned growth. [Bain-published; Strategic hypothesis]
6. **At the 2028 renewal, farm should ask only for the rights it uses:** marks at the point of sale and on the product, an allocation of tickets and shirts for the trade, hospitality seats, and player days for the mechanics' final. The case for the jersey itself belongs to car and truck tyres. [Interpretation]

## 3. Step two: the authenticity test

| Test | Evidence | Verdict | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does the sponsor's product belong in the sport's story? | Tyres have been in Indian cricket through MRF's bats since 1987, CEAT's ratings since 1995 and BKT's eight IPL teams since 2020. Tyre brands were the second most recalled category among IPL 2025 viewers | The category is authentic; the name is contested | [Verified fact; Fact - single source] |
| Is the message true to the product? | "Har Safar Mein Dum Hai" ("strength in every journey") fits a tyre that lasts; the film has no tractor and no farm angle | True for the brand; silent for farm | [Reported by press] |
| Will the audience credit Apollo? | YouGov, IPL 2026: brands not sponsoring RCB were wrongly linked to RCB by 17.2% of viewers on average (CSK 11.2%, KKR 9.5%, PBKS 8.2%); a viewer recalls three to five brands a game; only a quarter of about 200 tracked brands crossed 4% recall | On the screen, partly; a new tyre logo risks being credited to MRF or CEAT | [Verified fact; Reported by press] |
| Can a tractor owner see it where he buys? | No farm activation; no marks at farm counters; the Champions Offer was for car and bike buyers | Not today | [Not found; Verified fact] |
| Is the rural audience reached at all? | No rural share of cricket viewing published since 2019 (the last hard figures: 43% rural for IPL 2016; one women's final where rural exceeded urban); Ormax counts 612 million cricket viewers with no rural split; under the 2007 Act the free feed of national events runs only on Doordarshan's terrestrial network and DD Free Dish; whether the 2027 home Tests are carried there is not known | Unknown, and unknowable from public data | [Verified fact; Not found] |

Reading: the jersey is authentic for Apollo and unproven for farm. It becomes authentic for a tractor owner at the moment "Team India's sponsor" is read next to "Apollo" on his dealer's board and on his guarantee card, which no rival can copy and no viewer can misattribute. That is the McKinsey lens's conclusion too (M06 section 5); Bain's test gets there by a different route. [Interpretation]

## 4. Step three: experiences, not impressions

Bain's one measured case is hospitality: a wealth manager "invited a segment of HNW clients to events while holding back a matched control group" and found "incremental net new assets attributable to the program exceeded $50 million" in a year. The design, not the sector, is what transfers: invite the people who move the most business, hold back a matched set, measure the business. [Bain-published]

The village tournament is the one cricket property found where the audience is tractor owners by definition, the cost is a few lakh, no rival is present, and a control exists next door. [Interpretation]

### 4.2 What Bain's analogue says, and its limit

Bain's Latino-fans work found that 54% "say they are likely to purchase products for the first time from companies that sponsor their teams or leagues", in a segment that "feel under-engaged" (September 2024). It is a stated likelihood, not measured sales. The parallel with rural cricket fans is partial: the audience is avid and under-served by Apollo, but it has been engaged by tyre brands for thirty years. The analogue supports engagement; it does not support impressions. [Bain-published; Interpretation]

## 5. The scorecard, locked with finance

Bain's three tiers: "Brand impact: Shifts in 'share of mind,' measured by brand lift and Net Promoter Scores"; "Engagement: Measured by participation rates, attendance, and social listening tools"; "Commercial outcomes: Sales lift, conversion, retention, and lifetime value." Bain's leaders "establish a measurement framework shared by marketing and finance before budgets are allocated" and "lock metrics before campaigns begin" (July 2026). [Bain-published]

| Tier | Measure | Source | Baseline | Read-out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand impact | Unaided first mention of Apollo for tractor tyres; "who sponsors Team India" credited to Apollo; NPS of owners; belief that Apollo lasts as long as MRF | The owner survey of M08 (40 owners a tehsil, three waves) with two questions added | January 2027 | July and December 2027 |
| Engagement | Dealers and principals attending hospitality; mechanics entering the contest; attendance and registrations at tournaments; match-day footfall | Event registers; the dealer app | None | Monthly |
| Commercial | Farm sell-out in treated against control areas and dealers; registered owners; the share of revenue from repeat and referred buyers (earned growth); hospitality dealers' sell-out against the matched set | Apollo's billing; registrations | 24 months of billing | Gate 1 (August 2027), Gate 2 (January 2028) |

What is locked with finance in November 2026: the three tiers above, the control design for each experience, the gate thresholds in M08 and B07, and the rule that no cricket spending scales without a commercial-tier result. [Strategic hypothesis]

## 8. The renewal in 2028

| Question | The Bain-lens answer |
|---|---|
| Should farm's results decide the renewal? | No. Farm's share of the fee is about Rs 14 crore a year against a farm prize of Rs 100 to 200 crore of revenue; the jersey must be justified by car and truck tyres (M07 section 1). Farm should report its commercial tier honestly and claim no more |
| What should farm ask for if Apollo renews? | Marks at the point of sale and on the product; a stated allocation of tickets and shirts for the rural trade; hospitality seats at home matches; player appearance days for the mechanics' final; the right to associate with village tournaments under the Team India name; content in regional languages |
| What if Apollo does not renew? | The dealer board, the guarantee, the cover, the mechanic programme and the referral stand without it; only the trade rewards change. That is the point of using the jersey as a trust mark and not as the engine |
| What decides it, from farm's side? | The commercial tier: whether hospitality dealers out-sold their matched set, whether tournament tehsils out-registered their pairs, and whether the sponsor's rewards beat cash |

**Status of H4.** Supported: the jersey's measurable farm uses are experiences and the counter, each with a control; impressions cannot be priced for a rural audience from public data. One correction to the Day-1 answer: village cricket is worth backing after all, but as the sponsorship of tournaments that already exist, at a few lakh each, with a control, rather than as tournaments Apollo builds.
