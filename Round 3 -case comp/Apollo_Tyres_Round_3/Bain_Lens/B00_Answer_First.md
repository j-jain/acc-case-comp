## 1. The answer

> **Farm replacement is the next step of the replacement business Apollo already wins in. Be stocked where the owner buys, promise what he fears and settle it at the counter, pay the dealer and the mechanic only for extra tyres, lead the two segments Apollo can own, and use the jersey for experiences measured against a control. Fund it by reallocating about Rs 17 crore of the marketing money Apollo already spends, ask for no new money, and prove it by August 2027.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The situation** | Apollo's business is about 80% replacement, won through dealers, service and the trade. Farm is 6% of its Indian revenue and about 25% replacement: fitted at the factory and lost at the first replacement, with about 9% of a Rs 3,300 crore replacement market. [Verified fact; Fact - single source; Computed] |
| **What the evidence adds** | The farm leader earns 6% on fitment; the profit is at the replacement counter. Apollo is thin at that counter (a portal lists about 170 Apollo tractor-tyre dealers against about 3,690 for MRF). Owners become detractors through early failure and unanswered claims, not price; the commonest cause, trolley haulage, is excluded by rivals' warranties. Advice comes from the input dealer and the neighbour. The jersey has no farm activation, and the rural reach of cricket cannot be priced from public data. [Verified fact; Outside-in; Interpretation] |
| **The resolution** | Run farm with the truck business's formula: stocked and shown; a promise (hours, first-season cuts and bursts, trolley wear) settled at the dealer in seven days; four sales plays for the counter, the first replacement, the adviser and the haulier; the jersey as hospitality, a mechanics' final and village tournaments, each with a control; three micro-battles from December 2026 and the controlled test from 21 January 2027. [Strategic hypothesis] |

## 2. Three reasons

### 2.1 The profit is at the replacement counter, and Apollo is a follower there with a strong core next door

- Indian tyres are a Rs 1 lakh crore industry with an operating-profit pool near Rs 16,000 crore; farm is a corner of it, and within farm the leader on fitment (Goodyear, about 30%) earns about 6% while Apollo earns 14.6% in the segments it leads. [Verified fact; Computed; Interpretation]
- Apollo's relative market share in farm replacement is perhaps a quarter to a third of the leader's; Bain's research says only about 5% of weak followers change position. So lead two segments: the first replacement on Apollo-fitted tractors (Mahindra, 43.6% of tractor sales, has fitted Apollo for two decades), and hauliers. [Computed on planning assumptions; Bain-published]
- Farm replacement is one step from the core and shares its dealers, its claims line and its service network; what it lacks is the core's formula, last tried in two Farm Zones in 2019. [Interpretation; Verified fact]
- Detail: `B02`, `B03`.

### 2.2 Owners are lost to early failure and silence, and advised by the mechanic and the neighbour

- Of 39 tractor-tyre complaints coded, 23 are cuts, cracks or bursts within days or a few hundred hours and 10 are claims refused or unanswered; a claim takes the dealer, a company engineer and a "final and binding" decision, two months in one court record; no maker publishes a settlement time; one excludes trolley wear in writing. [Outside-in count; Verified fact]
- Any tyre problem costs 114 points of satisfaction; only 18% of owners with uneven wear buy the brand again; Bain puts a promoter's lifetime value at 3 to 12 times a detractor's. [Verified fact; Bain-published]
- In five Indo-Gangetic states 34% of farm households take advice from the input dealer and 33% from a progressive farmer; machinery advice is sought formally by 1.5%. Bain's Indian precedent, Tata Steel Wire's "Farming Gold", sold durability to farmers through distributors with a 15-year warranty at a 25% premium. [Verified fact; Bain-published]
- So: the promise, paid at the counter; the adviser, paid per extra tyre and verified; the owner, paid to refer; measured as NPS and earned growth. Detail: `B04`, `B05`.

### 2.3 The jersey is an unused asset for farm, and only experiences with a control can price it

- Farm's pro-rata share of the fee is about Rs 14 crore a year; no farm activation exists; the first full quarter's activation money (over Rs 100 crore above the usual spend) went to the brand film and car and bike tyres. [Computed; Verified fact]
- Bain's rule: "Without a rigorous test-and-control design, sponsorship ROI is merely an opinion." Its three steps (outcome, authenticity, experiences) and its one measured case (hospitality for a client segment against a matched control) transfer directly. [Bain-published]
- The experiences: hospitality at home Tests for the 300 top farm dealers and the Mahindra principals; a mechanics' final at a Test; a Kisan XI at the village tournaments of the cotton belt that run with no commercial sponsor on prize funds of up to about Rs 3 lakh; dealer match days if licensed; tickets tested against cash. Each has a control and a commercial measure. Total about Rs 2.2 crore to March 2028. [Strategic hypothesis; Verified fact for the tournaments]
- Detail: `B06`.

[Computed on planning assumptions; the share gains are M07's and are assumptions, not forecasts.]

Read it plainly:

- **The Bain-lens programme costs about Rs 6 crore more and pays later than the McKinsey-lens programme on the same share assumptions.** The difference buys the counter, the cover, the adviser and experiences with controls.
- **Those additions must add about three-quarters of a point of share by FY29 to pay for themselves.** The money map allows for far more (two points from first replacements, two from hauliers, one and a half from being stocked), but that is a claim; the test decides it.
- **No new money.** Rs 17 crore is about 3.5% of one year's advertising and promotion and about 40% of farm's pro-rata share over the period. The ask is a ring-fenced reallocation.
- **The national offer breaks even at about 1.5 points of share**, the hubs at about five points in a dense tehsil, and the experiences only where their control shows lift.

## 5. Same as the McKinsey lens, or different?

| | Same | Different |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis, prize, offer's core, jersey as trust mark, national offer, thirty districts, controlled test, gates, supply | Yes | – |
| Frame | – | An adjacency of the replacement core, with the truck formula translated, instead of a share-gain programme |
| First lever | – | Being stocked, counted first |
| Segment | – | Hauliers named, on verified evidence of rivals' exclusions |
| Promise | – | Cuts and bursts and trolley wear covered; settled in seven days; Rs 1,475 a tyre |
| Trade | – | Four separable plays on benchmarked rules; a competition-law guardrail |
| Jersey | – | Experiences with controls in place of a slim cricket arm; the December 2026 windows |
| Funding | – | Reallocation of Rs 17 crore, no new money, against Rs 11 crore of new budget |
| Economics | – | Break-even 1.5 points, not 1.0; honest about paying later |
| Delivery and measurement | – | Micro-battles, RAPID, a risk dialogue, NPS and earned growth, the scorecard locked with finance |

**Recommendation for the deck:** keep the McKinsey-lens deck's spine and add six things from the Bain lens (the stocking count, the cover and settlement clock, the legal guardrail, experiences with controls, the December start, the reallocation ask). The merged programme is about Rs 14 to 15 crore to March 2028, reallocated, with a break-even near 1.3 points. The case for each option is in `B10` section 4.

## 6. What we do not know

The whole case still turns on one figure, that about a quarter of Apollo's farm sales are replacement, from one broker's note. Three further unknowns now matter as much: how many dealers stock Apollo farm tyres; what farm's share of advertising and promotion actually is; and what the sponsorship contract allows at the shop and in hospitality. Each is Apollo's to answer in a week. The full list, with what settles each, is in `B10` section 5.
