# C03. Which strategy fits which part: the strategy palette, and the business de-averaged

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the BCG-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using BCG's strategy palette (Reeves, Haanaes and Sinha, "Your Strategy Needs a Strategy", 2015; the 2018 article on strategy process; the ambidexterity pages) and BCG's own India work on rural distribution (with the Confederation of Indian Industry, 2014 and 2015). It is not BCG's work. BCG sources are in `..\research_notes\S1_bcg_method_sources.md` section 3 and `S2b_bcg_india_rural_and_agriculture_sources.md`; the district data is `..\McKinsey_Lens\M03_Where_to_Play.md`, reused and not re-derived; the demand spaces are `C04`, the channel `C05`.

**Purpose.** To decide which approach to strategy each part of the farm problem needs, because BCG's own research says the approach must match the environment and that about half of companies get the match wrong; and to break the farm business into the parts that each need a different approach, which is the sense in which BCG's book uses the word "de-average".

**Labels.** [Verified fact], [Fact - single source], [Computed], [Planning assumption], [Interpretation], [Strategic hypothesis], [BCG-published]. Where nothing reliable exists: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **The farm replacement counter is a classical environment: predictable, not malleable, not harsh.** Demand follows the installed base and the monsoon; MRF sets the price; nobody can reshape how a tractor owner buys a tyre. BCG's imperative for that environment is "Be big", measured by "Scale" and "Market share", in the sector chosen, with the experience curve and the growth share matrix as its tools. [BCG-published; Interpretation]
2. **The jersey's farm uses are an adaptive environment: unpredictable, and shapeable only by trial.** No one knows the rural reach of cricket, whether a ticket moves a dealer more than cash, or whether a village tournament sells a tyre. BCG's imperative is "Be fast": "serial temporary advantage" found by experimentation. [BCG-published; Interpretation]
3. **So the programme is two things run two ways, which BCG calls ambidexterity by "separation".** The counter is planned and scaled; the cricket uses are experiments with controls that are scaled only when they show lift. One organisation, two cadences. [BCG-published; Strategic hypothesis]
4. **De-averaging, in BCG's sense, splits farm into three parts:** the counter (classical), the jersey (adaptive), and the haulier sub-sector, where Apollo can shape the terms of competition with a warranty clause nobody else offers. [BCG-published for the term; Interpretation]
5. **De-averaged by district and demand space, the profit map has three belts and three dominant uses.** The cotton belt (haulage and hiring out; the densest tractors; Apollo's weakest search share), Rohilkhand (own-use field work on small holdings; the most owners; the least television), and Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh (hard soil and haulage). Maharashtra's cane fleet, found at the mill gate, is the second wave. BCG's own India rural work says to target at "sub district / tehsil" level and warns that rural cost to serve runs "upto 300 bps" higher. [Verified fact; BCG-published; Interpretation]
6. **The map's left-hand column is still Apollo's to fill.** Apollo's replacement billing by district, its stocking dealers by pin code and its fitment by model decide which cells get the fixed-cost layers. Until then districts are sorted by prize only, as M03 said. [Interpretation]

### 2.3 What a mismatch would look like, and where the earlier lenses sit

BCG's finding is that "around half of all companies" mismatch, and that matching is worth "4 to 8 percent of total shareholder return". The two mismatches available here [Interpretation]:

- **Running the counter adaptively:** pilots, tweaks and campaigns in a few districts instead of a planned national offer and coverage model. The first-pass analysis (files 00 to 16) did this: a field programme in 200 tehsils with the offer waiting on the pilot. M10 lists it among the twelve faults.
- **Running the jersey classically:** planning a farm media campaign on an assumed rural reach, with a fixed budget and no control. Rivals' farm cricket work (BKT's eight IPL teams, CEAT's IPL time-out) looks like this from outside; none publishes a result (file 15).

The McKinsey lens is classical throughout, with the cricket layer handled as one arm of a test; the Bain lens is classical at the counter and adaptive in cricket (experiences with controls), with micro-battles as the cadence. Neither names the difference. The BCG lens's contribution is to say that the two parts need two processes, and to run them separately on purpose. [Interpretation]

## 3. De-averaging: the business decomposed

BCG's book: "The palette can also help leaders to "de-average" their business (decompose it into its component parts, each requiring a characteristic approach to strategy) and effectively combine multiple approaches to strategy across different business units, geographies, and stages of a firm's life cycle." (chapter 1, p.16). This is the only use of "de-average" found on a BCG domain; no BCG page uses the word for price or cost de-averaging, so that common consulting usage is not attributed to BCG here. [BCG-published; Verified fact: S1 row 4.1]

### 3.1 Farm, decomposed

| Component | Environment (section 2) | What "winning" is | How it is run | Who runs it | Reads out at |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The counter: stocking, the promise, the clock, registration, the trade's pay | Classical | Share of replacement in the sector, nationally | Planned; national from October 2027 on Gate 1 | Head of rural sales | Gate 1, August 2027 |
| The sub-sectors to lead: first replacement on Apollo-fitted tractors; hauliers | Classical, shaping at the edge | Retention of fitted owners; registered hauliers; claims per 100 covered tyres | Planned, with the cover's claim rate as the one experiment | Key-account head; district ARDs | Gate 1 and Gate 2 |
| The jersey's farm uses: the mark at the counter; rewards for the trade; hospitality; match days; tournaments | Adaptive | Lift against a control, per use | Experiments: each use against its own control; scale only what lifts | Head of brand and sponsorship | Gate 2, January 2028; the 2028 renewal |
| The field engine in dense tehsils | Classical, but unproven | Five points of local share at full cost | Tested as an arm; funded per tehsil on Gate 2 | District ARDs | Gate 2 |

Reading: three of the four components are planned and gated; one is experimental. That is "separation" in BCG's list of ambidexterity routes, and it is cheap, because the experimental part costs under Rs 2 crore to March 2028 in any lens. [Interpretation]

### 3.2 What BCG's own India rural work adds to the decomposition

BCG and CII, "Re-Imagining FMCG in India" (December 2015): "cost to serve remains high in low population stratas and there is significant volatility in growth in rural"; "High attrition (> 15% for some players) driven by challenged partner economics (RoI, Low absolute earnings)"; "Higher cost-to-serve in lower pop rural areas (upto 300 bps)"; winners "go deep into rural with the most cost efficient Go To Market model" and use "geospatial data (GIS) overlaid with some consumption metrics & infrastructure metrics at the level of a sub district / tehsil"; "> 30% increase in effectiveness of rural sales teams when these enablers and monitoring mechanism are in place." BCG and CII, "Changing your Orbit" (June 2014): "no Simple Answer to 'Winning' Distribution"; "Myriad forms of retailers 12+ mn outlets estimated"; a case that "Appoints 200 super distributors in a 'hub and spoke' model to cover 3,000 new towns and semi-urban cities". [BCG-published; S2b rows 15, 16]

Applied [Interpretation]:

- **The tehsil is BCG's own unit for rural targeting, and it is M03's and M08's unit.** The de-averaged map below is built at that level where data exists (Haryana, Punjab) and at district level elsewhere.
- **The 300-basis-point cost-to-serve warning is the reason the fixed-cost layers are tested and gated rather than rolled out.** M07's break-even for the field engine (about five points in a 5,000-tractor tehsil) is the farm version of that warning.
- **"Partner economics" and attrition above 15% is the reason the dealer is paid per tyre and given the claim authority (`C05`), not a target and a trip.** A farm tyre is a working-capital decision for a shop whose whole opening stock is Rs 3 to 8 lakh (S5 row 2.3).

### 4.2 What the map says [Interpretation]

- **Each belt has a different lead demand space, so the offer leads with a different promise in each.** In the cotton belt the haulier and the hirer-out (the cover for trolley wear, the load-and-pressure camp before the cane season); in Rohilkhand the own-use field worker on a small holding (the hours promise, credit, the neighbour's word); in Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh hard soil (Virat 23) and the operator (the cover). The product range already matches (`C04` section 3); the promise and the camp calendar should.
- **The haulier is findable at a place, not only by a segment.** The sugar mill gate and the purchase centre (nearly 700 of them set up by the mills in the five states the government study covered) are where several hundred tractors a day stand still with their loads fixed by the mill. That turns a demand space into a route-to-market stop (`C05` section 5).
- **Cricket's reach and the owners' density run in opposite directions,** as M03 found. The adaptive part of the programme belongs where the experiments can be read, which is the cotton belt and Haryana (the highest television and internet use among the first-wave belts), not where the most owners are.
- **The cheap tier is a demand space, not a geography.** Trolley-for-money operators buy per-kilogram and second-hand tyres everywhere; the map does not chase them except through the haulier programme's cover, which gives the operator a reason to pay for a branded tyre.

### 4.3 The profit de-averaged

Using M03's district values (Rs 3,900 of replacement spend per working tractor a year; a point of share in a district worth a few lakh) and M07's per-tyre economics. [Computed on planning assumptions]

| Cell | A point of share is worth, a year | What the offer costs in that cell | Net per extra rear tyre | What the cell needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sirsa (32,309 tractors), field and haulage | Rs 12.6 lakh of revenue; Rs 3.8 lakh of contribution | The hours promise plus the trolley cover on haulier-registered tyres: Rs 1,255 to 1,475 a rear tyre | Rs 2,785 to 3,005 | The counter and the camp; a field engine only if it clears five points |
| Badaun (about 4,300 registrations a year; count in use unknown), own-use field | Not computable without the in-use count | The hours promise: Rs 1,255 | Rs 3,005 | The counter, credit, the neighbour's referral |
| A cane district in Maharashtra, mill gate | Not computable; 18,850 tractors across 26 mills' fleets | The trolley cover; a mill-gate camp (about Rs 1 lakh a mill a season, `C05`) | Rs 2,785 | The haulier programme; second wave |
| A 3,000-tractor tehsil anywhere | Rs 1.2 lakh of revenue | Fixed field engine cost Rs 2.06 lakh | – | No field engine: break-even 7.9 points (M07 section 4.4) |

The de-averaged arithmetic repeats M07's lesson with a sharper edge: the fixed-cost layers pay only in dense cells, and the haulier cover is worth its Rs 220 a tyre only where hauliers are. Everywhere else the counter and the promise are the whole programme. [Interpretation]
