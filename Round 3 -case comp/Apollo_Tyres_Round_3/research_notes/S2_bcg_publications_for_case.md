## 5. What BCG has published on tyres and what it has not

BCG's published work on tyres is thin and recent. The only dedicated piece is the February 2026 Tire Industry Paradox (Schmidt, Dal Pan, Stellmaszek, Farag, Collino, Brenner), a ten-page TSR study of 25 listed tyre makers: a 2020 to 2024 median industry TSR of about 7% against a cross-sector 10%, premium and mid-tier returns of about 6% and 5% against double-digit budget returns, credit to "Asian budget challengers from India and China" without naming a company, five value-creation archetypes, and a prescription of 80% utilisation, pricing discipline, EV-specific products and partnerships with regional budget makers; it says nothing about farm, OE or dealers, and Tyrepress has called it "exclusively shareholder-centric". Otherwise tyres appear only incidentally: MRF sixth in the 2014 automotive-components TSR ranking; Bridgestone and Hankook in a 2015 supplier study; a Bridgestone EMEA M&A case; "Tires make up their own segment" and +20% BEV tyre consumption in the 2021 European aftermarket report; tyre Tier 1s' strength with truck fleets in the 2023 Fleet Imperative; and 82% independent share of tyre replacement on cars over eight years old in the 2014 European study. There is no BCG publication on Indian tyres, tractor or farm tyres, tyre dealers, rural channels or tyre brand advocacy; any such content in the C-files is our own analysis and must be labelled as such.
