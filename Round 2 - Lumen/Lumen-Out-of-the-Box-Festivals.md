# Lumen: sixteen out-of-the-box festivals

Fourth file in the set. Only the strange ones: nothing here is a festival of lights, a moon night, a coming of age, a stargazing night or a masquerade, and none repeats the fourteen festivals already in `Lumen-Round2-Scenarios.md` and `Lumen-Consultant-Lens.md`. Each is generated by one of the case's eleven facts, so each is authentic as well as odd. Written Monday 2 Nov 2026, deadline day.

Format per idea: what happens · why only Lumen could have it · the twist · the catch.

---

## A. The Open Sleep
- **What happens.** Once a year the whole city sleeps outdoors, together, on the open ground: 50,000 people, one ten-hour sleep, under the sky, while the night's Watch keeps guard. The festival's main activity is unconsciousness. The peak is the waking: 50,000 people opening their eyes at the same dawn.
- **Why only Lumen.** Fact 9 (a 10-hour sleep stretch) is the one fact no team will build on; fact 8 means the sky above them is doing something; open air is uncapped, so the 1,000-room is irrelevant.
- **The twist.** A culture whose central act is the thing its people do alone every night, done together once a year. "Go home alone" is answered by not going home.
- **The catch.** Weather; the old; the Council will call it a safety drill, which it also is.

## B. The Unsealing
- **What happens.** Each year 1,000 citizens by lot write a letter to Lumen fifty years hence and seal it in the room's vault. From Year 51 onward, each year's festival opens the vault sealed one Ring ago and the letters are read aloud.
- **Why only Lumen.** On Earth a time capsule outlives its writers. On a 300-year life, almost every writer is alive to hear their own fifty-year-old letter read to the city.
- **The twist.** The festival manufactures a past by mailing it forward: the longest anticipation on any planet, fifty years long, for a people who had "nothing to look forward to".
- **The catch.** Fifty empty years before the first opening. Fix: a second, one-year vault from Year 1, so the city practises on short letters while the long ones wait.

## C. The Thousand Witnesses
- **What happens.** Every year 1,000 citizens by lot are forbidden to take part in the festival. They sit on the steps of the room and only watch, and their account is the year's sole official record. Everyone else is free to be completely absorbed, because someone has been appointed to remember.
- **Why only Lumen.** 1,000 a year for one Ring of 50 years is 50,000: in exactly one Ring, every citizen has served as a Witness once. The venue cap becomes the size of the city's memory.
- **The twist.** A festival that designs its own historians, in a culture with no history. The Witnesses' accounts are the only prose Lumen has.
- **The catch.** The Witnesses miss the fun, which is the cost that makes it a rite (costly rituals bond; the year you are a Witness is the year you understand the festival).

## D. The Only
- **What happens.** Every year a festival is invented that will be held exactly once. A hundred citizens by lot design it in secret over the last moon; the city performs it on the day; it is then never repeated, and nothing about it is written down except its name.
- **Why only Lumen.** Fact 10: no inherited traditions. Make non-inheritance the tradition. The only recurring thing is the inventing.
- **The twist.** Anticipation comes from not knowing what the festival is; memory comes from the fact that nobody will ever see it again. After 300 years, Lumen has had 300 festivals and kept none.
- **The catch.** Quality varies by year; the hundred need a brief (a budget in gas, a safety rule, one shared act for all 50,000).

## E. The Fault
- **What happens.** Once a year the Council secretly breaks one public system (a water line, the light, the transport, the gas exchange). The city has thirty hours to find out what is broken and fix it, together. Nobody is told what; the fixing is the festival.
- **Why only Lumen.** "Food, shelter and work are all taken care of, and life runs smoothly." Nothing on Lumen needs anyone. The festival manufactures one day when everything does. The secret fault mirrors the secret Head.
- **The twist.** A holiday that is an emergency. The people who fill their lives with work finally have work that matters, and it is shared.
- **The catch.** Safety classes (never medical, never the Watch); the Council's credibility if the fault is too easy or too hard; it reads as a drill, so sell the finding, not the fixing.

## F. The Thirty-Kilometre Table
- **What happens.** One unbroken table runs through every street of the settlement, and all 50,000 sit at it at once, in one line (at sixty centimetres a person the table is about thirty kilometres long). Each dish is passed hand to hand along the whole length, so the food travels the city before anyone eats; the last dish to arrive is the signal to begin.
- **Why only Lumen.** One concentrated settlement; open air uncapped; a people who go home alone.
- **The twist.** The image is the argument: a city that is one table. The relay of dishes takes hours and is the festival's suspense.
- **The catch.** Logistics (5,000 segments, one per ten households); the old and ill are seated nearest their doors; an Indian judge may think of a community feast, so the single unbroken line and the hand-to-hand passing are what make it new.

## G. The Empty Night
- **What happens.** One night a year the room is lit, its doors are open, and nobody enters. The whole city walks past it, slowly, in silence, and goes on. Then the festival proper begins elsewhere.
- **Why only Lumen.** The only fact in Lumen's past is that its gatherings were empty. The culture keeps one empty gathering a year as its memorial of the time before culture.
- **The twist.** A festival whose first act is to honour the absence it was created to end. Fifty years on, people who never knew the emptiness walk past it anyway, which is what a tradition is.
- **The catch.** Too clever if it stands alone; it works as the opening hour of any other festival on this list.

## H. The Changed Voice
- **What happens.** At dusk every citizen spends one breath of helium, their own currency, and speaks in a changed voice for the dark hours. For five hours nobody on Lumen sounds like themselves.
- **Why only Lumen.** Fact 4 (gas money) and fact 5 (a secret Head): a night when nobody can be recognised by voice, the Head included, and nobody can be solemn.
- **The twist.** The one Earth-impossible thing about money that glows is that you can breathe it. The festival of mirth, paid for in currency, on a planet with no comedy tradition.
- **The catch.** Small doses only (helium is safe briefly, the heavier gases are not); judges may find it silly, so present it as the night of laughter a culture needs and does not have.

## I. The Switch-Off
- **What happens.** Each year one thing the Council provides is switched off for thirty hours, chosen by lot the night before: water, light, transport, the food distribution, the gas exchange. The city lives one day without it, and talks about nothing else for a moon.
- **Why only Lumen.** The case's premise is the problem: when everything is taken care of, nothing is noticed. A planet with no scarcity has no gratitude; the festival manufactures one scarcity a year.
- **The twist.** A different festival every year with the same rule. The lottery the night before is the suspense.
- **The catch.** Exemptions for the vulnerable; the Council will fear it; it is the Fault without the puzzle, so choose one of the two.

## J. The Great Move
- **What happens.** For one moon a year, every household lives in another household's home, assigned by lot across the whole settlement. Work stays the same; home changes. At the moon's end everyone moves back, and the two households meet for the first time.
- **Why only Lumen.** One concentrated settlement, housing taken care of, and a symptom that is literally "go home alone": for a moon, you go home to a stranger's home.
- **The twist.** The social graph of the city is rewired once a year by force. After a Ring, everyone has lived in fifty homes.
- **The catch.** Property and privacy; gas vaults stay sealed; the Council will reject it unless it is opt-in, and opt-in with a prize (the ballot) is how it should launch.

## K. The Youngest Rule
- **What happens.** For one day the youngest Ring governs: everyone under 50 (on Lumen, that includes 49-year-olds) holds every post, including the Council's chairs; the elders may advise only if asked. Decrees last one moon.
- **Why only Lumen.** Fact 11: a fifty-year childhood. Lumen's "children" are adults by any Earth measure, which is what makes the inversion real rather than cute.
- **The twist.** The hierarchy inverted by age, the one axis a 300-year society will harden along.
- **The catch.** Safety classes again; the Head is exempt, which is itself the joke (nobody knows whether the Head is under 50).

## L. The Fixing
- **What happens.** Once a year, in public, the exchange rates between the precious gases are reset for the year, by a mechanism everyone can see: the result of a contest, a sealed lottery, or a vote of the room. Every household's wealth changes that night; the city watches the numbers move.
- **Why only Lumen.** A capitalist economy with several currencies at once (neon, argon, xenon, helium) has a rate between them, and someone sets it. Make the setting a festival.
- **The twist.** Monetary policy as a public rite; the anticipation is real because money is at stake.
- **The catch.** Technical; splits judges; strongest for a consulting jury, weakest for a creative one.

## M. The Introduction
- **What happens.** One night a year every citizen must introduce two people who do not know each other, and stay until they have exchanged names. 50,000 introductions in five hours.
- **Why only Lumen.** 50,000 strangers in one place who go home alone; a festival that measurably shrinks the distance between any two of them every year.
- **The twist.** The simplest rule on this list and the only one with a number that improves every year (the share of citizens who know each other).
- **The catch.** It is a ritual more than a festival; pair it with the Table (F) as the thing that happens at the table.

## N. The Full Meeting
- **What happens.** A festival fifty years long: every citizen must meet every other citizen once (49,999 meetings each, about 2.4 a day for a Ring), recorded by a token exchanged. The night the last pair on the planet meets is the festival, and nobody knows in advance which night it will be.
- **Why only Lumen.** 50,000 people, 300-year lives, no history: the first complete acquaintance of a whole population is an event no Earth city could hold.
- **The twist.** A festival with no date, decided by arithmetic.
- **The catch.** Convoluted for a slide; keep it as the long-form version of the Introduction.

## O. The Open Doors
- **What happens.** For thirty hours every door in the settlement stays open and anyone may enter any home; hosts feed whoever arrives. The gas vaults are the only thing sealed.
- **Why only Lumen.** A single settlement where everyone has a home and nobody is in anyone else's.
- **The twist.** A planet-wide open house; the one day the Head might be sitting in your kitchen.
- **The catch.** Visiting days exist in several Earth cultures, so the universality (every door, any home) must be the point.

## P. The Flip
- **What happens.** One day a year the city inverts its clock: work is done in the fifteen dark hours, by the glow of the city's own money, and the fifteen light hours are given to play. Sleep shifts into daylight.
- **Why only Lumen.** Facts 1, 4 and 7: light is money, so a night of work is a night of spending; the light hours, which work has always owned, are taken back for one day.
- **The twist.** The festival is an ordinary day run backwards, and the city glowing at night is the city paying for it.
- **The catch.** The glowing-city image is the Diwali reading again; lead with the daylight play, not the lit night.

## Which of these would survive a jury

The three with the strongest fact-chains are **the Thousand Witnesses** (1,000 × 50 = 50,000: the venue cap becomes the size of the city's memory), **the Open Sleep** (built on the one fact nobody else will touch) and **the Unsealing** (a 300-year life makes a time capsule a letter to yourself). **The Fault** is the one a consulting jury will like best; **the Only** is the purest answer to "no inherited traditions"; **the Thirty-Kilometre Table** is the best single image.

Each needs a ritual and an entertainment to make a full answer. Pairings that interlock without new invention: the Open Sleep with the Watch and the Naming (from Design 1); the Thousand Witnesses with the Day Line and the Long Story (from Design 2); the Unsealing with the Day Line and the Case (from the consultant lens); the Fault with the Circle and the Pull (from Design 5).

No Earth festival, country or religion is named on the slide.
