## Q4. Decision-making unit and published tyre buying-behaviour surveys

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed or thesis-level Indian study of farmers' tractor-TYRE buying behaviour (attribute rankings, brand awareness shares, who decides) was found in 6 searches across Google-indexed journals, KrishiKosh, Academia, ResearchGate and Scribd. The best tyre-specific evidence is J.D. Power's syndicated India tractor studies (large samples, 14 states), known only through press releases. Everything else on "who decides / what matters" is tractor-purchase or lubricant proxy evidence from single districts.

### Cited Findings
- [Single source, syndicated] J.D. Power 2018 India Tractor Tyre Satisfaction Index (TTSI), the first tyre-specific J.D. Power study in India: four factors measured - tyre durability, traction/handling, ride quality, appearance; durability was "the most important factor" for overall satisfaction (no weights published in the release). Satisfaction is higher when customers personally choose the original tyre brand fitted on their new tractor than when they have no say. Sample size, states and brand scores NOT given in the release; known only via MRF's press release (8 Feb 2019), which claims MRF ranked first. Reliability: medium-low (vendor-reported). — [MRF press release](https://www.mrftyres.com/news/mrf-ranks-highest-in-tractor-tyre-customer-satisfaction)
- [Single source, syndicated] J.D. Power 2014 India Tractor PPI and CSI (inaugural): n=4,789 owners who bought a new tractor July 2012-Nov 2013, fieldwork Jul-Nov 2014, 14 states. Tyres are one of 7 performance categories; engine/transmission, hydraulics/couplings and tyres have the greatest impact on satisfaction yet the lowest satisfaction. Excessive/uneven tyre wear is among the most frequently cited problems and ranked most bothersome. Industry average 203 problems per 100 tractors. Only 8% of owners are contacted by dealers about routine maintenance; 13% say service charges were higher than expected. — [Autocar Professional, 20 Feb 2015](https://autocarpro.in/news-national/tractor-owners-key-tyre-quality-service-timing-inaugural-jd-power-survey-7733)
- [Single source, syndicated] J.D. Power 2017 India Tractor PPI: n=3,440 owners in 14 states who bought new tractors Dec 2014-Mar 2016. Excessive/uneven tyre wear was the most frequently reported and most bothersome problem for the third straight year; owners with tyre problems rated overall satisfaction 99 points lower (1,000-pt scale). — [Tyrepress, Jun 2017](https://tyrepress.com/2017/06/j-d-power-tyre-wear-most-bothersome-problem-for-tractor-owners-in-india)
- [Proxy evidence - tractor purchase, one district] Adilabad district, Telangana (Kaustub et al., 2020, AJAEES): Garrett ranking of tractor-purchase factors - fuel efficiency 1st, then subsidy, maintenance cost, horsepower, low price, recommendations, brand reputation. Tyres not a named factor. — [AgEcon Search PDF](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/357870/files/Kaustub3892020AJAEES60581.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345223655_Research_on_Factors_Influencing_the_Buying_Decision_on_Tractors_by_Farmers_in_Adilabad_District_Telangana)
- [Proxy evidence - tractor purchase, one district] Banswara district, Rajasthan: durability of tractor the leading factor (29% of farmers), brand image 28%. Sample size not seen in snippet. — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/96662532/A_Study_on_Factors_Influencing_Buying_Behavior_of_Tractor_Customers_in_Banswara_District_of_Rajasthan)
- [Proxy evidence - tractor brand, n=50, one district] Ambala district, Haryana: n=50 randomly selected respondents; Farmtrac most-preferred tractor brand, HMT close behind (old, unpeer-reviewed upload). — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/4858322/To_study_the_factors_effecting_sales_of_leading_tractor_brands_in_Haryana_India_)
- [Proxy evidence - tractor repurchase, one state] Mohanty & Patro (2020/2022), Journal of Agribusiness in Developing and Emerging Economies: tractor repurchase intention driven most by consumption experience, then image, satisfaction, trust; sample from one Indian state, authors warn against generalising. — [Emerald / DOI 10.1108/JADEE-05-2020-0103](https://www.doi.org/10.1108/JADEE-05-2020-0103)
- [Proxy evidence - lubricants, qualitative, n=80 farmers + 10 mechanics] Afton Chemical (additive supplier) tractor-operator study, presented Apr 2017; UP, Punjab, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, MP; wet and dry farming; >40 hp tractors. Operators trust peer groups and local mechanics most for oil selection and maintenance; sales reps, retailers, newspapers and advertisements play "a much smaller role". Farmers trust mechanics more than shop owners; one parts seller estimated 7-8 of every 10 customers follow the mechanic's advice (anecdote, not a measured share). Mechanic influence is earned over time; farmers follow it only if they believe the mechanic has no vested interest. No percentages published. — [Lube Report Asia / Lubes'n'Greases](https://www.lubesngreases.com/lubereport-asia/4_20/trust-key-for-india-tractor-operators/)
- [Proxy evidence - dealer study, Malwa MP, author/year/n not stated] Uploaded MBA report "Dealer satisfaction in Malwa tractor brands": buyers weigh horsepower, running cost, price, resale value, recommendations; peak tractor buying season Aug-Oct. Low reliability. — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/398334930/Kumbharkar-Amol-Ananda)

### Inferences
- [Interpretation] The J.D. Power finding that tyre satisfaction is higher when the buyer chose the OE tyre brand implies that at new-tractor purchase most buyers do NOT choose the tyre brand (the OEM/dealer does); this matters for Q9.
- [Interpretation] Because tyre wear is the top "bothersome" problem across three J.D. Power waves, tyre life/durability is the salient attribute for owners; this is consistent with, but not proof of, durability being the top replacement-purchase criterion.

### Gaps
- Who decides, who pays, who influences the replacement tyre purchase (owner vs son vs driver vs dealer vs mechanic): Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Attribute rankings for REPLACEMENT tractor tyres (price vs life vs grip vs warranty vs dealer credit): Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Unaided/aided brand awareness and brand preference shares for tractor tyres among farmers: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation (J.D. Power TTSI full report is paywalled; Apollo may hold it).
- Sample size and states of the 2018 TTSI: not published in available sources.

## Q3. Replacement behaviour

### Takeaway
Replacement is the larger part of Indian tyre demand overall (about half to 56%), and tractor tyres are a meaningful share of industry units; no credible published figure exists for tractor-tyre life in hours/years in Indian conditions, for purchase seasonality, or for the share of retreads/second-hand tyres. Non-farm haulage use is linked by J.D. Power to faster wear and more retreading.

### Cited Findings
- [Single source, dated] CARE Ratings (Apr 2017): replacement = 56% and OEM = 44% of total Indian tyre sales (all categories, FY2015-16); tractor front tyres ~9% and tractor rear tyres ~7% of industry units in FY16. — [CARE Ratings, Tyre industry April 2017](https://www.careratings.com/uploads/newsfiles/Tyre%20industry%20April%202017.pdf)
- [Single source] CRISIL Ratings (Jul 2025): replacement is "roughly half" of tyre sector annual sales; FY26 revenue growth forecast 7-8%, OEM demand from two-wheeler and tractor makers to rise 3-4%; flags cheap Chinese tyres intensifying price competition in replacement. [Forecast for FY26 parts] — [Business Standard, 18 Jul 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/tyre-industry-growth-forecast-fy26-crisil-125071800815_1.html)
- [Unverified secondary claim - market research vendor] Credence Research: replacement is >65% of agricultural OTR tyre demand in India; attributes wear to harsh soil, seasonal overuse, long hours. Low reliability (vendor estimate, no method). — [Credence Research](https://www.credenceresearch.com/?p=142372)
- [Single source, syndicated] J.D. Power 2017 PPI (n=3,440, 14 states): using tractors for non-agricultural work raised reports of excessive tyre wear by 6.5 problems per 100 tractors vs farm-only use; a higher share of non-farm users had already replaced or retreaded their original tyres (no share given). Fieldwork Dec 2016-Apr 2017, eligible tractors 12-24 months old, so this replacement/retreading happened within the first two years of ownership. Industry average 150 problems per 100 tractors in 2017 (245 in 2016); 49% of owners reported at least one problem. — also [Motor India, 27 Jun 2017](https://www.motorindiaonline.in/excessive-tyre-wear-due-to-non-farming-activities-a-bothersome-problem-for-tractor-owners-in-india-j-d-power-finds/) Causes cited: wrong inflation pressure, switching between field and paved road, uneven load during haulage. — [Tyrepress, Jun 2017](https://tyrepress.com/2017/06/j-d-power-tyre-wear-most-bothersome-problem-for-tractor-owners-in-india)
- [Single source, dated] Farm tyre radialisation in India "at a very nascent stage", majority bias-ply (TechSci-based trade piece, c.2013). — [The Machinist](https://www.themachinist.in/news/368/farm-tyre-market-grows-2009-techsci-research-report)

### Inferences
- [Interpretation] Since tractor fleet (park) far exceeds annual new tractor sales (see Q1), replacement must be the dominant source of tractor-tyre volume, but the actual OEM:replacement split for farm tyres is not published by a credible independent source.

### Gaps
- Rear/front tractor tyre life in hours or years in Indian conditions: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Triggers for replacement (wear vs cuts/punctures vs season start vs resale): Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Seasonality of tyre purchases (pre-kharif, pre-rabi, post-harvest): Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. Only proxy: Malwa tractor dealers report Aug-Oct as peak tractor-buying season (low reliability, see Q4).
- Share of retreads, second-hand tyres, unorganised/lookalike brands in farm tyres: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Trailer-tyre demand from tractor-trolleys: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

## Q1. Who owns tractors

### Takeaway
India's tractor park is roughly 6 million four-wheel tractors (late-2010s estimates), heavily concentrated in UP, Rajasthan, MP, Punjab and Haryana; ownership is concentrated among farmers with >2 ha, while most small and marginal farmers access tractors by hiring. The latest farm-size-wise national ownership figure found is for 2009; no 2016-17 Input Survey, NSS 77th (2019) or NAFIS 2021-22 tractor-ownership table could be retrieved in this round.

### Cited Findings
- [Single source - registration data] Cumulative tractor registrations, Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (via IFPRI): 5.81 million tractors all-India in 2012 vs 0.52 million in 1982. State shares 2012: Uttar Pradesh 1.106 m (19.0%), Rajasthan 0.700 m (12.0%), Madhya Pradesh 0.661 m (11.4%), Punjab 0.518 m (8.9%), Haryana 0.517 m (8.9%), Gujarat 0.495 m (8.5%), Maharashtra 0.419 m (7.2%), Karnataka 0.364 m (6.3%), Andhra Pradesh (undivided) 0.342 m (5.9%), Bihar 0.267 m (4.6%), Tamil Nadu 0.187 m (3.2%), Odisha 1.4%, West Bengal 0.6%, Kerala 0.2%. In 1982 UP+Punjab+Haryana held ~60% of tractors; by 2012 <40%, though they had only 18% of cropped area (2010). Caveat: registrations are cumulative and may include scrapped tractors, so this likely overstates tractors in use [Interpretation]. — [Bhattarai, Singh, Takeshima & Shekhawat, IFPRI Discussion Paper 1715 (2018), Table 3.2](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/handle/10568/146967)
- [Single source - expert estimate] About 90% of land preparation in India is done by four-wheel tractors, which "number about 6 million" (citing CSAM & UNESCAP 2016); 2.6 million tractors in 2000. Popular horsepower for 4WTs has been 30-50 hp. — [IFPRI DP 1715 (2018), note to Table 2.1 and conclusions](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/handle/10568/146967)
- [Single source - secondary compilation] Tractor ownership by farm size, 2009: 38% of large farmers (>10 ha) owned tractors, 18% of medium farmers (2-10 ha), and less than 1% of small and marginal farmers (<2 ha). Large and medium tractor owners provide hiring services to smallholders. (IFPRI cites Shah & Kanodia 2015 and Goel & Kumar 2013; underlying survey not named.) — [IFPRI DP 1715 (2018), Table 3.1 and text](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/handle/10568/146967)
- [Single source] Even small and medium farmers with 2-3 ha now buy tractors individually and hire them out to fellow farmers "for both farm and nonfarm uses", making the purchase "more of an entrepreneurial move" than a simple investment in ploughing their own land. Econometric analysis (ICRISAT VDSA panel, 18 villages) finds tractor ownership is led by the motive to expand farm size. — [IFPRI DP 1715 (2018), pp. 5-6, 40](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/handle/10568/146967)
- [Single source - small-farm machine use, Input Survey 2011-12 via secondary] Even among small and marginal holdings (<2 ha), about 44% of farmers use farm machinery (owned or hired). — [Bonn University (ZEF) dissertation record, cites Input Survey 2011-12](https://bonndoc.ulb.uni-bonn.de/xmlui/handle/20.500.11811/9508) (page blocked to automated fetch; figure from search snippet only)
- [Single source, historical] NSS 1991-92 Land & Livestock Holdings survey: positive relationship between tractor ownership and operated area, getting less steep over time. — [RBI Bulletin review](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/publications/rbi-bulletin/survey-on-land-and-livestock-holdings-1991-92-a-brief-review-735)
- [Unverified secondary claim - industry presentation] Afton Chemical (lubricant additive firm) at the ICIS Indian Base Oils & Lubricants Conference, Apr 2017: "about 30% of India's 7 million+ tractors are used in non-farming applications"; >600,000 tractors sold in 2016. No method stated; the 7 million figure conflicts mildly with the IFPRI ~6 million (different years/bases). — [Lube Report Asia / Lubes'n'Greases](https://www.lubesngreases.com/lubereport-asia/4_20/trust-key-for-india-tractor-operators/)
- [Verified fact - context] NABARD NAFIS 2021-22: 57% of rural households were agricultural households (48% in 2016-17); average landholding of agricultural households fell from 1.08 ha (2016-17) to 0.74 ha (2021-22). NAFIS headline releases contain no tractor-ownership figure. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/monthly-income-of-rural-households-increased-by-57-6-per-cent-in-5-years-nabard-survey/2305670); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/agrarian-crisis-landholding-recedes-by-13rd-loans-swell-for-farm-households-finds-nabard-survey)

### Inferences
- [Interpretation] The tractor-owning decision maker is typically a medium/large landholder or a 2-3 ha "entrepreneur" farmer who also rents out; the much larger mass of marginal farmers are users of hired tractor services and so are not direct tyre buyers. Tyre marketing that targets "farmers" in general reaches mostly non-owners.

### Gaps
- Tractor ownership by size class from Input Survey 2016-17 (all-India report on inputsurvey.da.gov.in) - not retrieved; Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation (or a direct download of the Input Survey 2016-17 all-India report).
- NSS 77th round (2019) SAS / Land & Livestock tractor ownership share - not found in published summaries; may exist only in unit-level microdata (microdata.gov.in). Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Current (2025-26) tractor park in use: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Share of tractors jointly/shared owned: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Share of tractor hours spent on haulage / non-farm work (national): Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. J.D. Power confirms non-farm use exists and wears tyres faster but gives no share (see Q3).

## Q7. Information and influence channels

### Takeaway
The only nationally representative evidence (NSS SAS 2012-13 and 2018-19) shows that fewer than half of agricultural households get any technical advice in a season; the largest named sources are progressive (fellow) farmers (~23% in kharif 2018-19) and input dealers (~20%), while KVKs (1.3%), extension workers (3.1%) and agricultural universities (0.3%) reach very few. This is about crop advice, not tyres [Proxy evidence]. Digital reach in rural India is large (488 million rural internet users, 2024) and farm YouTube channels have multi-million subscriber bases, but credibility/influence on purchases is only measured in small district studies.

### Cited Findings
- [Verified fact - official survey, all-India] NSS 77th round SAS (Jan-Dec 2019; NSO report 2021): 48.7% of agricultural households accessed technical advice on farm activities in Jul-Dec 2018 (kharif visit) and 42.2% in Jan-Jun 2019 (rabi visit). Only 0.5% of persons over 15 had formal training in agriculture. — [PARI summary](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/library/resource/situation-assessment-of-agricultural-households-and-land-and-livestock-holdings-of-households-in-rural-india/); corroborated by [RAS statistical note](https://ras.org.in/situation_assessment_survey_of_agricultural_households_2019_a_statistical_note); full report [MoSPI Report 587](https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/Report_587m_0.pdf) (>10 MB, not opened)
- [Single source - analysis of official survey, all-India] Patra (Foundation for Agrarian Studies, 10 Aug 2021), from NSS 70th (2012-13) and 77th (2018-19) SAS: any source kharif ~41% (2012-13) to ~49% (2018-19); rabi ~35% to ~42%. Progressive farmers (incl. private farmer organisations) kharif ~20% to ~23%. Input dealers ~20% (2018-19; not a listed category in 2012-13). Extension worker/ATMA kharif 6.2% to 3.1%. KVK 2.7% to 1.3%. Agricultural university 1.2% to 0.3%. All government sources combined under 10% (2018-19). Mass media: "slight reduction", no figure in text. — [FAS article](https://fas.org.in/access-to-information-and-agriculture-extension-services-for-farmer-households-evidence-from-the-situation-assessment-surveys/)
- [Verified fact] IAMAI-Kantar "Internet in India 2024": 886 million active internet users in 2024, of whom 488 million (~55%) rural (53.8% in 2023); 98% access the internet in Indic languages; one in five users uses a shared device, and women are 58% of rural shared-device users. — [IAMAI-Kantar report PDF](https://www.iamai.in/sites/default/files/research/Kantar_%20IAMAI%20report_2024_.pdf); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/india-to-exceed-900mn-internet-users-by-2025-125011600669_1.html); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-rural-adoption-ai-india-internet-2024-kantar-iamai-report/)

Bio-resource & Stress Management 14(12): Bhuvanagiri and Jangaon districts, Telangana, 12 villages, n=120 farmers: 84.16% used mobile phones/social media for agricultural information; YouTube the preferred social site (67.50%); WhatsApp the preferred messaging app (44.17%); 78.33% used messaging apps several times a day. — [IJBSM](https://ojs.pphouse.org/index.php/IJBSM/article/view/4919)

## Q2. Custom hiring

### Takeaway
District studies consistently show Indian tractors run roughly 780-860 hours a year and that a third to over half of those hours are hired-out (custom) work; in one Maharashtra district transport was ~31% of tractor use. This makes owner-operators who rent out (and haulage users) the heaviest tyre users, but there is no national figure for the share of owners who rent out.

### Cited Findings
- [Single source, one district; sample size not verified] Ahmednagar district, Maharashtra (KrishiKosh thesis; title/author/n could not be opened, PDF >10 MB): average annual tractor use 830 hours = 535 hours custom hiring + 295 hours own work (i.e. ~64% custom hire [Interpretation, arithmetic]); by operation, tillage 39.75%, transportation 30.72%. — [KrishiKosh bitstream](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/ce361500-67ae-4895-ac77-b69cb9b9267d/content) (figures from search snippet)
- [Single source, one district; sample size not verified] Sirsa district, Haryana ("An economic analysis of tractorisation in Sirsa district of Haryana", KrishiKosh/CCSHAU thesis): average annual use 784.41 hours, of which 321.90 hours (41.04%) own work and 462.51 hours (58.96%) hired-out operations; tillage the main operation. — [KrishiKosh record](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/items/989d45bc-569c-4227-aa8d-9762d41b4ff5) (figures from search snippet; page did not render)

## Q8. Regional differences

### Takeaway
Tractor stock is concentrated in UP, Rajasthan, MP, Punjab and Haryana, and mechanisation is most intense in the north-west rice-wheat belt; wet (paddy puddling) and dry farming stress tractors differently, and haulage on paved roads accelerates tyre wear. No study quantifies tyre wear or tyre choice by region or soil type.

### Cited Findings
- [Single source] State tractor stock 2012 (registrations): UP 19%, Rajasthan 12%, MP 11.4%, Punjab 8.9%, Haryana 8.9%, Gujarat 8.5%, Maharashtra 7.2%, Karnataka 6.3%, undivided AP 5.9%, Bihar 4.6%, TN 3.2% (see Q1). Tractors and combines are used most intensively in Punjab, Haryana and western UP and in the central rice-wheat belt; power tillers dominate in Kerala, West Bengal, Karnataka, Odisha and NE hill states. Popular 4WT power 30-50 hp. — [IFPRI DP 1715](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/handle/10568/146967)
- [Single source] J.D. Power 2017: faster tyre wear linked to wrong inflation pressure, switching between fields and paved roads, and uneven load during haulage; mismatched implements aggravate tyre problems (2014 wave). — [Tyrepress](https://tyrepress.com/2017/06/j-d-power-tyre-wear-most-bothersome-problem-for-tractor-owners-in-india); [Autocar Professional](https://autocarpro.in/news-national/tractor-owners-key-tyre-quality-service-timing-inaugural-jd-power-survey-7733)
- [Proxy evidence] Afton study: wet farming brings water ingress, dry farming higher hydraulic demand, dust and metal contamination (for transmission oils). — [Lube Report Asia](https://www.lubesngreases.com/lubereport-asia/4_20/trust-key-for-india-tractor-operators/)
- [Single source - engineering] In some regions the tractor cage wheel is the only means of puddling; Indian engineering papers study cage-wheel slip and fuel use but not tyre wear. — [ICAR IJHF paper](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJHF/article/view/94690)
- [Single source - product evidence] Tyre makers sell paddy-specific rear tyres (e.g. CEAT "Puddle X3" listed in 13.6-28), indicating a distinct wet-land segment. — [TractorJunction](https://www.tractorjunction.com/tyres/rear/13-6-x-28/)
- [Single source] Mahakoshal MP: 75% of sampled owners had ~35 hp tractors (Vegetos 2017, see Q2).

### Gaps
- Tyre life / wear by region, soil type (puddled paddy vs hard dry vs sugarcane/sand/brick haulage): Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

## Q9. OEM fitment and brand loyalty
