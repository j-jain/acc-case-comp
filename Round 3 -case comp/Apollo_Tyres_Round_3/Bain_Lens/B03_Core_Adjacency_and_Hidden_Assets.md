# B03. The core, the adjacency and the hidden assets

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the Bain-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using three ideas Chris Zook published at Bain: Profit from the Core (2001), the adjacency test (Beyond the Core, 2003; Lin and Zook, 2004), and hidden assets (Unstoppable, 2007), with the Repeatable Models test (Zook and Allen, 2012). It is not Bain's work. Sources: `..\research_notes\Q4_apollo_core_playbook_hidden_assets.md`, `Q3`, `Q6`.

**Purpose.** To answer three Bain questions: what is Apollo's core and how does it win there; is farm replacement a step away from that core or a different business; and what assets does Apollo already hold that farm has not used?

**Labels.** [Verified fact], [Fact - single source], [Reported by press], [Outside-in count], [Computed], [Planning assumption], [Interpretation], [Strategic hypothesis], [Bain-published], [Not found].

---

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **Apollo's core is a replacement business won through the trade.** Replacement is about 80% of its revenue; it holds about 29% of truck-bus radial replacement and about 20% of car replacement, built with branded outlets, a service and retread network, fleet programmes, warranty schemes matched to rivals, awards and driver welfare, not with consumer advertising. [Verified fact; Fact - single source; Interpretation]
2. **Farm is the one Apollo business run the other way round.** About 25% replacement; a Farm Zone format tried in 2019 at two outlets and dropped; the 2024 rural plan names two-wheeler, car and truck tyres and not farm; no farm trade scheme, loyalty programme or buyer record found. [Fact - single source; Verified fact; Not found]
3. **Farm replacement is one step from the core, not a new business.** It shares the rural dealer, the claim process, the service network and the trade levers; only the customer differs. Bain's research says more than three adjacency moves in four fail, and the ones that work repeat the core's formula. [Bain-published; Interpretation]
4. **The repeatable formula already exists in the truck business and translates line by line:** a branded point of sale with service, a settlement act the owner can see, a programme for the operator, a warranty matched to rivals, and welfare and recognition for the trade. [Strategic hypothesis]
5. **Apollo holds at least a dozen hidden assets that farm has not used**, including Rs 1,315 crore of dealer deposits, a warranty registration that already issues a number per tyre, a toll-free claims line, 25 rural vans, the Mahindra relationship, the trailer-segment position, a dormant farm awards platform, and the jersey itself, which carries about Rs 14 crore a year of farm's share of the fee and no farm activation. [Verified fact; Computed; Interpretation]
6. **The most important unknown is numeric distribution.** A third-party portal lists about 170 Apollo tractor-tyre dealers against about 3,690 for MRF. If even the direction is right, Apollo's farm problem at the counter is availability before it is awareness. Bain's Indian shopper research says brands lead through penetration, not loyalty. [Outside-in count; Bain-published; Interpretation]

## 4. The adjacency test

Bain's rule is that adjacency moves succeed when they are close to the core on customers, channels, capabilities, cost structure and competitors, and when the company repeats a formula it already knows. "More than three in four failed to deliver sustained growth" (Lin and Zook, 2004); new growth initiatives "have success rates of only about 20-25 percent" (Repeatability, 2012). [Bain-published]

| Dimension | Shared with the core? | Evidence | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customers | Partly: the tractor owner is also the village household most likely to own a car and motorcycles; the haulier is a small fleet operator | M02 section 6; Q6 section 6 | Half |
| Channels | Yes in principle: the same rural dealers; the same principal-to-principal terms; the same claims line. Unknown in practice: how many stock farm tyres | Q4; Q6 | Half, pending the count |
| Capabilities | Yes: tyre design (steel-breaker rear, 23-lug, radial), warranty administration, retreading, field service, dealer management | Q4 | Full |
| Cost structure | Yes: the same plants; farm earns less per kilogram, which is why no farm capacity is being added and 20 to 25% of farm volume will be outsourced | M02 section 5; Q4 section 4 | Full, with a supply limit |
| Competitors | Mostly the same names (MRF, CEAT, JK, Goodyear) plus BKT and the cheap tier | B02 | Full |
| A repeatable formula to copy | Yes: the truck playbook, translated | Section 5 | Full |

Four and a half of six. Farm replacement is a one-step adjacency: the kind that succeeds when the core's formula is actually applied, and fails when it is treated as a marketing campaign. [Interpretation]

Bain's Indian shopper research: "Brands lead because of penetration, not loyalty"; the leading brand in a category had 26.4 times the penetration of the tenth but only 1.7 times the frequency; winners invest in shopper visibility, hero SKUs and numeric distribution (2016). [Bain-published]

What follows [Interpretation; Strategic hypothesis]:

- Before any cricket activity, Apollo should know how many of its 6,500 dealers hold a rear pair of Virat in stock, and raise that number. A farm tyre that is not on the shelf cannot be recommended, guaranteed or registered.
- The portal counts are weak evidence, but they point the same way as the search share (14% against MRF's 47%) and the 9% replacement share: Apollo is thin at the counter.
- This is the first micro-battle in `B08_Delivery_Micro_battles_RAPID_Results.md`: "stock and show" in ten districts, measured as farm-stocking dealers and rear pairs on display.
