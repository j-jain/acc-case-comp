# 15. Consulting Benchmarks

Prepared 6 November 2026

**Purpose.** This file collects published consulting, research and company work that bears on one question: can a cricket sponsorship make Apollo Tyres the first tyre brand farmers think of, and turn that into farm tyre sales? It shows what each source did, what it found, and what the team can and cannot borrow.

Full source list with reliability ratings: 17_Source_Register_and_Verification.md

**Only publicly documented work is used. Nothing here claims that a consulting firm advised Apollo Tyres or any named company unless the source says so.**

> **How to read this file**
> - **[Verified fact]**: a primary source, or two independent credible sources.
> - **[Fact - single source]**: one source only. Where the notes read a report through a press summary or search snippet, the entry says so.
> - **[Interpretation]**: the team's reasoning, not a source claim. Questions 3 to 5 of every entry carry this tag.
> - **[Forecast]**: a projection, not an outcome.
> - **[Unverified]**: a secondary claim the notes could not confirm.
> - **ROI** (return on investment) is the gain from spending divided by the spend. **Activation** is the extra spending that brings a sponsorship to life (events, offers, advertising) on top of the rights fee. A **geo-experiment** compares sales in regions that get a campaign with regions that do not. **Difference-in-differences** compares the change over time in test regions with the change in control regions.
> - Each entry has five parts. 1 and 2 report the source. 3, 4 and 5 are interpretation.
> - Older sources are marked with their year. Rival tyre makers are covered in 07_Competitor_Analysis.md, cricket rights in 02_Cricket_Assets_and_Sponsorships.md, the measurement design in 14_KPI_and_Impact_Measurement.md and the budget in 13_Budget_and_Commercial_Model.md.

## What matters most

- **Sponsors rarely prove return.** The McKinsey piece (2014, US data, read through a summary) reports that about one-third to one-half of US companies lacked a system to measure sponsorship return fully. McKinsey attributes that figure to industry research, not to its own survey. [Fact - single source; read through a copy of the article]
- **Rights fees are only part of the bill.** The same piece puts activation at USD 0.50 to 1.60 per USD 1 of rights. Apollo's reported Rs 579 crore fee is therefore not the whole cost. [Fact - single source; read through a copy of the article]
- **No audited proof exists for a Team India jersey.** No source links awareness to sales for any past jersey sponsor. [Fact - single source]
- **The one consulting farmer survey found points to dealers plus digital.** In McKinsey's 2024 India survey, only about 1 in 10 farmers preferred digital at every purchase stage. [Fact - single source]
- **Farm buyers rank demonstrations first.** A 2020-21 Telangana study of 120 farmers ranked demonstrations above word of mouth and hoardings. The sample is small. [Fact - single source]
- **Trade-influencer programmes are common but unmeasured in public.** Castrol, Fevicol and Ambuja publish enrolment counts, not sales uplift. [Fact - single source]
- **Cricket success stories come from cheap, frequent purchases.** Cadbury and Thums Up numbers are self-reported award entries. [Fact - single source]
- **Rival farm-tyre programmes publish no results.** CEAT, BKT, JK and MRF show activity, not outcomes. [Verified fact for existence]

## (a) Sports sponsorship return and activation

### 1. McKinsey, "Is sports sponsorship worth it?" (2014)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Sponsors pay large rights fees and cannot show return. [Fact - single source] The McKinsey page was not opened; the notes used a [summary by Roaring Fork Agency](https://paul-birdwell.squarespace.com/rf-blog/2014/12/8/mckinsey-company-asks-the-question-is-sports-sponsorship-worth-it-measuring-sponsorship-roi) and snippets of the [McKinsey page](https://www.mckinsey.com/business-functions/marketing-and-sales/our-insights/is-sports-sponsorship-worth-it). US data, 12 years old.

**2. What approach was used?** Five metrics: cost per reach, unaided awareness per reach, sales or margin per dollar, long-term brand attributes, indirect benefits. [Fact - single source] Reported results: activation of USD 0.50 to 1.60 per USD 1 of rights; one consumer-goods firm lifted unaided awareness 15% by moving money to activation; one handset maker saw a 10x ROI gap across sponsorships. These are reported figures. [Fact - single source; read through a copy of the article] McKinsey attributes the one-third to one-half figure to industry research. "Up to 30%" gain from measurement is a consultancy claim, not a study.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Report cost per reach and unaided recall for the target group, not the total audience. Treat the rights fee as a fraction of true cost.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] The US ratios and the 30% claim. A higher ratio of USD 1.50-3.00 circulates on agency blogs but is not in the verified text [Unverified].

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Define reach as farmer reach by district, make unaided recall the main metric, and link it to dealer enquiries.

### 2. Nielsen Sports, "Sports sponsorships are raising more than just brand awareness" (March 2022)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Whether sponsorship does more than build awareness. [Fact - single source] ([Nielsen](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2022/sports-sponsorships-are-raising-more-than-just-brand-awareness/))

**2. What approach was used?** Analysis of 100 sponsorships in 2020-21 across seven markets and 20 industries. Method, sample sizes and controls are not disclosed. [Fact - single source] Result: an average 10% lift in purchase intent among exposed fans. Nielsen's own experience base says a 1-point awareness gain drives about 1% in sales.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Measure consideration and intent, not recall alone.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] The 1:1 awareness-to-sales rule: no data is shown. The study measures intent, covers a pandemic period, and is not rural or durable-goods focused.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Use it as an upper-bound sanity check and require a district control group before claiming a sales effect.

### 3. Team India jersey sponsors (2017 to 2025)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Whether the most expensive Indian sponsorship property paid back. [Fact - single source for outcomes; [Verified fact] for deal facts] ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/curse-of-indias-cricket-jersey-beyond-the-sponsorship-jinx-behind-sahara-byjus-and-now-dream11-79459.htm))

**2. What approach was used?** BCCI lead-sponsor rights across bidding cycles. Oppo (2017-19) claimed 80%+ aided awareness, from one brand commentator [Unverified]. Byju's left early (deal reported at Rs 1,079 crore; other reports differ). Dream11 signed at Rs 358 crore in 2023 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/corporate/story/dream11-will-be-jersey-sponsor-for-indian-cricket-team-till-2027-bags-rights-for-rs-358-cr-report-387789-2023-06-30)) and exited in August 2025 after India's real-money-gaming ban. Press blames specific events, not a "curse". Apollo's reported deal is in [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/this-partnership-is-about-national-pride-apollo-tyres-md-on-new-team-india-sponsorship-81033.htm); its stated aims were national pride, trust and category leadership, with no farm statement. Audited awareness-to-sales figure for any jersey sponsor: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] The exits were company-specific, so the jersey itself is not the risk.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Oppo and Dream11 sold to urban or digital, frequent buyers.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Measure Apollo's own farm-segment recall before and after, because no precedent exists.

## (b) Marketing-effectiveness frameworks

### 4. Binet and Field, "The Long and the Short of It" (IPA, 2013)

**1. What problem was addressed?** How to split budget between brand building and short-term activation. [Verified fact as a framework] ([IPA presentation](https://ipa.co.uk/media/14463/long_and_short_of_it_presentation_final_030424.pdf); [IPA blog](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it/))

**2. What approach was used?** Study of 996 UK campaigns, 1980-2010. Optimum about 60% brand, 40% activation; a 2018 follow-up gives 62:38. Brand effects need about six months or more to overtake short-term effects. A B2B split near 46:54 circulates but is not verified [Unverified].

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Plan two tracks: recall and a conversion mechanism.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] 60:40 as a rule. The data is UK award-winning campaigns from the mass-media era.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Show a separate budget line for each track.

### 5. Ehrenberg-Bass Institute and Byron Sharp: mental and physical availability

**1. What problem was addressed?** What drives brand growth. [Verified fact as a framework; read through summaries only] ([WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/feed/Physical_and_mental_availability_key_to_growth/9092); [Mi3, 2022](https://www.mi-3.com.au/28-03-2022/mental-availability-brand-rejection-binet-field-esov-and-ehrenberg-bass-new-b2b-data-0))

**2. What approach was used?** Panel data on buyer behaviour. Findings: growth comes from reach and being easy to buy; smaller brands have fewer and slightly less loyal buyers ("Double Jeopardy"); distinctive assets build memory.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] "First thought of" is mental availability. The link to sales needs physical availability: a dealer who stocks and fits the tyre.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] FMCG claims about reach and promotions, applied to a durable bought rarely through a dealer.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Track both by district: share of farmers naming Apollo first, and share of dealers stocking it.

### 6. McKinsey, "The Consumer Decision Journey" (2009)

**1. What problem was addressed?** The funnel misdescribes how people buy. [Fact - single source] Read through summaries; 2008-09 data, 17 years old. ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/consumer-insights/the-consumer-decision-journey), snippet only)

**2. What approach was used?** Survey of 20,000 decisions in five industries on three continents. Roughly two-thirds of touchpoints during active evaluation were consumer-driven (word of mouth, reviews). A "three times more likely" claim for the consideration set comes from a secondary summary [Unverified].

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Peer and mechanic voice matters late in the journey, which fits a tyre purchase.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Western consumer-goods touchpoints.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Map the farmer journey from first thought to fit-and-pay, and place cricket at the first stage only.

## (c) Rural consumer and go-to-market research by consulting and research firms

### 7. Accenture, "Masters of Rural Markets" (third edition, January 2015)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Why firms struggle to reach, acquire and retain rural customers. [Fact - single source, 2015] ([Retail4Growth](https://www.retail4growth.com/news/indian-companies-need-to-build-trust-and-loyalty-in-a-market-accenture-research-1331))

**2. What approach was used?** Survey of 2,800 consumers and customer service officers at 40 businesses; four rural segments. Finding: loyalty and trust initiatives improve rural competitiveness, yet firms still lean on conventional channels such as celebrity advertising.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Trust is the scarce asset; the reach-acquire-retain frame is usable.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] The 2015 numbers and the FMCG and finance skew.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Add a fourth stage, "fit and service", for a tyre.

### 8. Godrej micro-markets and NielsenIQ "Village Hit List" (2019, 2025)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Spreading distribution cost across India's villages. [Fact - single source, company-reported] ([Godrej CP annual report](https://www.godrejcp.com/annual-reports/2018-19/strategic-pillars/enhancing-go-to-market); [NielsenIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/report/2025/village-hit-list-optimizing-reach-in-hinterland))

**2. What approach was used?** Godrej defines micro-markets (district clusters) per brand and added over 100,000 rural outlets. NielsenIQ sells a priority-district report; its method is not disclosed on the page. No sales outcomes. A McKinsey, BCG or Bain route-to-market text: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Rank districts and concentrate effort.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] FMCG outlet counts.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Rank districts by tractor population and dealer coverage.

## (d) Farmer-specific research

### 9. McKinsey Global Farmer Insights, India view (survey early 2024, published 17 January 2025)

**1. What problem was addressed?** What Indian farmers want and how they buy. [Fact - single source] Read through [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/43-of-indian-farmers-are-embracing-digital-payments-in/485712) and snippets of the [McKinsey page](https://www.mckinsey.com/in/our-insights/indian-farmers-optimism-is-driving-interest-in-new-products), which was not opened.

**2. What approach was used?** Survey of 1,031 Indian farmers, part of a 4,400-producer global survey. Results: digital payments 11% (2022) to 43% (2024), mostly in person at retail stores; equipment insurance 4% to 23%; bank financing 9% to 36%; three-quarters cite high interest; only about 1 in 10 prefer digital at every purchase stage. McKinsey recommends mixing physical and digital. Share trusting dealers versus online: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] The retail point is where farmers still transact. Credit looks like a purchase lever.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Input-purchase findings applied to a tyre without checking.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Run a primary dealer-and-farmer survey in two districts.

### 10. Tractor buyer studies: Adilabad (2020-21) and J.D. Power (2018)

**1. What problem was addressed?** What drives tractor choice. [Fact - single source each; small or old] ([KrishiKosh](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810195610); [J.D. Power](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2018-india-tractor-product-performance-index-ppi-study))

**2. What approach was used?** 120 farmers and dealers in Telangana; 3,835 owners across 14 states. Fuel efficiency ranked first, then subsidy and maintenance cost. Demonstrations ranked first among promotions, then word of mouth, then vans and hoardings. In J.D. Power's study, 21% considered another brand in 2018 against 14% in 2015; younger cohorts leaned less on word of mouth.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] In-person demonstration and peers beat mass hoardings for farm buyers.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Tractor findings for a replacement tyre, which has a lower ticket and mechanic influence. This is untested.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Test whether the same ranking holds for tyres.

## (e) Indian rural go-to-market cases

### 11. HUL Project Shakti (from 2001)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Reaching villages with no distributor. [Fact - single source, company-reported] ([HUL, 2021](https://www.hul.co.in/news/2021/hul-enhancing-livelihoods-for-millions/), snippet)

**2. What approach was used?** Women micro-entrepreneurs selling door to door. 1,36,000+ entrepreneurs across 18 states by end 2020. Cost and incremental sales: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] A local person carries trust the last mile.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] The model sells low-ticket goods bought often.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] The analogue is a trusted local mechanic or dealer.

### 12. HUL Khushiyon Ki Doli (March 2010)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Reaching "media dark" villages. [Fact - single source, company-stated, 16 years old] ([Campaign India, 2010](https://campaignindia.in/article/all-about-khushiyon-ki-doli/412631))

**2. What approach was used?** Three steps: village gatherings (TV ads, demos, games), door-to-door trial, and a separate retail visibility team; several brands shared cost. Target: 14 million consumers in 35,000 villages in three states. HUL said trials, sales and distribution rose; no figures. Cost undisclosed.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Awareness, trial and retail availability arrived in the same wave.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] FMCG sampling.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Pair a cricket moment with a village demonstration and a dealer visit.

## (f) Influencer-channel programmes

### 13. Castrol India Fast Scan and Super Mechanic (2017 to 2019)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Lubricant choice sits with the mechanic. [Fact - single source, company press release, 2019] ([Business Wire India](https://www.businesswireindia.com/castrol-india-enrols-over-1-5-lakh-mechanics-and-retailers-on-a-unique-digital-incentive-platform-65085.html))

**2. What approach was used?** A QR code in the pack cap, scanned by a mechanic or retailer, paid into their bank account; plus a skills contest since 2017. Over 1.5 lakh mechanics and retailers enrolled by September 2019. Sales uplift: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. Gulf Oil programme: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Pay the influencer fast and tie the reward to a verifiable pack.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Oil is bought often and carries a pack code; tyres are bought rarely.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Use a tyre serial-number scan.

### 14. Pidilite Fevicol Champions' Club (2002 to 2014)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Carpenters recommend adhesive brands. [Fact - single source, old] ([Indian Television, 2014](https://www.indiantelevision.com/?p=129005))

**2. What approach was used?** A club for woodworking contractors since 2002, a points scheme from 2012, and an annual service day. About 22,000 contractors in 145 cities repaired furniture at about 325 institutions in 2013. Sales effect: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Identity and community, not only points.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] A charity event as a sales tool.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Offer recognition for mechanics.

### 15. Ambuja Abhimaan, Asian Paints Masterstrokes and agri-input farmer programmes

**1. What problem was addressed?** Influencers steer purchase. [Fact - single source each] ([World Cement, 2021](https://www.worldcement.com/asia-pacific-rim/25102021/ambuja-cements-contractor-loyalty-programme-wins-award-at-mint-marketing-summit-2021); [Campaign India, 2025](https://campaignindia.in/article/farming-for-change-how-innovative-marketing-is-reshaping-indias-agricultural-la/500465))

**2. What approach was used?** Ambuja: relationships, skills, rewards, certification; 70,000+ contractors enrolled in Ambuja Abhimaan in 2021 (2.93 lakh by its FY2025 annual report, [Adani annual report 2025](https://connect.adani.com/annual_report/2025/ambuja/intellectual-capital.html)) and an award at the Mint Marketing Summit 2021 (an award, not a sales result). Asian Paints: points for contractors. Agri-input firms: demonstrations and meetings (one firm held 514 meetings for 25,000+ farmers). UPL, Syngenta and Bayer figures: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Certification and training give the influencer a reason to stay.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Treating enrolment as success.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Count enrolled mechanics who generate leads.

## (g) Cricket and sport for mass or rural brand building, including critiques

### 16. Cadbury Dairy Milk x IPL and Thums Up x ICC events (2023 to 2025)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Growing share through cricket moments. [Fact - single source; self-reported award entries] ([WARC, Cadbury](https://www.warc.com/content/article/rankings-media/cadbury-proud-sponsors-of-the-ground-staff/en-gb/154445); [WARC, Thums Up](https://www.warc.com/content/article/EMVIES/Thums_Up_Toofani_Tour/161186), snippet)

**2. What approach was used?** Cadbury: IPL campaign, third year (2024): sales +45%, penetration +6.9%, spontaneous awareness +3%, consideration +2%; budget not stated. Thums Up: "Toofani Tour" +9% spontaneous awareness, +1% share, 100 million reached.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Tie a cricket moment to a purchase mechanic.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] The sales percentages: low-ticket, frequent purchases, self-reported.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Set targets in awareness points, not sales percent.

### 17. Tractor and tyre brands in cricket (2016 to 2026)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Building recall for farm and tyre brands. [Verified fact for existence; outcomes unavailable] ([Autocar Pro on BKT](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/bkt-tyres-signs-on-as-official-tyre-partner-for-eight-ipl-teams-ahead-of-2026-season-131803); [Tyrepress on CEAT](https://tyrepress.com/2024/02/ceat-extending-cricket-remit-with-ipl-partnership); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/india-funding-world-cricket-and-its-riding-on-paan-masala-tractor-rebar-deo-market/1841139); [Meri Kheti on Sonalika](https://www.merikheti.com/en/blog/sonalika-tractors-brings-jeetne-ka-dum-tata-ipl))

Independent recall survey of CEAT, MRF, Apollo and JK: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. Deal details are in 02_Cricket_Assets_and_Sponsorships.md.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Rivals already occupy farm-cricket space, so "first to think of" is contested.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Announcements without measurement.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Benchmark Apollo's recall against BKT and CEAT.

### 18. Critique: Business Standard, "Sticky wicket" (2007)

**1. What problem was addressed?** Whether Hero Honda's long cricket advertising protected its lead over Bajaj. [Fact - single source, opinion, 19 years old] ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/management/sticky-wicket-107040301017_1.html), snippet)

**2. What approach was used?** An opinion column. It questioned the protection and argued cricket suits mass FMCG better than other categories. No data is shown in the notes.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Cricket exposure does not by itself prove category defence.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Treat as a caution, not evidence.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Write the test so that it can show cricket did not work.

### 19. Documented farmer programmes of CEAT, BKT, JK and MRF (2012 to 2026)

[Verified fact for existence; outcomes unavailable] ([CEAT press release, 2016](https://www.rpggroup.com/assets/images/media/press-release-ceat-to-organise-15-in-uttar-pradesh-for-the-next-three-m.pdf); [BKT interview, 2018](https://sportzpower.com/sports-sponsorship-is-about-positive-contribution-to-society-2/); [JK Tyre, 2025](https://www.tyrepress.com/2025/05/jk-tyre-expands-retail-footprint-in-rural-india/); [MRF, 2019](https://www.mrftyres.com/news/mrf-ranks-highest-in-tractor-tyre-customer-satisfaction))

Power satisfaction ranking (2018). No sales or share results were found for any. Detail: 07_Competitor_Analysis.md.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Physical presence and third-party proof are the rival playbook.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Activity counts as proof.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Match the format, but add control districts.

## (h) Measurement methods

### 20. Google and Meta geo-experiment methods

**1. What problem was addressed?** Proving incremental sales from media. [Fact - single source] The notes read only the abstract and secondary summaries of the Google paper. ([Google Research, 2017](https://research.google/pubs/estimating-ad-effectiveness-using-geo-experiments-in-a-time-based-regression-framework/); [Google Meridian](https://developers.google.com/meridian/geox/time-based-regression); [Meta GeoLift](https://rdrr.io/github/facebookincubator/GeoLift/f/website/docs/Methodology/Methodology.md))

**2. What approach was used?** Vary exposure by region and compare. Google's time-based regression builds on 2011-12 geo-based regression, which needs many regions, and handles few regions. GeoLift uses an augmented synthetic control and runs power simulations to choose markets. Minimum regions and test length: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. No India effect sizes exist. Vendor blogs add that matched markets leak, so synthetic controls are preferred [Unverified].

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] This is the standard design for a district-level "measurable" claim.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] Online-conversion assumptions. Farm tyre sales are offline and slow.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Pick matched districts by tractor population and dealer count, and track dealer enquiries and invoices. Design: 14_KPI_and_Impact_Measurement.md.

### 21. Synthetic difference-in-differences and cluster-randomised village trials

**1. What problem was addressed?** Testing with few districts, and sizing a survey. [Fact - single source; the DiD points come from vendor documentation, low reliability] ([AEA trial registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/11589); [CGIAR paper](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/bitstream/handle/10568/117330/Berber_Discussion%20Paper%20Draft%20v1.10%20-%20in%20CCAFS%20Template.pdf); [diff-diff docs](https://diff-diff.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorials/18_geo_experiments.html))

**2. What approach was used?** Synthetic DiD (Arkhangelsky and others, 2021) suits a few treated markets; synthetic control needs a long pre-period and many comparison units. An Eastern Uttar Pradesh trial randomised 160 villages (40 per arm), assumed an intra-cluster correlation of 0.15 for rice and aimed to detect 0.3 standard deviations; awareness was a primary outcome. A Punjab and Haryana trial randomised 203 villages and found knowledge gains but no short-run adoption change (paper title unverified). Sample size for a proportion uses n = z²p(1-p)/E², multiplied by a design effect of 1 + (m-1) x ICC [Verified fact]. No brand-awareness ICC was found.

**3. What insights can be applied?** [Interpretation] Randomising villages is feasible in India, and adoption can lag knowledge.

**4. What should not be copied?** [Interpretation] The 0.15 ICC, which belongs to crop yield.

**5. How could it be adapted?** [Interpretation] Replace the ICC with a pilot value before sizing the survey.

## Implications for the case

- Published consulting work on sponsorship stresses two things: many sponsors do not measure return, and rights fees need activation spending on top to pay back. Apollo's farm tyres have had neither so far. [Interpretation]
- No audited evidence shows that a Team India jersey sponsorship turned awareness into sales for any earlier sponsor. A measured farm pilot would give Apollo evidence that no previous sponsor produced. [Interpretation]
- The one farmer survey found from a consulting firm suggests that Indian farmers still buy in person and that few prefer digital channels at every step. That supports a dealer-first design with digital in support. [Interpretation]
- Programmes that enrol trade influencers at scale (mechanics for lubricants, masons and contractors for cement, carpenters for adhesives) are an established format in neighbouring categories, although their sales effect is not published. A tyre mechanic programme would be following a known pattern, not inventing one. [Interpretation]
- Cricket results reported by consumer-goods brands come from low-priced, frequently bought products. They should not be used to forecast sales of a tyre that costs about Rs 16,000 and is bought every few years. [Interpretation]
- The rural cases that lasted (local entrepreneurs as the last mile, local-language demonstration, repeat contact) built physical presence first and advertising second. [Interpretation]
