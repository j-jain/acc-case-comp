# The Unsealing, refined: one idea, worked properly, with eight variations

Fifth file in the set. Everything here is one idea: messages sealed on Lumen and opened one Ring (50 years) later, by people who are still alive to receive them. Written Monday 2 Nov 2026, deadline day.

---

## 1. Ritual or recurring tradition?

The brief treats "recurring tradition or ritual" as one slot, so either label is accepted. The useful distinction is this: a **ritual** is a fixed sequence of acts with meaning in the form (you write, you fold, you seal, you hand it to the Hall); a **tradition** is the same act carried across generations by custom (the chain of sealings, Ring after Ring). The Sealing is a ritual in form and a tradition in time, and the slide can say so in one line: *ritual: the Sealing; tradition: the chain it makes; festival: the Unsealing.*

What makes the Unsealing a festival and not just the other half of the ritual: the whole city takes part, work stops, there is a visible countdown, there is a climax, and the emotion is high. Section 2 builds all five in.

## 2. The refined design: the Crossing Letters

### The arithmetic that makes it fit
- About 167 people reach each age every year (50,000 ÷ 300, stable population).
- Five Ring crossings in a working life (50, 100, 150, 200, 250) → five cohorts of 167 ≈ **835 crossers a year**.
- The year's dead ≈ 167; their households open the dead's last letters and seal a reply → **about 1,000 people a year with a letter in their hands, which is the size of the room.**
- The Hall (the 1,000-person room) is therefore the vault: its walls hold fifty shelves, one per year ahead, each holding a thousand letters. The room's cap is exactly the number of people whose turn it is. Nobody else ever needs to enter it.
- Five letters in a life: written at 50, 100, 150, 200, 250; opened at 100, 150, 200, 250, 300. Four you open yourself. The fifth is opened at 300 by you if you are there, and by your household if you are not.

### The ritual: the Sealing (Crossing Day, in the Hall)
1. Each crosser writes to the self who will open it in fifty years. The prompt is fixed, three parts, so letters can be read aloud without exposing anyone: **what I hope will still be true · what I hope will be gone · one thing I am doing this year that I want you to judge.** A fourth, private fold may be added and is never read aloud.
2. The letter is rolled into a glass vial (Earth element; glass keeps fifty years) and sealed with the Hall-keeper's mark. The keepers are elders past 250 (fact 11: the idle corps).
3. The vial goes on the shelf for its opening year. The shelf for *next* year's opening is lit all year: the countdown the whole city can see from the door.
4. One extra vial goes onto the shelf unmarked every year: the Head's. Nobody knows which of the thousand it is, and whoever reads it in fifty years reads the Head's words without knowing (fact 5, used lightly and without needing the Head's cooperation: if the Head writes nothing, there are 999 and nobody can tell).

### The festival: Unsealing Night (the last night of the year)
- **Dusk, the Hall.** The year's thousand enter the room, the only time most Lumenites ever will, and receive the vial that is theirs. The shelf empties. The next shelf lights.
- **The first dark hour, the open ground: the Last Words.** Before anything else, the households of the year's dead open the last letters, written at 250, and read them to the city. Lumen has no religion and no funeral; this is where one appears without being designed. It is the only hour of the year the whole settlement is silent.
- **Hours 16–19, the street tables: the Reading.** Each street of about fifty hears its own crossers' letters. The street's reader reads each letter without a name; the table guesses who wrote it (see the game); the writer stands, and then reads the private fold to no one. Fifty years of a neighbour's hopes, judged by the neighbour.
- **Midnight, the open ground: the Letter of the Year.** One letter drawn by lot is read to all 50,000. In some years it is the Head's, and nobody will know.
- **Dawn.** Opened letters go to the Archive, shelved by the year they were opened. Lumen's history is the list of what its people hoped, read in the order they found out.
- Work stops for the night; the city sleeps the first light hours.

### The entertainment: Whose Letter
A table game, native to the design and to fact 5. Letters are read without names; each listener names a writer before the writer stands; the street keeps a tally across the year's readings; the street with the best eye for its own people is named at the next Account or Crossing. It works because fifty years of living beside someone is the only study material, and because one letter a year was written by the Head and no one will ever score it.

### The fifty-year gap, solved: the Founding Ladder
In Year 1 nobody has anything to open. So in Year 1 **every citizen** writes one letter, and the Hall assigns each an opening year from 2 to 51 by lot, a thousand per year. From Year 2 there is an Unsealing every year, read by people one to fifty years older than when they wrote; from Year 51 the Crossing Letters take over and the Ladder is exhausted. The founders' hopes fill the first fifty years of the culture, which is the only founding myth Lumen will have.

### Assumptions to state
Stable population (~167 per birth-year); glass and a dry vault keep paper fifty years; the Hall's walls can hold 50 × 1,000 vials (about the size of a library wall); households are the civic unit for the dead; the Head's participation is optional; the prompt is public and the private fold is never read.

### Where it fails, and the answer
- *Maudlin.* The Last Words hour is one hour, first, then the night turns to the living; the Whose Letter game is there to keep it light.
- *Privacy.* The three-part prompt tells writers exactly what will be read; the private fold is theirs.
- *Storage and loss.* The Watch guards the Hall; a lost shelf is a lost year, so vials are duplicated: one stays, one goes home sealed.
- *"It is a time capsule."* A time capsule's writers are dead when it opens; here the receiver is the sender. That is only possible with a 300-year life, which is why the idea is Lumen's and not Earth's.
- *Copy-risk:* low. Schools on Earth do letters-to-your-future-self; no city does it as its year's end, and nowhere do the writers come back for them.

## 3. Eight variations (same seed, different plant)

| # | Variation | What changes | Who writes, who opens | Why it is different | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Crossing Letters** (the refined default above) | Letters to your own next crossing | The year's ~1,000 crossers and bereaved; opened by the writer 50 years on | The receiver is the sender; five letters in a life | Needs the Founding Ladder for Years 2–50 |
| 2 | **The Birth Letters** | Letters to a child born this year, sealed until the child turns 50 | Six strangers by lot write to each of the ~167 newborns; the child opens them at adulthood | The coming-of-age rite becomes reading what strangers hoped for you before you could speak; some writers will be dead, some will be in the room | The wait grows naturally: in Year 1 letters go to everyone already under 50, so openings begin in Year 2 and reach the full 50-year wait by Year 51 |
| 3 | **The Last Words** | Only one letter, written at 250, opened at death | Everyone retiring writes; the household opens it at the funeral; the year's ~167 are read together on one night | Lumen's funeral rite, on a planet with no religion; the dead speak once | Sombre; make it the first hour of a fuller night, not the whole festival |
| 4 | **The Promise** | Not hopes but one promise, sealed for a Ring | The year's crossers; opened at the next crossing in public | The Reckoning: the city checks what was kept; a culture of fifty-year accountability that a consulting jury will like | Shame; make unkept promises confessable, not scored |
| 5 | **The Question** | Each letter is a question to Lumen fifty years hence | The crossers ask; the city of 50 years later must answer each one on Unsealing Night and seal the answers for another Ring | A conversation with a fifty-year latency that never ends; each Unsealing is also a Sealing; three exchanges fit in one life | The answering takes work; cap it at one question per writer, one line per answer |
| 6 | **The Stranger's Letter** | Addressed to whoever draws it | The crossers write; fifty years later the vials are drawn by lot by that year's crossers | Every citizen receives, once a Ring, the hopes of a stranger, and if the writer is alive the two meet that night for the first time: 1,000 meetings a year across half a century | Less personal; the meeting is the festival, so stage it (the Meeting of the Letter, at midnight) |
| 7 | **The Breath Vault** | Each letter is sealed with one breath of the writer's gas | As in 1; at opening the fifty-year-old breath is charged to light the reading, then vented | Fact 4 joins fact 11: a forced fifty-year saving, read by your own old light, spent on the night you hear yourself; the vault is also the planet's only long-term fund | The glow risks the "lights" reading; keep it to one vial's glow at the table |
| 8 | **The Hopes Archive** | The opened letters are Lumen's only written history: no ledger, no chronicle | As in 1; the Archive is the festival's only product | A past made entirely of what people hoped, read in the order they found out; historians are unnecessary | Thin on facts; pair with the Day Line if the team wants a record of events too |

**Which to take to the slide.** Variation 1 is the complete one: it uses facts 3, 5, 10 and 11, has a festival with a climax, a ritual with a form, a native game, and a launch (the Ladder). Variation 2 is the most moving and the simplest to draw (a child, six vials, fifty years). Variation 6 is the most social. Variations 3, 4 and 5 are better as one hour inside variation 1 than as the whole night. Variation 7 is a one-line add to any of them.

**The slide triad for variation 1:** *Festival* Unsealing Night (the last night of the year; the Last Words, the Reading, the Letter of the Year). *Ritual* the Sealing (Crossing Day, in the Hall, three-part prompt, the lit shelf). *Entertainment* Whose Letter (the street guesses the writer; one letter a year is the Head's and will never be scored). *Assumptions* as listed above. No Earth festival, country or religion named on the slide.
