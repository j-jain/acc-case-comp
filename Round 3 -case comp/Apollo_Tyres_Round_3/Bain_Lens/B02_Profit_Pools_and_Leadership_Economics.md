# B02. Profit pools and leadership economics

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the Bain-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using two frameworks Bain has published: the profit pool (Gadiesh and Gilbert, Harvard Business Review, May-June 1998) and leadership economics with relative market share (Zook and Allen, Repeatability, 2012). It is not Bain's work. Sources: `..\research_notes\Q3_profit_pools_and_shares.md`; market arithmetic from `..\McKinsey_Lens\M02_Value_at_Stake.md`.

**Purpose.** To answer the question a Bain team asks before anything else: where does the profit sit in this industry, where does Apollo stand relative to the leader, and what does a follower do about it?

**Labels.** [Verified fact], [Fact - single source], [Reported by press], [Computed], [Planning assumption], [Interpretation], [Bain-published], [Not found]. Where nothing reliable exists: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

---

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **Indian tyres are a Rs 1 lakh crore industry with an operating-profit pool of roughly Rs 16,000 crore, and farm is a small corner of it.** Farm is 6% of Apollo's domestic revenue and 16% of CEAT's including off-road; no industry split by vehicle type is published. [Verified fact; Computed; Not found]
2. **Within farm, the pool worth fighting for is replacement.** It is half the units, the owner pays retail, and price rises pass through faster than at the tractor makers, where pass-through lags a quarter. But rating agencies say competition already caps replacement realisations. [Verified fact]
3. **At the counter the pool is thin.** A dealer keeps 10 to 16% of the price and loses much of it to slow stock; the retreader takes the tyre's second life at a quarter to a third of the new price; the mechanic takes nothing from the maker. [Verified fact; Interpretation]
4. **Leadership in farm has not bought margin.** Goodyear India, the farm leader at about 30% on original fitment, earned about 6% EBITDA in FY26 and saw farm sales fall 7%; Apollo earned 14.6% and MRF about 15%. The money is in the replacement counter, not in the tractor factory. [Verified fact; Interpretation]
5. **Apollo is a follower in farm replacement, at perhaps a quarter to a third of the leader's share.** Bain's research says only about 5% of weak followers materially change their position. Pushing harder across the whole market is the strategy that fails. [Computed on planning assumptions; Bain-published]
6. **So Apollo should lead two segments it can own:** the first replacement on tractors that left the factory on Apollo tyres, and the haulier, the owner who pulls a trolley and whose tyre wear every rival's warranty excludes. Everything else is reached through the national offer. [Strategic hypothesis]

## 3. Leadership economics

Bain's finding: "80 percent of variation in financial returns among all businesses in the world is accounted for by their performance relative to other companies within their industry"; leadership economics is "the greatest single explainer of relative business performance" (Repeatability, 2012). Bain's own caveat: "market share alone" does not correlate with profitability; "products at the high end stand to earn considerably more with a smaller share" (2005). [Bain-published]

### 3.1 Margins by company

| Company | FY26 EBITDA margin | Position | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| BKT | 23.9% (EBITDA including other income; about 21% without it) | Export off-highway specialist; premium niche with few rivals | [Vendor estimate from reported figures] |
| MRF | About 15% | Domestic volume leader; priced 8 to 9% above Apollo on the reference farm tyre; 47% of tractor-tyre searches | [Vendor estimate]; [Fact - single source]; [Outside-in] |
| Apollo | 14.6% | Leader in truck-bus radial (about 29%, now second by one broker's count); about 20% in car replacement; 6% of domestic revenue in farm | [Reported by press]; [Fact - single source] |
| CEAT | 13.1% | Leader in two-wheeler (about 31%); farm and OTR 16% | [Verified fact] |
| JK | 12.0% | Leader in truck-bus radial by CARE's description | [Verified fact] |
| TVS Srichakra | 7.6% | Two-wheeler and farm follower | [Vendor estimate] |
| Goodyear India | About 6% | **Farm leader at about 30%**, on original fitment to Mahindra, TAFE, Escorts, Sonalika, John Deere and Eicher | [Vendor estimate]; [Verified fact: HDFC Securities] |

Margins differ by up to half a point between financial-data sites (CEAT 13.1 to 13.2%; JK 12.0 to 12.4%); Apollo's 14.6% is the CFO's own figure on the Q4 FY26 call. The ranking does not change. [Vendor estimate; Verified fact for Apollo]

### 3.2 What the table says

- **Across the industry, the leaders in a segment earn more than the followers in it:** MRF, Apollo and JK in truck and car; CEAT in two-wheelers. Bain's rule holds. [Interpretation]
- **In farm it does not.** Goodyear's leadership rests on fitment to "almost all leading tractor manufacturers", where pass-through lags and the tractor maker holds the price. Its farm replacement fell 7% in FY26 on a "portfolio gap and intense competition", its parent spent 2025 reviewing a sale and then kept the business, and its Q1 FY27 margin was 4.3%. [Verified fact; Fact - single source]
- **So the lesson is not "be the farm leader"; it is "lead in farm replacement."** That is where MRF, with no published share, is the name owners search three times as often as Apollo and pay 8 to 9% more for. The premium-brand caveat also applies: BKT earns twice the industry margin on a small domestic share by owning a niche (radials, heavy duty, well reviewed online). [Interpretation]

## 4. Relative market share

Relative market share is the client's share divided by the largest rival's. No neutral farm shares are published, so every figure below is a triangulation and carries a wide band. [Planning assumption throughout]

### 4.1 The whole domestic farm market

| Step | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Replacement, at makers' prices | About Rs 3,300 crore | M02 [Computed] |
| Tractor makers' purchases | About Rs 2,500 to 3,000 crore: 3.8 million tyres at a realisation some 10 to 15% below replacement | [Planning assumption]; no OEM price is published |
| Domestic farm market | About Rs 6,000 crore (5,500 to 6,500) | [Computed] |
| Goodyear at about 30% | About Rs 1,800 crore | Consistent with Goodyear India's total revenue of Rs 2,476 crore, of which farm is the main part [Verified fact; Interpretation] |
| Apollo's domestic farm revenue | Rs 1,189 crore less exports; exports unknown; working figure Rs 900 to 1,050 crore | [Computed; Planning assumption] |
| **Apollo's share of the whole farm market** | **About 15 to 17%** | [Computed] |
| **Apollo's relative market share, whole farm** | **About 0.5 to 0.6 against Goodyear** | [Computed] |

### 4.2 Farm replacement

| Step | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Apollo's replacement share | About 9% (7 to 12%) | M02; rests on one broker note [Computed] |
| The leader's share | Not published. If shares of searches among the five brands (MRF 47%) over-state the leader by a third, MRF holds perhaps 30 to 35% of replacement | [Planning assumption]; Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. |
| **Apollo's relative market share, replacement** | **About 0.25 to 0.3** | [Computed on planning assumptions] |

A relative share below 0.5 is what Bain's published research treats as a weak follower, and "only about 5 percent" of them "materially change their positions over time" (Zook, 2004). [Bain-published]

### 4.3 Where a follower can lead

| Segment | Why Apollo could lead it | Evidence | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The first replacement on Apollo-fitted tractors** | Apollo is the incumbent: the tyre is on the tractor, the tractor dealer knows the owner, and 62% of owners did not choose the brand on their new tractor. Mahindra, Apollo's confirmed customer for two decades, sold 43.6% of India's tractors in the March 2026 quarter | J.D. Power 2018; Apollo press release 2017; M02 | [Verified fact; Fact - single source] |
| **The haulier** | Owners who pull trolleys wear rear tyres unevenly; Goodyear's warranty excludes that wear in writing and MRF's engineers reject it as "unequal contact pressure". Apollo holds 27 to 28% of the truck-trailer segment, introduced steel-breaker rear tyres in FY26, and positions Virat for "farm and haulage". Haulage is 31 to 34% of tractor hours in two district studies | Q6 section 3; Q4 section 1; Q6 section 6 | [Verified fact; Reported by press; Fact - single source] |
| **Hard soil** | Virat 23 is India's only 23-lug tyre; no rival product found | Q3 section 4 | [Verified fact: company claim; Not found for rivals] |
| **Farm radials** | Apollo claims "India's No. 1 farm radial"; radials are 2 to 3% of the market and need an Indian trial to prove their life | Q3; M05 | [Company claim; Interpretation] |

The first two are large enough to matter and are where Apollo's advantage is structural. The last two are regional or future. [Interpretation]

## 5. The money map: from whom would the points come?

A Bain team asks where each point of share would be taken from, because the answer sets the offer. [Interpretation on the evidence in Q3, Q6 and M04]

| Source of share | Why it is contestable | Why it is hard | The lever | Likely yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Goodyear** | Fitment-led, so its first replacements are open; replacement fell 7% in FY26; the lowest margin in the industry; its warranty excludes trolley wear; its search share is falling | Fitted to most tractor makers; the owner's first tyre | The haulier offer; the first-replacement programme on Mahindra tractors where Apollo, not Goodyear, is fitted | Highest |
| **The cheap tier** (market-channel pairs at half price, retreads, second-hand) | Large; GST on retreads cut to 5% from 1 November 2026 makes it cheaper still | Price-led buyers; the small owner of a second-hand tractor | Harvest credit on the entry tyre; cost per hour, not price | Low; do not chase |
| **MRF** | Six-year warranty against Apollo's seven; refused-claim complaints exist | Priced 8 to 9% above Apollo and still searched three times as often; owners rate it highest | Settlement speed; the haulier cover; the guarantee at the counter | Low to medium |
| **CEAT** | Most negative comments in the YouTube coding | Seven-year warranty plus a three-year non-defect cover ("Aayushmaan"); search share rising; off-highway 16% of its revenue and a stated push | At least match the cover; win on settlement at the dealer | Medium; CEAT is also the rival most likely to copy |
| **BKT** | Few complaints found; a radial and heavy-duty specialist | Strong reviews; eight IPL teams | Hold the mixed-use tyre; do not fight on radials without trial data | Low |
| **JK** | "Claims not heard" in comments; no cricket | 900 exclusive shops, instant claims on non-truck tyres in its rural format | Settlement speed | Low |

Reading: the base-case gain of 2.5 points in `..\McKinsey_Lens\M07_Economics.md` is most plausibly taken from Goodyear's first replacements and from hauliers of every brand, not spread evenly across rivals. That is what the segment choice in section 4.3 does. [Interpretation; Planning assumption]

## 6. What has to be true, and what Apollo can settle

| Belief | Today's evidence | What settles it |
|---|---|---|
| Apollo's farm replacement share is near 9% and the leader's near 30% | One broker note; search shares | Apollo's billing by channel; a dealer audit in 30 districts |
| Apollo's tyres are on a quarter or more of new tractors | Mahindra's two-decade relationship and 43.6% tractor share; no fitment share published | Apollo's supply records by tractor maker and model |
| Hauliers are a third of hours and the commonest early-wear case | Two district studies; the Goodyear exclusion; the MRF court case | Apollo's own claim records coded by cause; the owner survey |
| Replacement earns more per tyre than fitment for Apollo | Pass-through evidence; management's mix comment | Farm realisation and contribution by channel |
| The dealer's margin on a farm tyre is thin and slow | CCI record on JK; blogs; stock arithmetic | Forty dealer interviews |

For each row the data is Apollo's to pull in a week. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

**Status of the hypotheses tested here.** H2 ("Apollo is a follower and should pick a segment to lead") is **supported** on the market side and **open** on the fitment side: Apollo's share of new-tractor fitment, which decides how large the first-replacement segment is, is not public. Reason 1 of the Day-1 answer ("the profit is in replacement") is **supported**, with the correction that farm leadership through fitment earns little, which makes the replacement counter, not the farm market as a whole, the prize.
