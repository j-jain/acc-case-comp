# B05. Commercial excellence: the dealer, the mechanic, the coverage model and four sales plays

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the Bain-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using Bain's published commercial tools: the Sales Play System ("targeted, repeatable sales and marketing motions designed to achieve specific commercial missions"), route-to-market, the trade-spending rules (Vishwanath and Mark, 1998), best-demonstrated-practice benchmarking (2018) and the B2B Elements of Value. It is not Bain's work. Sources: `..\research_notes\Q5_influencer_loyalty_benchmarks.md`, `Q6`, `Q4`, `Q3`; the offer and dealer account in `..\McKinsey_Lens\M05_How_to_Win.md`, which this file extends.

**Purpose.** To turn the strategy into motions the rural sales organisation can run from December 2026: who covers what, which four plays, how the trade is paid, and what the best programmes in other adviser-led categories teach.

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **The dealer keeps 10 to 16% of a farm tyre's price and loses much of it to slow stock, and he holds about Rs 20 lakh of Apollo's deposit.** He is moved by money per extra tyre, by credit in the two buying seasons, and by the authority to settle a claim, not by margin points. [Verified fact; Computed; Interpretation]
2. **Cover the market in three tiers:** every rural Apollo dealer stocks and shows the hero pair; district farm hubs in dense tehsils carry service and the mechanic programme; the 25 rural vans reach villages under 5,000 people. [Strategic hypothesis]
3. **Run four sales plays, each with a mission, a target list, a motion and a number:** Stock and Show, First Replacement, The Adviser, The Haulier. [Strategic hypothesis]
4. **Pay the trade by Bain's rules:** only for incremental, registered sales; fewer and simpler schemes; never a tie-in, a price floor or a slab that rewards volume for its own sake. The last matters because the Competition Commission found tie-ins and resale price maintenance in this industry and fined Apollo Rs 425 crore for cartel conduct, upheld in 2022. [Bain-published; Verified fact]
5. **Build the adviser programme on what works in paints, cement, adhesives and lubricants:** verify the mechanic before paying him, credit within 24 hours, insurance and schooling for the family, a certificate, a public contest, and budget on active members, who are a quarter to a third of those enrolled. [Benchmark - external]
6. **Budget on numeric distribution first.** No programme pays until the tyre is on the shelf in the dealer the owner walks into; the count of such dealers is the first number the rural sales head reports. [Interpretation]

What this means for the plays [Interpretation]:

- A Rs 400 bonus on each rear tyre above the dealer's own baseline raises what he keeps on that tyre by 40 to 60% (M05). It is worth more to him than a point of margin on every tyre, and it costs Apollo nothing on tyres it would have sold anyway.
- Harvest-season credit on stock matters as much as the bonus; it costs Apollo about Rs 230 a rear tyre for 60 days (M05).
- The "creamy layer" complaint is a warning: a farm programme that concentrates rewards in the largest dealers will be resented by the rest and may repeat the 2007 grievance. Rewards should be per tyre and open to every dealer who stocks farm.
- One dealer app, not four, for registration, claims and rewards.

## 3. The coverage model

Bain's route-to-market question is where to deploy resources "to get your products to customers through the right channels", own salesforce or channel partners. [Bain-published] Here the channel exists; the question is which tier does what.

| Tier | Who | How many | What happens there | Cost behaviour | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Every rural Apollo dealer | The 6,500 dealerships and the farm-relevant share of 20,000 touchpoints | Target: every dealer in a tehsil with 3,000 or more working tractors stocks the hero pair; a count, not an assumption | Stock and show; the guarantee card; QR registration; the sponsor's mark; per-tyre bonus; harvest credit on stock | One-time board and display; working capital on stock; variable per tyre | Head of rural sales, through zonal teams |
| 2. District farm hubs | A revived Farm Zone in each dense district: a tyre changer, inflator, pressure gauge, an agronomist's visits in season | 30 in the first wave, up to 200 | The mechanic programme's home; claims settlement within the dealer's authority; camps; the haulier accounts | Fixed per hub, about Rs 1.3 lakh a year plus a share of the field team (M07's engine cost) | District ARD (the "One District, One ARD" owner) |

Tier 1 is the national offer of the McKinsey lens made physical: the offer cannot work in a shop that does not stock the tyre. Tiers 2 and 4 are where the field money goes, and only where a test shows they pay (M07, M08). [Interpretation]

## 4. Four sales plays

Each play has one mission, one target list, one motion, one offer, one number and one owner, which is what makes it repeatable. [Strategic hypothesis throughout; costs are planning assumptions]

### Play 1. Stock and Show

| | |
|---|---|
| Mission | Raise the number of dealers with Apollo rear pairs in stock and on display |
| Target | Every Apollo dealer in a tehsil with 3,000 or more working tractors; start with the 30 first-wave districts |
| Motion | The zonal team visits with the farm corner kit: a board ("Team India's sponsor"), a display stand for one rear pair, guarantee cards, QR registration on the dealer app; the first two pairs on 90 days' credit |
| Offer to the dealer | Rs 400 on each rear tyre above his own 12-month baseline; harvest-season credit; settlement authority up to Rs 5,000 a claim |
| Number | Farm-stocking dealers (count); rear pairs on display; registrations a week |
| Cost | Boards and stands about Rs 5,000 a dealer, Rs 1 crore for 2,000; credit on first stock about Rs 800 a pair for 90 days at 10%, under Rs 20 lakh for 2,000 dealers |
| Owner | Head of rural sales |

### Play 2. First Replacement

| | |
|---|---|
| Mission | Win the first replacement on tractors that left the factory on Apollo tyres |
| Target | Owners of Apollo-fitted tractors three to five years old, from Apollo's supply records and the tractor dealer's service records; Mahindra first |
| Motion | The tractor dealer's service visit or the health camp identifies the tractor; the owner gets a returning-owner card: the guarantee plus Rs 500 off a rear pair at any Apollo farm dealer; the mechanic who fitted the first tyres is enrolled |
| Offer to the owner | The guarantee and cover at today's price; the returning-owner credit |
| Number | Share of Apollo-fitted tractors replaced on Apollo, from registrations against the fitment list |
| Cost | Rs 500 a pair, only on sale; key-account time |
| Owner | Key-account head for tractor makers, with rural sales |

### Play 3. The Adviser

| | |
|---|---|
| Mission | Make the mechanic and the puncture shop recommend Apollo and register the buyer |
| Target | Eleven mechanics per field tehsil (M07's engine) and, nationally, every mechanic a dealer nominates; budget on active members |
| Motion | The programme in section 6: KYC and a unique ID; Rs 300 per rear tyre on a registered referral, paid to the phone within 24 hours; a certificate after a half-day on tractor tyres, pressure and load; a family accident cover and a child's scholarship draw; a district contest with a national final; tickets and shirts for the top referrers |
| Offer to the mechanic | Money, a credential, cover for his family, and standing |
| Number | Active mechanics (referred a sale in the quarter); referrals a mechanic a month |
| Cost | Rs 300 a rear tyre on sale; the platform and cover in section 6 |
| Owner | District ARD; programme design by the head of farm marketing |

### Play 4. The Haulier

| | |
|---|---|
| Mission | Lead the segment that wears tyres fastest and that rivals' warranties exclude |
| Target | Owners who pull trolleys and hire out (31 to 34% of tractor hours in two district studies); custom-hiring centres; brick-kiln and sand hauliers; FPOs and PACS that own tractors |
| Motion | The district hub holds a load-and-pressure camp before each season; the haulier's tyres are registered with the hour meter and the trolley's load; Virat or the steel-breaker rear is fitted; pressure checked each season |
| Offer | The guarantee with trolley use covered where pressure was checked; the first-season cut-and-burst cover; credit for a pair in the off-season |
| Number | Registered hauliers; rear pairs per haulier a year; claims per 100 covered tyres by use |
| Cost | The cover's reserve (B04 section 3.2); camp costs already in M07's engine |
| Owner | District ARD, with the head of farm product for the terms |

The McKinsey lens's "field engine" (a home-ground dealer, eleven mechanics, two camps) is Plays 1, 3 and 4 run in one tehsil. The Bain lens separates them so each can be measured, scaled or stopped on its own. [Interpretation]

## 5. Trade-spend rules, and the guardrail

Bain's 1998 findings: "only 50-60% of their trade promotion money ever reaches consumers"; the makers who do best "pay only for incremental improvements in sales" and "move to fewer and far simpler promotions". [Bain-published]

| Rule | Applied here |
|---|---|
| Pay for incremental sales only | The dealer bonus is on rear tyres above his own baseline; the mechanic and referrer are paid on registered sales; nothing is paid on volume Apollo would have sold anyway |
| Fewer, simpler promotions | One scheme for farm: the per-tyre bonus and the season credit; no quarterly slab contests, no gifts |
| Money that reaches the owner | The guarantee, the cover and the credit are the owner's; the trade's money is per tyre |
| Measure active share, not enrolment | Benchmarks show members give 80% of purchases at 35% engagement (JK Advantage) and 20 to 30% of registered advisers transact in a quarter (Q5) |

**The competition-law guardrail** [Verified fact; Interpretation]. The Competition Commission's record of this trade is the warning. In its order of 30 October 2012 the makers' own submissions put JK's dealer margin at 10.2 to 16.2% and CEAT's dealer discounts at 5 to 7%; the majority found "not sufficient evidence to hold a violation", but the dissenting member, drawing on a 2007 study cited by the Director General, held tie-ins (tubes and flaps sold with tyres), exclusive supply and resale price maintenance "so as to bind their dealers" proven. Its order of 31 August 2018 fined Apollo Rs 425.53 crore for cartel conduct through the makers' association; the Supreme Court dismissed the makers' appeals on 28 January 2022, and the penalty was sent back to the Commission for review that December (Q6). A Bain Results Delivery risk dialogue would put this first: the farm programme must contain no minimum resale price, no tie-in of tubes or front tyres to the guaranteed rear, no exclusivity condition on the dealer, and no exchange of scheme terms with rivals. The guarantee and the per-tyre bonus pass; a "sell only Apollo farm" condition would not. Legal review sits in the first 100 days (B08).

## 6. The adviser programme, designed from best demonstrated practice

Bain's benchmarking tool is "identifying and applying best demonstrated practices" (2018). [Bain-published] Thirty-one programmes were reviewed in Q5; none publishes its cost per member. The rules below are the ones the evidence supports, applied to a rural tractor mechanic. Figures in the "Evidence" column carry the labels given in Q5 (company pages are [Verified fact]; trade press [Fact - single source]; vendor case studies [Benchmark - external], the vendor's own numbers); the "Applied to Apollo" column is [Strategic hypothesis].

| Design rule (Q5) | Evidence | Applied to Apollo |
|---|---|---|
| Verify before you pay | A masons' programme with KYC and dealer-authenticated sales went from 4,662 to 13,347 members and +326% attributed bags in a year | KYC by Aadhaar-linked phone; a unique ID; a referral counts only when the dealer registers the sale with the mechanic's ID |
| Credit within 24 hours, deliver to the door | A rebar programme's active share rose from 40% to 72% on nightly credit and courier delivery | Rs 300 to the phone wallet within 24 hours of registration; physical rewards couriered |
| Points pay for the sale; welfare buys the relationship | Shell, JK Uphaar (10,000+ policies), Berger, Pidilite all carry insurance or scholarships | A group accident cover and a yearly scholarship draw for active mechanics; health camps through the foundation's centres |
| Certify, do not only train | ASDC-certified Castrol mechanics; Ambuja's empanelment; JCB-certified operators | A half-day course on tractor tyres, pressure and load, with a certificate the mechanic can hang |
| Public, competitive recognition | Castrol's contest drew 1.27 lakh entrants; Amaron's gives motorcycles | A district "Tyre Ustad" contest with a national final at a home Test; tickets and shirts as prizes |
| Budget on active members | 20 to 30% transact in a quarter; JK's members gave 80% of purchases at 35% engagement | Targets and budget on active mechanics, not enrolled |
| Feature-phone first | IVR in nine languages (Castrol); a radio programme onboarding 11,000 painters by missed call | IVR and missed call in Hindi, Punjabi and the regional language; the app for the smartphone tier |
| Turn the outlet into the claims point | JK's rural shops settle claims instantly; CEAT Shoppes settle on the spot | Dealer settlement authority (Play 1) |
| Field cadence beats media | Hero's rural vertical and camps in over 1 lakh villages took rural from 42-43% to 45% of its volumes in two years, and by press reports to about half by 2017-18 | The district ARD's calendar: two camps a season per hub; the vans' circuits |
| Plan a two-to-three-year ramp | Every benchmark's year one looks small | Gates in August 2027 and January 2028 read run-rates, not totals (M07, M08) |

**Cost of the programme at scale** [Planning assumption]:

| Item | Basis | Rs a year |
|---|---|---|
| Platform: KYC, IVR, app module, payouts | Vendor platforms for such programmes | About 60 lakh |
| Group accident cover | Rs 300 to 500 a mechanic for 10,000 enrolled | 30 to 50 lakh |
| Scholarship draw and certificates | 200 awards at Rs 10,000; printing | 25 lakh |
| District contests and a national final | 200 districts at Rs 25,000; a final | 60 lakh |
| **Fixed total** | | **About Rs 1.8 to 2 crore** |
| Variable | Rs 300 a rear tyre on referral, inside the Rs 1,255 to 1,475 per-tyre offer cost | Per tyre |
