# C07. The economics: what it costs, what it must earn, and why a chief financial officer would fund it

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the BCG-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis. It rebuilds the business case on the market and cost arithmetic of `..\McKinsey_Lens\M02_Value_at_Stake.md` and `M07_Economics.md`, reconciles every change to M07's lines as `..\Bain_Lens\B07_Full_Potential_and_Value_Creation_Plan.md` did, and then frames the result the way BCG's published value-creation work frames any investment: by what it does to total shareholder return and return on capital, and by what option it buys. It is not BCG's work. The TSR inputs are in `..\research_notes\S3_portfolio_cost_position_tsr.md` section 5; the BCG tyre study in `S2` section 1. Almost every figure here is [Computed] on [Planning assumption] inputs; none is a forecast.

**Purpose.** To show what the BCG-lens programme costs against the two earlier programmes on identical share assumptions, what its additions must earn, where the money comes from, and why it is fundable even though farm cannot move Apollo's share price.

**Labels.** [Verified fact], [Fact - single source], [Computed], [Planning assumption], [Interpretation], [Strategic hypothesis], [BCG-published].

## 1. The answer in seven lines

1. **The BCG-lens programme costs about Rs 13.7 crore to March 2028,** between the McKinsey lens's Rs 11 crore and the Bain lens's Rs 17 crore. The Rs 2.7 crore above M07 buys the haulier cover where hauliers are, the registration bonus, the mill-gate camps, one hospitality window with a control, the two clocks' stock and systems, the billing model and a small transformation office. [Computed]
2. **A point of share is worth about Rs 33 crore of revenue and about Rs 7.2 crore of net contribution a year,** against Rs 7.4 crore in M07 and Rs 7.0 crore in B07, because the cover is paid only on haulier-registered tyres. [Computed]
3. **The national offer breaks even at about 1.3 points of share,** against 1.0 in M07 and 1.5 in B07. [Computed]
4. **On M07's share assumptions the programme is cumulatively about Rs 6.9 crore negative at March 2029 in the base case,** against Rs 1.1 crore negative for M07 and Rs 10.9 crore negative for B07. The BCG additions must add about four-fifths of a point of share in FY29, or about four-tenths in each of FY28 and FY29, to beat the leaner plan. That is a claim; the test decides it. [Computed; Interpretation]
5. **Raw materials have moved since M02's 30% contribution assumption was set.** At a 25% contribution a point is worth about Rs 5.6 crore and the base case is about Rs 4 crore worse. The deck should show both. [Computed]
6. **The money is mostly a reallocation.** The trade and promise lines sit inside Apollo's Rs 632 crore of accrued incentives, discounts and warranty; the new money is about Rs 3 crore for measurement, the office and the hospitality experiment. [Verified fact; Computed; Strategic hypothesis]
7. **Farm cannot move Apollo's TSR, and that is not the test.** Six points of share is 0.7% of revenue. The test is ROCE and option value: the programme needs no plant, ties up a few crore of working capital, earns a contribution in the base case of Rs 18 crore in FY29 on about Rs 15 crore of spend, and leaves behind a counter, a clock and a registered base that rural car and two-wheeler replacement can reuse. [Computed; Interpretation]

### 5.1 What the comparison says, plainly [Interpretation]

- **On identical share assumptions the BCG-lens programme is about Rs 5.8 crore worse than M07's at March 2029 and about Rs 4.0 crore better than B07's.** It spends on the clocks, the registration, the haulier cover where hauliers are, the camps, one hospitality test and an office; it earns slightly less a point because of the cover.
- **The additions must earn the difference.** Rs 5.8 crore over two years is about four-fifths of a point of FY29 share at Rs 7.2 crore a point, or about four-tenths of a point in each of FY28 and FY29. The BCG lens's claim is that registration, speed, the haulier at the mill gate and the first replacement through the tractor dealer will add at least that; `B07`'s money map allows for six points from the same levers. It is a claim, and the test decides it.
- **The low case is the guard.** If only the offer passes and gains one point, the programme loses about Rs 15 crore by March 2029 against M07's Rs 10 crore. The avoidable lines are the haulier cover (narrows if claims exceed reserve for two months, B08's rule), the camps and the office (stop at Gate 2), and the hospitality (one window, not two).
- **What the cases do not count** is unchanged from M07 section 7 and B07 section 6.4: the won owner's later purchases, the car and two-wheeler tyres the same counter sells, the fitment business, the referral. The BCG lens's registered base acts on the first and the last directly.

## 6. The chief financial officer's three questions

BCG's value-creation work measures everything by total shareholder return and says of tyre makers that "sales growth remains the primary engine of TSR" (February 2026). Apollo's own return anchor is a mid-teens ROCE (the Q2 FY27 transcript prints "a mid-teem ROCE"). [BCG-published; Verified fact] The three questions a chief financial officer would put, and the honest answers [Computed; Interpretation]:

| Question | Answer | Arithmetic |
|---|---|---|
| **Does this move the share price?** | No. Six points of farm replacement share is about Rs 200 crore of revenue, 0.7% of FY26 consolidated revenue, and Rs 42 to 60 crore of contribution, 1 to 1.5% of FY26 EBITDA; on the revenue-growth leg of Apollo's TSR (10.3 points a year over five years) it is worth about 0.17 points a year. Do not present it as a TSR lever; judges with a finance background will see through it | M02 section 4; S3 section 5 |
| **Is it a good use of capital?** | Yes, on ROCE. It needs no plant (the alternative, a farm line, is Rs 430 to 790 crore for Rs 60 to 110 crore of contribution a year, M02 section 5); its working capital is a few crore of dealer credit and depot stock; in the base case it earns Rs 18 crore of contribution in FY29 on about Rs 15 crore of spend; at the FY30 full potential (B07 section 2) it earns about Rs 42 crore a year on about Rs 10 crore of fixed cost. Against the Rs 5,800 crore car and truck expansion that earns nothing until FY28, it is the cheapest growth Apollo has | M07; B07; S3 section 2 row 10 |
| **What does it leave behind if the share does not come?** | A registered base of farm buyers; a counter that stocks and settles in days; a trade paid per registered tyre; a tested measurement design; and the first Indian evidence on tractor-tyre life. The counter, the clock, the registration and the geo-test transfer to rural car and two-wheeler replacement, where management says the jersey's impact is "strongest" and where M07 section 7.2 valued half a point of rural car share at about Rs 7 crore of contribution. That is the option the programme buys, and it is worth stating as the second reason to fund it | M07 section 7.2; Q4 |

BCG's 2014 revisit of the matrix asks companies to "Measure and manage portfolio economics of experimentation". The programme is an experiment with a cash cap (about Rs 15 crore of loss in the worst case), two gates and a repeatable output. That is the sentence for the chief financial officer. [BCG-published; Interpretation]

If farm's actual share of the pool is far below its pro-rata Rs 38 crore, the ask becomes incremental and is still about 2% of one year's trade accruals. [Strategic hypothesis]
