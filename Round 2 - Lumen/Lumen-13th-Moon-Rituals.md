# Lumen: thirteen rituals for the thirteenth moon

Fifth file in the set. Written Monday 2 Nov 2026, deadline day. All thirteen are recurring traditions held in the thirteenth moon, and each plugs into the shared backbone in `Lumen-Round2-Scenarios.md`: a 32-day moon, 13 moons a year (13 × 32 = 416, no day left over), the 13th moon belonging to Sky and standing outside the four element quarters. None repeats the Watch, the Naming, First Rising or the Still (Design 1), or any of the 16 in `Lumen-Out-of-the-Box-Festivals.md`.

Format per idea: what happens · why only Lumen · the twist · the catch.

**Numbers used throughout.** A day is 30 h; sleep 10 h, so 20 waking hours a day and 640 waking hours in the moon. A relay of all 50,000 citizens at 40 seconds each is 2,000,000 s ≈ 556 h ≈ **27.8 waking days**, so a relay through the whole city fits inside one moon with four days spare. 50,000 words read aloud at 150 words a minute ≈ 5.6 h, about one dark. About 167 people are born each year, so about 13 a year are born in the 13th moon, and about 3,850 of the living (1 in 13) are "Skyborn".

---

## 1. The Whisper
- **What happens.** On the first day of the 13th moon, the secret Head writes one sentence and seals it with the Council. The sentence is then whispered from citizen to citizen, street by street, through all 50,000, never written down. On the last night, the 50,000th citizen says aloud what reached them. Then the seal is broken and the original is read. The gap between the two is the festival.
- **Why only Lumen.** A secret Head, exactly 50,000 people, and a moon long enough for one chain through the whole city (27.8 days at 40 seconds a link).
- **The twist.** The one time a year everyone hears the Head's voice, and it has passed through every mouth on the planet first. The difference between what the Head said and what the city heard is Lumen's first comedy and its sharpest politics. After 300 years, 300 pairs of sentences are the planet's literature.
- **The catch.** Earth's children's telephone game is the cousin; a whole state passing its ruler's words through every citizen is not. If the chain breaks, it restarts from the last link that can repeat it, and the break is remembered.

## 2. The Subtraction
- **What happens.** In the 12th moon the city votes on a ranked list of 32 things it would give up. Each day of the 13th moon, one more is removed and stays removed: day 1 music, then wheels, then trade, then light after dusk, then names ... until on day 32 the city has only people, ground and sky. At first light of the new year, everything comes back at once: the loudest morning on Lumen.
- **Why only Lumen.** Fact 7: "life runs smoothly" and nothing is noticed. And 32 days is exactly enough for a long, ranked list.
- **The twist.** The last thing the city agrees to give up is the thing it values most, and the ranking is public. Read year by year, the list is Lumen's history of what it loves: no other record shows a culture changing its mind this plainly.
- **The catch.** Water, food, medicine and the night watch are never on the list. Giving things up for a season has Earth cousins (a fast, a day of silence); the cumulative, voted, ranked removal is the new part, so lead with the ranking.

## 3. The Lowered Eyes
- **What happens.** In the Sky's own moon, nobody looks at the sky. For 32 days the city walks with eyes down; only the Skyborn (about 1 in 13) may look up, and each night they tell the street what the sky did. On the last night, at one fixed moment, all 50,000 look up together for the first time in a moon.
- **Why only Lumen.** Fact 8: astronomical events are common, so a moon of not looking means a moon of missing things, and a moon of having to be told.
- **The twist.** The moon that belongs to the sky is the one moon its people don't look at it. Not looking builds the hunger, and the shared look-up is the release. The Skyborn become the city's eyes without becoming priests: they only describe.
- **The catch.** It is an honour custom, not a law (hoods and brims are allowed, never masks). It needs clear skies on the last night; if cloud, the look-up waits for the first clear dark, and that wait is part of the story.

## 4. The Long Sentence
- **What happens.** Across the 13th moon, every citizen adds one word to a single sentence, in turn. You may read only the last thirteen words before yours. On the last night the whole sentence, about 50,000 words with no full stop, is read aloud through the dark.
- **Why only Lumen.** One chain through 50,000 fits in a moon (40 seconds each); 50,000 words read aloud take about one dark (5.6 h). The thirteen-word window is the moon's number.
- **The twist.** A people with no stories writes one a year, and nobody knows what it says until everyone hears it. Over 300 years, 300 sentences: a whole literature in which every citizen wrote one word of every book.
- **The catch.** The surrealists' parlour game is the cousin; a planet's only literature, one word per citizen, is not. Expect nonsense in places; nonsense is part of what gets loved.

## 5. The Uncounted Moon
- **What happens.** Nothing done in the 13th moon goes on any record. No wages, no ledgers, no minutes, no debts, no scores. It never appears in the count of anyone's age, and by custom it is not spoken of afterwards.
- **Why only Lumen.** A 300-year life piles up reputation and grudges; a capitalist economy records everything. The Sky's moon sits outside the four element quarters, so it sits outside the ledgers too.
- **The twist.** A planet that has to invent a past also invents a hole in it. Every Lumenite keeps two ages, the lived one and the counted one; over a 300-year life about **23 years never happened**. That is the year's freest moon, because nothing in it can be held against you.
- **The catch.** Crimes still count (the law runs all 416 days). Earth has days outside the calendar; a whole moon struck from every record is the new part.

## 6. The Last Time
- **What happens.** In the 13th moon, every adult does one thing for the last time in their life, in front of one witness, and says so: "This is the last time I will ___." The witness's name is entered against the vow. Break it later and you owe the witness a breath of gas.
- **Why only Lumen.** Fact 11: on a 300-year life nothing ends. A culture with no endings makes them on purpose: about 250 deliberate lasts in an adult life.
- **The twist.** Earth's new year adds habits; Lumen's sheds them. Breaking a vow carries a capitalist fine paid to your witness, so the vow is a small contract and the witness is a creditor.
- **The catch.** People pick trivial lasts; the street reads its best ones aloud on the last night, and trivial ones get laughed at, which raises the standard.

## 7. The Backward Moon
- **What happens.** Each night of the 13th moon, every street retells twelve days of the year, newest first (the twelve counted moons are 384 days; 384 ÷ 32 nights = 12 a night). On the last night of the year, the city is telling its first day.
- **Why only Lumen.** The 13 × 32 calendar divides exactly, so the year rewinds in exact, even steps, and the 13th moon is the only moon long enough to hold it.
- **The twist.** The year ends where it began, so every new year starts with the old year's first day freshly in mind. The Sky's moon is never retold itself, which fits the Uncounted Moon (5).
- **The catch.** A storytelling ritual needs material: pair it with a nightly one-line diary (the Day Line in Design 2).

## 8. The Lent Name
- **What happens.** On the first day of the 13th moon, every citizen's name is given by lot to another citizen, who lives under it for 32 days. On the last night the names go back, each with a spoken account from its borrower of what it did while away.
- **Why only Lumen.** 50,000 strangers in one city: names already mean little. A lot of 50,000 is a single shuffle of the whole city.
- **The twist.** You get your name back with a reputation you didn't earn. Over 250 adult years you carry 250 other names, and 250 people have carried yours.
- **The catch.** Misbehaviour under someone else's name is charged to the borrower (the lot is recorded, and stays secret until the last night). Gas vaults stay under your real name.

## 9. The Small Heads
- **What happens.** By lot, every citizen secretly becomes the "Head" of one other citizen for the 13th moon, and may issue them three anonymous decrees (harmless ones: eat with a stranger, walk to the edge, learn a song). The lot is one unbroken loop through all 50,000. On the last night, everyone stands beside the person who governed them, and the city finds it has formed a single ring about 40 km long.
- **Why only Lumen.** Fact 5: a secret Head of Government. For one moon, everyone is governed by someone they can't identify, and everyone governs someone.
- **The twist.** The state in miniature, played by the whole city. The ring is the revealing image: 50,000 people in one line, each beside a stranger who ran their moon.
- **The catch.** Secret gift-giving games are the Earth cousin; the decrees and the single loop are what make it new. A short list of allowed decrees keeps it safe.

## 10. The Sky Market
- **What happens.** In the 13th moon every citizen is dealt, free, one fifty-thousandth of the sky for the coming year, and the moon is the market: patches are bought and sold for gas. Whatever appears in your patch during the year (a meteor, a flare, a new light) is yours to name, and the holder of the patch where the year's brightest event happened names the next 13th moon.
- **Why only Lumen.** A capitalist planet whose sky is busy (fact 8) and whose people own nothing equally except time.
- **The twist.** The only thing every citizen is dealt an exactly equal share of, once a year, is the sky, and the city immediately starts trading it. Sky-watchers become traders, and forecasts of the sky become a market.
- **The catch.** The rich buy the good patches. Fix: patches return to the lot every year, and you may sell only half of yours.

## 11. The Fifty-Thousand-and-First
- **What happens.** In secret during the 12th moon, a hundred citizens by lot invent one person: a name, a job, a past, a voice. On day 1 of the 13th moon, citizen 50,001 "arrives": an empty house is theirs, letters from them appear in the streets, anyone may write to them and gets an answer. On the last night they leave, and their house is closed for a Ring.
- **Why only Lumen.** A planet with no fiction and a population that never changes in size: one extra person is the most noticeable event there is.
- **The twist.** Lumen's first characters live among the people instead of in books. After 300 years, 300 invented citizens are the cast children grow up with: a mythology made of neighbours.
- **The catch.** Letters answered by a fictional figure have an Earth cousin; a fictional citizen with a house, a job and an account is the new part. The hundred need rules: no copies of real people, no politics.

## 12. The Spare Moon
- **What happens.** In the 13th moon nobody works for their employer. Every citizen pledges their moon of labour to any project another citizen pitched in the 12th moon; projects with enough pledges are built in 32 days. That is about 24 million working hours a year (50,000 × 32 days × 15 light hours), allocated by choice.
- **Why only Lumen.** Fact 7: all adults work and have nothing else to do. Labour is the planet's real surplus, and this is the one moon it is free.
- **The twist.** A capitalist planet runs a free market in its time once a year. Every building on Lumen that wasn't planned by the Council is a 13th-moon build, so the city's skyline is a record of what citizens chose.
- **The catch.** Popular projects crowd out useful ones; the Council keeps a veto on safety only.

## 13. The Colour Turn
- **What happens.** Each year one gas lights every public lamp: neon red-orange, argon violet-blue, xenon blue, krypton lavender, helium peach. Which gas lights the coming year is chosen in secret by the Head and revealed at one moment on the last night of the 13th moon, when every lamp in the city changes colour together. That gas's price leaps; the whole moon is rumour and speculation.
- **Why only Lumen.** Money that glows (fact 4) and a secret Head (fact 5): the city's colour is monetary policy.
- **The twist.** The city changes colour overnight, and fortunes are made and lost in the same second.
- **The catch.** Anything with lamps risks the "festival of lights" reading; sell the colour change and the market, never the lighting. Insider trading by the Council is the obvious scandal, and Lumen's first one.

## Which would survive a jury

- **Most surprising:** the Whisper (1). It's funny, political, and only works on Lumen, and the arithmetic fits (one chain through 50,000 in 27.8 days).
- **Most beautiful:** the Lowered Eyes (3), which inverts the obvious "sky moon = stargazing" idea every team will have.
- **Strongest mechanism:** the Subtraction (2). Its public ranking of what the city would give up last becomes a yearly record of what Lumen values.
- **Purest answer to "no history":** the Long Sentence (4) and the Uncounted Moon (5).

## Combinations that interlock

- **Subtraction + Lowered Eyes.** The last thing given up on day 32 is not looking up, so the city's final act of the year is looking up together.
- **Whisper + Long Sentence.** Both are a chain through the whole city in one moon. Pick one per slide, or run the Head's sentence as the Long Sentence's first line.
- **Uncounted Moon + Backward Moon.** The year is retold backwards, and the 13th moon itself is never retold.
- **With Design 1.** The Lowered Eyes replaces the Watch's role for the moon (the Skyborn describe the sky), and First Rising is the look-up.

No Earth festival, country or religion is named on the slide; the Earth cousins above are for Q&A only.
