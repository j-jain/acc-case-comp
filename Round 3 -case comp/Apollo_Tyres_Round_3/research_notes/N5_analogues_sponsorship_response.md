## 1. Analogues with published outcomes

### Takeaway
The only analogues with published share-of-sales outcomes are Maruti Suzuki and Hero Honda, both high-ticket, infrequent-purchase goods with a dedicated rural sales force plus a dealer/outlet build-out. Both show rural share rising a lot over years, but the sources describe the sales build-out, not a sponsorship, and none proves causation. For cricket-linked brand pushes (Vivo, Oppo) share rose while retailer margins were also raised, so the effect of cricket cannot be separated. No tyre maker's farmer programme with a stated sales result was found.

## 4. McKinsey frameworks (short, own words)

Access: primary mckinsey.com pages NOT opened (timeouts). Items come from search snippets or named secondary copies.

(a) The consumer decision journey (McKinsey Quarterly 2009; Court, Elzinga, Mulder, Vetvik per snippet). Replaces the funnel with a loop: initial consideration, active evaluation (the set of brands can grow), closure/purchase, then post-purchase experience that feeds the next decision. Research Live says the study drew on a survey of 20,000 consumers across three continents; McKinsey's exhibit note (snippet) cites 2008 surveys in US auto, US skin care and German mobile telecom, and a 2009 US auto-insurance survey. Opened: Research Live https://www.research-live.com/article/news/purchase-behaviour-in-need-of-a-rethink-says-mckinsey/id/4000345 (secondary). Primary PDF (not opened): https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/McKinsey/Business%20Functions/Marketing%20and%20Sales/Our%20Insights/The%20consumer%20decision%20journey/The%20consumer%20decision%20journey.pdf . [Single source]

(b) Is sports sponsorship worth it? (June 2014; Jacobs, Jain, Surana per snippet). Five measures as relayed by a secondary copy (Birdwell blog, opened, 8 Dec 2014, https://paul-birdwell.squarespace.com/rf-blog/2014/12/8/mckinsey-company-asks-the-question-is-sports-sponsorship-worth-it-measuring-sponsorship-roi): (1) cost per reach (a retailer found 15% of its properties cost twice the average; reallocating lifted reach 20% at the same cost); (2) unaided awareness per reach (companies spent USD 0.50 to 1.60 on activation per USD 1 of rights; a CPG firm moving from an 80/20 rights-to-activation split lifted unaided awareness 15% by shifting toward activation); (3) sales or margin per dollar (a handset maker saw a tenfold ROI gap between top and bottom quartile sponsorships); (4) long-term brand attributes (brand strength said to drive 60-80% of sales; poor-fit sponsorships had negative ROI); (5) indirect benefits (a financial institution's golf sponsorship covered its cost through indirect sales). Primary (not opened): https://www.mckinsey.com/business-functions/marketing-and-sales/our-insights/is-sports-sponsorship-worth-it . [Single source, secondary]

(c) The granularity of growth (McKinsey Quarterly 2007; book by Baghai, Smit, Viguerie, Wiley, 2008). Core finding per snippet: split growth into portfolio momentum (growth of the markets a company is in), market-share gain, and M&A; where a company competes, down to fine segments, matters more than how. A food-industry follow-up put portfolio momentum at 71% of growth for 2008-12. Primary (not opened): https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/employment-and-growth/the-granularity-of-growth . [snippet only]

(d) McKinsey on Indian farmers (2024). India cut of the Global Farmer Insights survey: 1,031 Indian farmers (global 4,400, early 2024); electronic payment above 40% (43% in press) vs 11% in 2022; about one in ten prefer digital at all purchase stages; barriers cited are customer service and ill-matched payment terms; biostimulant use 13% vs 5%. Primary (not opened; fetch timed out): https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/agriculture/our-insights/indian-farmers-optimism-is-driving-interest-in-new-products . Summary: https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/43-of-indian-farmers-are-embracing-digital-payments-in/485712 [snippet only]. Other McKinsey rural-India or agriculture publications since 2019: Data unavailable – not searched beyond this survey.

(e) Pocket price waterfall. Introduced by Marn and Rosiello (HBR, 1992, "Managing Price, Gaining Profit"): starts from list price and subtracts on-invoice and off-invoice items (rebates, discounts, freight, co-op, financing) to reach the pocket price actually kept. Secondary sources only (Umbrex https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/pricing-frameworks/mckinsey-price-waterfall/ ; Vendavo https://www.vendavo.com/all/the-power-of-the-price-waterfall/). A vendor claim that pocket price runs 20-30% below invoice for most B2B firms is NOT McKinsey's.
