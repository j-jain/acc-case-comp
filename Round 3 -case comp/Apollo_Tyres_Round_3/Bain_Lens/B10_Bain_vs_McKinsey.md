# B10. The Bain lens against the McKinsey lens: same, different, and what to present

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the Bain-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** A plain comparison of the two re-analyses of the same case: `..\McKinsey_Lens\M00` to `M10` (6 November 2026) and `B00` to `B09` (7 November 2026). Both are our own work using frameworks the two firms have published; neither firm had any part in either. The Bain lens was written from a fresh Day-1 answer (B01 section 2) and reconciled here only at the end, so where the two agree, the agreement is a finding and not an echo.

**Purpose.** To answer the user's question: is the Bain answer the same as the McKinsey answer, or different, and what should the team present?

---

## 1. Where the two lenses agree

| Element | Both say | Why it is robust |
|---|---|---|
| The diagnosis | Apollo's tractor tyres are fitted at the factory and lost at the first replacement; about 9% of replacement against about 20% in car tyres; the jersey has not visibly moved farm demand | Two methods (an issue tree sized from the market; a profit pool and relative share) reached it from different starting points |
| The prize | About Rs 3,300 crore of replacement; a point worth about Rs 33 crore of revenue; 15% by FY30 as the aspiration | The Bain lens reuses M02's arithmetic and adds the money map |
| The offer's core | A guaranteed number of hours, registered at purchase and settled at the dealer; no price cut; pay the dealer and the mechanic per extra tyre; harvest credit; start with the owners of Apollo-fitted tractors | Both find the refused claim as the grievance and the adviser as the decider |
| The jersey's role | A trust mark at the counter and rewards for the trade, not the engine; no farm television film; no player endorsement; no IPL deal | McKinsey's five measures and Bain's three steps both fail the impression buy for a rural audience |
| Geography | The prize is spread out; the offer is national; field money goes only to dense tehsils; thirty first-wave districts in three belts | Unchanged |
| The test | A controlled, paired-tehsil design in thirty districts with gates in August 2027 and January 2028; no claim of pay-back by March 2028 | Bain's own playbook insists on test-and-control |
| Supply | From allocation and outsourcing, not a new line | Unchanged |
| The data request | The same five data sets in the first two weeks | Unchanged |

In short: the two lenses give the same answer to the brief's question. [Interpretation]

## 2. Where they differ, and why

| Element | McKinsey lens | Bain lens | Why the Bain lens differs | Which is better supported |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The frame** | A share-gain programme: size the prize, design layers, gate each | An adjacency of the core: farm replacement is the next step of a business that is 80% replacement and wins through the trade; repeat the truck formula | Bain's tools start from the core and its repeatable formula | Both; the Bain frame explains why farm is where it is (B03), which the McKinsey lens did not ask |
| **The first lever** | The national offer (guarantee, marks, rewards, credit) | Being stocked: numeric distribution counted and raised before anything else | Portal counts (about 170 Apollo tractor-tyre dealers against about 3,690 for MRF) and Bain's "penetration, not loyalty" finding | The McKinsey lens assumed 2,000 stocking dealers; the Bain lens makes it the first number to measure. Evidence is thin for both; the Bain order is safer |
| **The segment** | Heavy users, whoever they are locally; owners of Apollo-fitted tractors | The same two, named by what rivals cannot copy: Apollo's own first replacements, and hauliers whose trolley wear every rival's warranty excludes | The complaint coding, the court cases and Goodyear's written exclusion | The Bain lens's haulier segment rests on three verified sources; it is the sharper target |
| **The promise** | Hours guaranteed; cuts and impact damage excluded "as now" | Hours guaranteed, plus a first-season cut-and-burst cover and trolley wear covered; settled at the dealer in seven days | 23 of 39 tractor complaints are cuts, cracks and bursts; CEAT already covers non-defect damage for three years | The Bain cover answers the evidence but costs Rs 220 more a rear tyre and Rs 1.9 crore a year on existing sales; the test must earn it |
| **The trade** | A field engine: a dealer, eleven mechanics, two camps a year per tehsil | Four separable sales plays built on 31 benchmarked programmes: verify, pay in 24 hours, insure, certify, recognise; budget on active members | Bain's sales-play tool and best demonstrated practice | The Bain design is more specific; the McKinsey engine is the same elements in one bundle |
| **Competition law** | Not raised | A guardrail: no price floor, tie-in or exclusivity; written clearance before spending; Apollo's Rs 425 crore penalty named | Bain's risk dialogue asks what can stop the programme | A real omission in the McKinsey lens; this should be in any version presented |
| **The jersey** | A slim cricket layer (match days, support for one tournament) tested as an arm at Rs 1.2 lakh a tehsil | Experiences, each with its own control: hospitality for the 300 top dealers, a mechanics' final at a Test, sponsorship of existing village tournaments, dealer match days if licensed; tickets tested against cash | Bain's Zero-Click playbook and its matched-control hospitality case | The Bain version is more measurable and uses the rights Apollo already holds; it costs about Rs 2.2 crore against Rs 0.4 crore in the test |
| **The calendar** | Launch 21 January 2027 with the Australia Tests | Prototype from 1 December 2026 with the Sri Lanka series and the Zimbabwe ODIs; launch 21 January 2027 | A corrected calendar (BCCI schedule of March 2026) | Bain lens, on a verified schedule |
| **Funding** | Rs 11 crore of new budget in four tranches | Rs 17 crore reallocated from the farm share of advertising and promotion; no new money | Bain's zero-based tools; the Q4 FY26 call's 4% of sales | The reallocation is the better ask to a board; the farm allocation is not public |
| **Economics** | National offer pays at 1.0 point; Rs 7.4 crore a point | National offer pays at 1.5 points; Rs 7.0 crore a point; on identical share assumptions the Bain programme is about Rs 10 crore worse at March 2029 | The cover and the adviser cost money on every tyre | The McKinsey lens is leaner; the Bain lens claims its levers add at least three-quarters of a point, which the test decides |
| **Delivery** | Six initiatives with owners; a steering group; the first 100 days | Three micro-battles on three-to-four-week cycles writing a repeatable model; a RAPID chart with a named D for six decisions; a ten-risk dialogue; a three-layer NPS closed loop | Bain's micro-battles, RAPID, Results Delivery, NPS | The Bain version is more operational; both keep the same gates |
| **Measurement** | Owner survey in three waves; billing against control; recall explains, sales decide | The same, plus NPS and "whom do you ask", earned growth (repeat and referred revenue), the scorecard locked with finance before the budget | Bain's NPS system and the marketing-finance work | The Bain additions cost little and give the CFO a reason to believe |
| **Method** | One governing question; an issue tree; sixteen sub-hypotheses tested | One written answer; six hypotheses the research tried to break; three of six changed | Answer First against the issue tree | Each produced the same direction; the Bain route exposed two things the tree did not ask: the stocking count and the legal guardrail |

## 4. What the team should present

Three options, with our recommendation. [Interpretation]

| Option | What it is | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. The McKinsey-lens deck as built (7 November) | The existing twelve slides | Built, consistent, leaner, breaks even at one point | Misses the stocking count, the legal guardrail, the cover the evidence asks for, the December windows and the reallocation ask |
| **B. The McKinsey spine with six Bain additions (recommended)** | Keep the diagnosis, the prize, the offer, the thirty districts, the four-arm test and the gates; add the six items below | The best-supported elements of both; the deck changes on eight slides, not twelve; the economics stay honest | About Rs 3 to 4 crore more to March 2028; needs one more evening on the deck |

### 4.2 What option B leaves out of the Bain lens, and why

- The full adviser programme at national scale (Rs 1.9 crore a year of fixed cost) waits for Gate 2; the test tehsils run it at test scale, as M07's engine already does.
- The three micro-battle teams become the way the six initiatives of M08 are run in the first wave, without renaming them on the slide.
- The haulier is named as the priority segment inside slide 5 (the customer) rather than given a slide of its own.

## 6. Limits of the comparison

- Both lenses are our own work from public sources, with no Apollo data, no interviews and no contract. A consulting team of either firm would have all three in its first two weeks.
- The Bain lens uses Bain's published frameworks and its nearest published cases; Bain has published nothing on tyres, tractors or cricket, so no "Bain view of this industry" exists to borrow.
- The dealer counts, the complaint coding and the search share are outside-in signals, not measurements; each is labelled so.
- The economics of both lenses rest on the same planning assumptions, above all the 25% replacement figure from one broker note and the share gains in the three cases, which are assumptions and not forecasts.
