# C04. The customer by occasion, not by farm size: demand spaces and advocacy

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the BCG-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using two customer tools BCG has published: demand-centric growth, in which a market is cut into "demand spaces" defined by the occasion and the need rather than by who the buyer is (Silverstein, Bolden and Wald, 2015; Bolden and others, 2016; BCG's 2024 study of Indian two-wheeler buyers), and the Brand Advocacy Index, which measures recommendation as reported behaviour rather than stated intention (Knox and others, 2013; BCG's 2014 India study). It is not BCG's work. BCG sources: `..\research_notes\S2_bcg_publications_for_case.md` section 3 and `S2b` section 1. Field evidence: `S4_demand_spaces_tractor_owner.md`, with the earlier coding in `..\McKinsey_Lens\M04_Customer.md` and `..\Bain_Lens\B04_Customer_NPS_Episodes_Value.md`, which this file builds on and does not repeat.

**Purpose.** To replace farm-size segments with occasions of use, to say what each occasion needs and where it fails a tyre, to design the promise per occasion rather than one promise for all, and to say how advocacy should be measured.

**Labels.** [Verified fact], [Fact - single source], [Reported by press], [Outside-in; not a survey], [Computed], [Planning assumption], [Interpretation], [Strategic hypothesis], [BCG-published]. Where nothing reliable exists: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **The tractor owner buys a tyre for an occasion, and the occasions wear tyres differently.** Field work on his own land, hiring out by the hour, a trolley on the road with crops or cane, a trolley for money with sand or bricks, wet-paddy puddling, and the all-rounder who does not know which job comes next. Hours are published for the first three; failure is documented for the third and fourth; products already exist for the fifth. [Verified fact; Fact - single source; Interpretation]
2. **Makers already segment the product by occasion and nobody segments the promise.** Apollo sells FX 222 for "Deep Puddling", Virat 23 for "Hard Soil" and Virat as "an all-rounder" for agri and haulage; CEAT, BKT and Birla sell puddling and haulage lines; and in 2026 no maker in India offers any hours, kilometre or occasion-specific guarantee. The promise is the open ground. [Verified fact]
3. **The haulage occasion is where the tyre fails, where the buyer is an operator, and where rivals exclude the failure in writing.** Trolleys rated for about four tonnes carry ten to twelve tonnes of cane; a court has recorded that a farm trolley may lawfully run on the road only "for transportation of crops to the grain market"; Goodyear's warranty excludes "prolonged usage of Tractor with trolley in Haulage application". [Reported by press; Verified fact]
4. **So the promise is built per demand space:** hours for the field worker; a trolley-wear and first-season burst cover for the haulier, registered with the load and the pressure check; a season promise for the puddler; the two together for the all-rounder on Virat. Cover goes only where the failure is, which is cheaper than one cover for everyone. [Strategic hypothesis]
5. **Advocacy should be measured as what owners did, not what they say they would do.** BCG's index "looks at actual advocacy, not intention"; in BCG's India survey "57 percent of auto buyers cited friends and family as a source of information" against "only 35 percent who mentioned dealers", and "reaching the right 2 percent of advocates" "opens the door to 90 percent of a community". For tractor tyres the advocate is the heavy user whose tractor works most, and the mechanic who sees the failed tyre. [BCG-published; Interpretation]
6. **Apollo's advocacy problem is silence, not hostility.** In the coded comments Apollo has 19 positive mentions, 13 of them with no reason given; nobody has asked its owners to speak, and it holds no record of who they are. Registration at the counter creates the base; the BAI questions in the owner survey measure whether it talks. [Outside-in; Interpretation]

## 3. The demand spaces of the tractor owner

### 3.1 The occasions, with the evidence

Built from S4 section 7 on the district hours studies (M04 section 2; S4 section 1), the complaint and warranty record (Q6; S4 section 2) and the product lines (S4 section 3). The occasion is the first axis; the purchase moment (planned before a season, or on the day of failure) is the second and runs across all of them. Three readings [Interpretation]:

- **Spaces 3 and 4 are where the tyre fails fastest, where the buyer is an operator rather than a farmer, and where warranties exclude the failure.** That is the sub-sector `C02` chose to lead, seen from the customer's side.
- **Space 1 is the volume and space 7 is how most owners describe themselves.** The hours promise and Virat as the all-rounder serve both; the McKinsey lens's offer (M05) was built for them without naming them.
- **Space 4 is the cheap tier.** M04 said not to chase it. The one way to reach the operator who buys per kilogram is a cover that pays when the tyre bursts on stone, which is the haulier programme's cover extended to him at a registered camp, and nothing else.

### 3.2 Why occasion beats farm size

M04 cut owners by hours (heavy users, light users, small owners of second-hand tractors) because the only survey with detail gave hours by farm size. Two findings since say the occasion is the better cut [Interpretation]:

- **The same owner moves between spaces within a year,** and the tyre is judged by the harshest one: a medium farmer in the cotton belt tills in April, hires out in June and pulls cane to the mill from November. Farm size does not predict which occasion wears the tyre out; the loads do (10 to 12 tonnes on a 4-tonne trolley).
- **BCG's own Indian evidence on vehicle buyers says "Mobility needs supersede demographics"** (2024, 48% rural and small-town sample). The 2016 rural segmentation it published a decade earlier was demographic (the "late adopter": a man of 30 to 50 who owns a farm and prefers value-for-money brands) and does not say what he does with his tractor.

The baseline survey (04 section 9; M08 section 3.2) should therefore add: hours by occasion last year; loads carried and to where; cage-wheel use; the month and the trigger of the last tyre purchase; and the brand of the trolley's tyres. [Strategic hypothesis]

### 4.2 The design

The McKinsey lens designed an hours promise for all covered rear tyres (M05 section 3); the Bain lens added a first-season cut-and-burst cover and trolley wear for all of them, at Rs 220 more a tyre (B04 section 3.2). The BCG lens keeps the hours promise everywhere and puts the cover only where the failure is. [Strategic hypothesis; costs are planning assumptions]

| Space | Product that leads | The promise | Registered how | Reserve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Field work; 7 All-rounder | Virat; Virat 23 on hard soil; Krishak Gold with credit | A stated number of hours of tread life (Apollo sets it from its wear data; 2,000 used for arithmetic), settled at the dealer in seven days | QR scan at purchase with a photo of the hour meter | 2% of covered sales (M07) |
| 3 Road with crops; 4 Trolley for money | Virat; the steel-belted rear; Dhruv on the trolley | The hours promise plus first-season cuts and bursts and trolley wear covered, where the tyre was registered with the trolley's load and the pressure was checked at a camp | At a load-and-pressure camp (mill gate, custom-hiring centre, dealer) | 1.5% of the sales so registered (B04's rate), not of all sales |
| 5 Wet paddy | FX 222 | A stated number of seasons rather than hours, because puddling tractors seldom have working hour meters and the wear is seasonal | QR scan; the season recorded | 2% |
| 2 Hired out | As 1 or 3 by the work done | As above; the hirer-out is the heaviest registrant and the likeliest advocate | As above | As above |

What this does to the cost [Computed on planning assumptions]: if hauliers register about a third of covered rear tyres, the cover's reserve on sales Apollo would make anyway is about Rs 0.6 crore a year against the Bain lens's Rs 1.9 crore, and the per-tyre offer cost is Rs 1,475 on a haulier's tyre and Rs 1,255 on everyone else's. `C07` carries this through. The claim rate by space is the one number the test must produce; no maker publishes one. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

### 4.3 What the owner is told, in his own words

BCG's definition says spaces are "expressed in everyday language, the kind of language used by apostle customers". The guarantee card should carry the occasion in the owner's words, not the maker's: "Mera khet" (hours), "Trolley se mill" (load, cover), "Kaddu" (seasons). The product names stay (Virat, FX 222); the promise is named by the job. [Strategic hypothesis]

### 5.2 A Brand Advocacy Index for tractor tyres

No BAI exists for any tyre brand or for rural India (S2 section 6). The design below applies BCG's published method to the owner survey the McKinsey lens already designed (40 owners a tehsil, three waves). [Strategic hypothesis; BCG-published for the method]

| Element | Design |
|---|---|
| Who is asked | Owners in the test tehsils, whatever brand they last bought; the brand of the last rear pair is recorded |
| The behaviour questions, not intention | In the last year, did you recommend a tractor tyre brand to anyone? Which? Were you asked, or did you volunteer it? Did you warn anyone against a brand? Which? |
| Scoring | Positive less negative advocacy, spontaneous weighted above prompted, at BCG's three levels: Apollo's own buyers; buyers of other brands who know Apollo; the whole category |
| Baseline | January 2027 wave; the test arms read in July and December 2027 |
| What it adds to the NPS question the Bain lens added | NPS asks what the owner would do; BAI asks what he did. Both go on the survey; the gate measure stays sales |
| Cost | Four questions on an existing survey; nil |

### 5.3 Making the advocate visible

BAI measures advocacy; it does not create it. Three things do, each already in the programme in one lens or another [Interpretation]:

- **A reason to speak:** a promise that was kept in public. The seven-day settlement at the dealer (`C05`) is the event a neighbour hears about; a refused claim is the event he hears about today (10 of 39 complaints).
- **A record of who spoke:** registration at the counter, with a referral code on the card (B04 section 5.2's design, kept). Apollo cannot find its advocates until it knows its buyers; today the warranty number is issued by phone and held nowhere the farm business can use (S6 section 4 row 1).
- **A place to speak:** the mechanic's bench and the mill gate, not the screen. BCG's India finding that "the bulk of word of mouth in India still occurs in offline meeting places" (2014) is a decade old and still the best evidence available; the Deoria study says WhatsApp and calls to other farmers now sit beside it.
