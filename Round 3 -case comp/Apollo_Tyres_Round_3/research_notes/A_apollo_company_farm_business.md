Research notes for the Apollo Tyres x ACC Round 3 case. Compiled 6 November 2026. About 23 web searches used, plus direct downloads of primary PDFs (FY26 annual report, Q4 FY24/FY25/FY26 investor presentations, Q2/Q3/Q4 FY26 call transcripts).

**Reading rules:**
- Each finding carries a tag: [Verified fact] (primary source, or two independent credible sources), [Single source], [Interpretation], [Forecast] or [Unverified secondary claim].
- "Consolidated" means the whole group (India + Europe + others). "Standalone" means the Indian listed entity (India operations, including exports from India). The company does NOT report farm-tyre revenue, profit or volumes as a separate segment. The only farm numbers it publishes are percentage slices in a product-mix pie chart.

## 1. Business model and revenue

### Takeaway
The brief's figure is correct. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 28,471 crore, up 9.0%. EBITDA was Rs 4,143 crore (14.6% margin) and PAT was Rs 1,372 crore. India is about 63% of group revenue. Inside the Indian entity the business is mostly truck-bus tyres (55%) sold through the replacement market (63%). Farm is a small 6% slice.

## 2. Farm share of revenue and market share

### Takeaway
The brief's "8-10% of Indian revenue as of 2019" comes from Apollo's own press release of 31 May 2019. The company's latest disclosures put farm much lower: **5% of standalone revenue in FY24 and FY25, and 6% in FY26.** Apollo publishes no market-share figure for Indian farm tyres (OEM, replacement or radial).

### Cited Findings
- 31 May 2019 Apollo press release (Farm Zone launch): "farm segment contributes nearly 8-10% to Apollo Tyres Indian revenues". The same text is repeated by trade press, which is not independent. [Single source (company claim), dated] — [Apollo press release](https://corporate.apollotyres.com/press-and-media/news/corporate/apollo-farm-zone/); [Tires & Parts, 31 May 2019](https://tiresandparts.net/?p=27896)
- Latest company disclosure: Farm 5% (FY24), 5% (FY25) and 6% (FY26) of standalone revenue. [Verified fact; CRISIL corroborates the 6%] — [Q4 FY26 deck](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/APOLLOTYRE1_15052026115522_intimationpreseentation.pdf); [Q4 FY25 deck](https://corporate.apollotyres.com/content/dam/orbit/apollo-corporate/investors/announcements/2025-26/Investor%20Presentation-Q4%20FY25.pdf); [Q4 FY24 deck](https://corporate.apollotyres.com/content/dam/orbit/apollo-corporate/investors/ir-presentation/2023-24/Quarterly%20Investor%20Presentation-Q4FY24.pdf); [CRISIL Aug 2026](https://www.crisil.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/RatingList/RatingDocs/ApolloTyresLimited_August%2014_%202026_RR_399633.html)

The company has not explained it. [Interpretation]
- **Europe agri (not India):** on the Q4 FY26 call the CFO said the European "agri-contribution overall was about 12% of our revenues" (context: European manufacturing revenue of about EUR 670 million), with OE about half of that. This European agri/OHT business was made only at Enschede. [Unverified secondary claim; seen in a search summary only, original not opened] — [Business Standard broker-report PDF](https://bsmedia.business-standard.com/_media/bs/data/market-reports/equity-brokertips/2025-12/17669995660.82069300.pdf)

## 3. Farm portfolio

### Takeaway
On the company's India site the farm range is: Farm King (radial), FX 222 (puddling), Virat (20 lugs) and Virat 23 (23 lugs, hard soil), Krishak Gold, and Dhruv (trolley/trailer). Retail sites also list Krishak Premium (Drive / Steer / CR), Powerhaul, Agrex 85 and FX 212. All farm tyres carry a **7-year manufacturing-defect warranty**. Street prices for common rear sizes are about Rs 16,000-22,000 and front 6.00-16 about Rs 3,000-3,500. All "India's first" claims are company claims with no independent verification.

## 4. Manufacturing

### Takeaway
Apollo has 5 Indian plants (Perambra and Kalamassery in Kerala, Limda in Gujarat, Chennai in Tamil Nadu, Chinnapandur in Andhra Pradesh) plus Gyöngyöshalász in Hungary. Enschede (Netherlands) stopped production by 30 June 2026, and it was the group's only European agri/OHT plant. Capex is stepping up sharply: Rs 1,403 crore in FY26, about Rs 3,000-3,500 crore guided for FY27, and Rs 5,810 crore committed to Andhra Pradesh PCR/TBR capacity by FY29, with **none of it stated as farm capacity**.

R&D centres in Chennai and Enschede. ## 8. Constraints admitted by the company (rural reach / farm)

### Takeaway
The admitted constraints are: farm capacity shortage (Feb 2026), high overall utilisation (85-94%), cost inflation forcing price increases of about 15%+, A&P to normalise at about 2.5% of sales, no market-share data, the end of European agri manufacturing, and a stated threat of dealer consolidation and online channels. Nothing in the filings admits rural reach weakness directly, but the run of new rural fixes (rural head, ARD strategy, mobile vans) implies a gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Farm capacity:** "we are also seeing shortages in TBR and passenger car, and in farm" (Neeraj Kanwar, 5 Feb 2026). [Verified fact] — [Q3 FY26 transcript](https://corporate.apollotyres.com/content/dam/orbit/apollo-corporate/investors/shareholding-information/investors-call-transcript/2025-26/Q3FY2026%20Analyst%20Call%20Transcript.pdf)
- **Overall capacity:** about 90% in Q4 FY26; "Through April, we struggled in terms of keeping up with the demand" (Gaurav Kumar, 15 May 2026). [Verified fact] — [Q4 FY26 transcript](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/APOLLOTYRE1_18052026174646_transcriptintimation.pdf)
- **Costs and margins:**
  - Raw materials expected to rise "high teens" sequentially; 6-8% price increases announced for Q1 FY27, with "More price increases would further be needed". [Verified fact] — same source
  - CRISIL expects the operating margin to moderate to about 10-11% in FY27, RoCE below 10%, and net debt/EBITDA of 1.8-2.0x. [Forecast] — [CRISIL Aug 2026](https://www.crisil.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/RatingList/RatingDocs/ApolloTyresLimited_August%2014_%202026_RR_399633.html)
- **Budget discipline:** A&P to settle around "2.5% plus" of sales; Q1 FY27 A&P reportedly cut about 50%. [Verified fact / Unverified secondary claim, respectively]
- **Measurement:** "We don't have the official data ... or it comes with a significant lag" on market share (CFO, 15 May 2026). [Verified fact]
- **Pricing power:** intense competition (MRF, CEAT, JK) limits pricing power (CRISIL). A pending CCI penalty of Rs 425.5 crore has not been provided for. [Single source]
- **Distribution risk (AR SWOT threat):** "Structural changes in the distribution landscape, including consolidation of dealer networks and increasing influence of digital and online channels, may impact traditional distribution models and profitability." [Verified fact] — [FY26 AR](https://corporate.apollotyres.com/content/dam/orbit/apollo-corporate/investors/financial-reporting/annual-report/2025-26/AGM%20Notice%20and%20Annual%20Report%20FY2026.pdf)
- **Europe agri:** the Enschede closure ends the only European agri/OHT production, and some agri OE business that was loss-making may be lost (CFO). [Verified fact]

[Verified fact] — [FY26 AR](https://corporate.apollotyres.com/content/dam/orbit/apollo-corporate/investors/financial-reporting/annual-report/2025-26/AGM%20Notice%20and%20Annual%20Report%20FY2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Interpretation] The binding constraints on a farm push seem to be supply (capacity) and budget (A&P back to about 2.5%) more than brand, judging by management's own words.
- [Interpretation] The 2019 aim of a Farm Zone "in almost every state" has no reported follow-through. That suggests the farm-specific retail push was not scaled, though this is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- Any explicit company admission of weak rural or farm reach, or of farmer-recall levels: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
