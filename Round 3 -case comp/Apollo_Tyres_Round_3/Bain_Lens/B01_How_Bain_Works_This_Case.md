### 1.2 The method, as a case team would run it

Read together with accounts by former Bain consultants (labelled as such in Q1), the method has five habits:

1. **Write the answer first.** State the best defensible answer from what is known on day one, then build a workplan whose only purpose is to test it: which analysis, if it came out the wrong way, would break the answer. Revise when the evidence says so. [Bain-published for the term "Answer First" and "Answer First workplanning"; the mechanics are from former consultants and coaching sites, listed in Q1 section 3, and are not attributed to Bain]
2. **80/20 and the Monday-morning test.** Eighty per cent of the best answer, provided the client can act on it; every recommendation must be something the client can start on Monday morning. [Former-consultant accounts; not on any bain.com page]
3. **Size the full potential.** A Bain recruiting deck (2015) frames an engagement as the point of departure ("what is the problem?"), "identify and achieve full potential of business", the point of arrival ("what needs to be done?") and execution. The published tools are profit pools (1998), leadership economics (Repeatability, 2012), the core and its adjacencies (2001 to 2004) and hidden assets (2007). [Bain-published]
4. **Prove it with the customer and with a test.** Net Promoter System, customer episodes, Elements of Value; and for marketing or sponsorship spend, a test-and-control design: "Without a rigorous test-and-control design, sponsorship ROI is merely an opinion." [Bain-published]
5. **Deliver it.** RAPID decision rights, micro-battles (small cross-functional teams, three-to-four-week cycles, prototype, build the repeatable model, deploy), and Results Delivery to manage the risk of change. [Bain-published]

## 2. The Day-1 answer

Written on 7 November 2026 from the brief, the first-pass research set and the facts established in the McKinsey-lens files, before any new research. It is a hypothesis to be tested, not a conclusion. [Strategic hypothesis]

> **Apollo should treat farm replacement as the next step of the truck-replacement business it already wins in, not as a cricket-marketing problem. Pick the one segment Apollo can lead (the first replacement on tractors that left the factory on Apollo tyres), fix the episode that turns owners into detractors (the refused claim) with a tyre-life guarantee paid at the counter, run the dealer and the mechanic as sales plays borrowed from the truck business, and use the jersey for experiences that can be measured against a control. Fund it by zero-basing the farm trade money Apollo already spends, and run it as three micro-battles with a scale decision in August 2027.**

Three reasons, as written on day one:

1. **The profit is in replacement, and Apollo is a follower there.** Apollo is near 9% of tractor-tyre replacement against about 20% in car tyres and about 29% in truck radials. Followers rarely change their position by pushing harder; they lead a segment. [Computed, from M02; Bain-published for the follower finding]
2. **Owners and dealers tell Apollo what to fix.** The commonest grievance online is the refused warranty claim; loyalty sits with the shop, not the brand. That is a customer episode to redesign and a dealer to reward for incremental tyres, which is what Apollo's truck business already does. [Outside-in, from M04; Interpretation]
3. **The jersey is an asset nobody has used for farm.** Fourteen months on, there is no farm activation. Bain's own 2026 sponsorship work says the sponsor must name the commercial outcome first and measure it against a control; the one measured case is hospitality for a target segment, not impressions. [Verified fact; Bain-published]

What the client would do on Monday morning: pull the fitment records, name the owner of the farm micro-battles, and freeze the farm trade budget pending a zero-based review.

## 3. Six hypotheses the research sets out to break

| # | Hypothesis | What would break it | Tested in |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Farm replacement is a one-step adjacency from Apollo's truck-radial replacement core, sharing dealers in mandi towns; the repeatable formula is the truck replacement playbook, not a new farm engine | Farm and truck tyres are sold through different dealers; Apollo's truck replacement win came from product or price, not a trade programme that farm can copy | `B03`, `B05` |
| H2 | Apollo is a follower (relative market share perhaps 0.2 to 0.3 against MRF) and should pick a segment to lead: first replacement on Apollo-fitted tractors, and the heavy-user or hard-soil segment | Shares are closer than thought (Apollo near the leader), or Apollo's fitment share is too small for first replacement to be a segment worth leading | `B02` |
| H3 | The largest detractor driver is the refused claim and uneven wear; fixing that episode is the same move as the McKinsey lens, and Bain adds earned growth: promoters refer owners | Complaints are dominated by price or availability, not claims; or owners do not refer each other in this category | `B04` |
| H4 | The jersey is a hidden asset best used for experiences with a matched control (dealers, tractor makers, opinion-leader farmers), not for village tournaments; any cricket spend must name its commercial outcome first | Rural cricket reach among tractor owners is high and cheap enough that impressions beat experiences; or the rights do not allow hospitality at the scale needed | `B06` |
| H5 | Part or all of the Rs 11 crore can be found by zero-basing existing farm trade and promotion spend, so the incremental ask is smaller | Apollo's farm trade spend is already thin, or is contractual with dealers and cannot be redirected | `B07` |
| H6 | Three micro-battles in a few districts with three-to-four-week cycles and an August 2027 scale decision beat a four-arm statistical test | The effect is too small to read without a controlled design; or management will not scale without statistical evidence | `B08` |

## 5. How this differs from the McKinsey-lens method

Filled in after the research; see also `B10_Bain_vs_McKinsey.md`.

| | McKinsey lens (M01) | Bain lens (this file) |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | One governing question and an issue tree with six branches | One written answer and a workplan built only to break it |
| First number sought | The size of the prize (M02) | Where the profit sits and Apollo's share relative to the leader (B02) |
| View of the business | A share-gain programme with layers and gates | A core, an adjacency one step away, and hidden assets to exploit |
| Customer tool | Decision journey; coded comments | Net Promoter proxies, the episode that makes detractors, Elements of Value |
| Sponsorship tool | McKinsey's five measures (2014) | Bain's Zero-Click playbook (2026): outcome first, authenticity, experiences, control group |
| Economics | Break-even by layer; three cases | Full potential, money map, zero-based funding, value-creation plan |
| Delivery | Four-arm test with gates; first 100 days | Micro-battles, RAPID, Results Delivery risk dialogue; NPS cadence |
| Test of a recommendation | What management would need to believe | The Monday-morning test: can Apollo start it next week? |
