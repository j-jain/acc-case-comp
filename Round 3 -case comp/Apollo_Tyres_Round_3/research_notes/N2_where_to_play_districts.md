## (d) WHAT THE DATA CAN AND CANNOT SUPPORT (for anyone building a district ranking)
1. No single clean, current, national district table of tractors in use exists in the open sources searched. What exists is patchy by state and by measure: Haryana (official stock, 2022-23, all 22 districts, ties to state total); Punjab (official density only, 2024, all 23 districts); older plan-table stocks (c. 2008-09) for about 11 districts; and annual or part-year registrations (FY25, FY26 YTD) for only the top 20 or so districts in the country, which come from one trade site.
2. The three measures must stay apart: stock in use (Haryana abstract), cumulative registrations (state totals as at 31 Mar 2020) and registrations in one period (Tractor Gyan). Haryana's registered stock (4.53 lakh, 2020) is 1.5 times its in-use stock (3.02 lakh, 2019-20) [Computed], so cumulative registrations overstate tractors in use.
3. Annual registrations measure replacement and new demand where tractors are sold and RTO-registered, not where they work. Some rows are RTO offices rather than districts (Malegaon and Srirampur appear beside Nashik and Ahmednagar), so an RTO row may overlap the district that hosts it and buyers may register away from where they farm [Interpretation]. Registrations cover top-20 districts only in the sources found, so UP (only Badaun and Aligarh) and Punjab and Haryana (none) cannot be ranked on them.
4. A ranking from this data would be biased to Gujarat, Rajasthan, Maharashtra and MP (their districts dominate the top-20 lists) and to a trade site's categories; Punjab and Haryana districts with high densities do not appear in the registration lists, probably because their annual volume per district is lower [Interpretation, not shown by data].
5. Households: NFHS-5 gives state-level tractor-owning household shares; I did not find district-level shares. Rural household counts use Census 2011 (15 years old), so the computed owning-household counts are low.
6. Media exposure is for men aged 15-49 at state level, not for tractor owners, and not for rural areas only; farmer-specific media habits are Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
7. Field-test design: tehsil counts and borders came from Wikipedia pages that cite the official directory, with conflicting counts for 9 of 17 districts and recent reorganisations (Anupgarh, Malerkotla). Verify on the Local Government Directory before using tehsils as units. Bordering lists are not distances or a map.
8. Density caveat: Punjab's official density (81 per 1,000 ha) implies about 3.3 lakh tractors [Computed], far below registrations (4.69 lakh in 2020; 6.41 lakh in 2025 press), so use it as a relative index.
9. Not found: district stock for Punjab (other than three plan-table districts), UP (other than Aligarh), Rajasthan, MP, Gujarat, Maharashtra after 2014; official in-use tractor statements after 2018 for UP, MP, Gujarat and Maharashtra (only registered stock at 31 Mar 2020: UP 15,22,750; MP 13,30,745; Maharashtra 8,25,189; Gujarat 8,20,111), Rajasthan 12,23,825 (2020), and an unverified 11,80,769 (March 2022); Punjab 4,68,532 (2020) and "nearly 6.41 lakh" (press, Sep 2025); Haryana 3,30,769 in use (2022-23).
