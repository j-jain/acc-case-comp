# S5. The rural tyre channel: structure, economics, speed and the haulier (verified 7 Nov 2026)
Round-7 research note for the BCG-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case. Feeds `..\BCG_Lens\C05` and `C03`.

**Scope.** The economics and speed of the channel from maker to tractor owner, and the haulier segment that wears tyres fastest. Builds on `06_Rural_Distribution_and_Geography.md`, `Q4` sections 1 and 2 and `B05` section 2 (the 2012 Competition Commission margins, deposits and credit norms), which are cited, not repeated. 24 searches and about 40 page reads on 7 November 2026; the sugarcane transport study and Apollo's FY26 annual report were downloaded and read in full text.

## 1. Channel structure

What the record supports: Indian tyre makers sell to dealers principal-to-principal, mostly without a distributor tier and without written dealer agreements; most dealers are multi-brand; exclusive-outlet formats exist for cars and two-wheelers and are now being pushed into small towns; no public source gives a farm-tyre share for tractor dealerships against tyre dealers.

## 9. Cautions and discrepancies

1. **Counts measure different things.** Apollo's 6,527 are invoiced dealers and distributors; CEAT's 50,000 (2022) are every outlet reached, kirana and puncture shops included; JK's 6,000 are "channel partners"; the 170-against-3,690 farm comparison (Q4) is a portal listing. None should be divided by another.
2. **Margins are from 2012 and for two makers.** JK's 10.2 to 16.2% and CEAT's 5 to 7% discounts are the makers' own submissions in a case the Commission dismissed; CEAT said it did not know its dealers' margins. The 12 to 18% in row 2.3 is a lender's illustration. Nothing is farm-specific.
3. **Deposit and investment figures come from franchise portals** that copy each other and vary two-fold for the same brand. Only the CRISIL-counted Rs 1,315 crore (B05) is verified, and that is a total.
4. **The delivery promises are for tractors and cars, not tyres.** Mahindra's 48 hours covers service and parts; CEAT's "within minutes" and TVS Eurogrip's "less than 24 hours" are car and two-wheeler formats; MRF's 7 days is an intimation of inspection, not a settlement. No farm-tyre clock exists.
5. **The sugarcane study is 2018-19 data in eight states**; Maharashtra, Karnataka and Gujarat are outside its sample; trolley loads are what mills allowed at the gate and may have changed. Row 5.6 is an illustration, not a measurement.
6. **Custom-hiring centre counts disagree** (44,573 to 2023-24; "more than 41,900" in a 2025 reply seen only in summary; 27,554 on an unsourced site), and the annexure bundles hubs and machinery banks. State rows were read from the annexure image because text extraction misaligned the columns.
7. **Tractor-maker dealer counts** for four of six brands rest on one insurer's listicle, and two of its figures (Escorts 800 touchpoints against 185 dealers) do not reconcile; Mahindra's and TAFE's are from their own pages.
8. **Attrition figures** are uncited recruiter blogs and one staffing report with no tier 3 to 4 number. Use directionally.
9. **The 2018 cartel order** (Rs 425.53 crore on Apollo; dated 31 August 2018, released February 2022, remitted for penalty review in December 2022) began as a dealer-federation complaint about pricing. A channel programme that sets dealer prices, ties tubes to tyres or exchanges scheme terms with rivals carries that history; see B05 section 5.
10. **Blocked or rate-limited pages:** two tirereview items, the 2008 and 2012 business-daily items, the IGI sugar-logistics paper, the MRF marketing-mix page, the IDBI loan page, the Lok Sabha PDF, the Lucknow tractor-use study. Where a headline is cited the label says so.
