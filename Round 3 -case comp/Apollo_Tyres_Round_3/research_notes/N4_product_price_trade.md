- Per rear pair multiply by 2 (Krishak Gold at 2,000 h: Rs 16.00 per tractor hour for both rear tyres).
- Break-even: for Shakti Super (17,670) to match Krishak Gold (16,000) at 2,000 h, it needs 17,670 / 8.00 = 2,209 h (+10.4%, or +209 h). Vajra Super (17,440): 2,180 h (+9.0%).
- Radial against bias, taking Farm King 340/85 R28 (22,488, the radial counterpart the brief names for 13.6-28) against Krishak Gold 13.6-28 (16,000): break-even life multiple = 22,488 / 16,000 = 1.41. Apollo claims 1.5 times the life of a bias tyre [S3], which is a company claim with no stated test. At a bias life of 2,000 h and a 1.5 multiple (3,000 h), Farm King = 22,488 / 3,000 = Rs 7.50 per hour vs bias Rs 8.00 per hour (-6%). At a multiple of 1.41 the two are equal; below it bias is cheaper per hour on tyre cost alone.
- The 12.4 R28 radial (18,973) cannot be compared with a 13.6-28 bias tyre (different section width). The price of a 12.4-28 bias tyre was not collected.
- Fuel is the other half of the cost per hour. Litres per hour and fuel price were not sourced for Indian tractors, so no fuel cost per hour is computed. The formula for the writer is: fuel saving per hour = litres per hour x price per litre x percentage saving (3.08% in the Turkish radial study, 1-3% for ballast in the IIT Kharagpur study, see Section 2).

### Inferences
- With ply 12 bias and a similar load index across brands, the Rs 1,000-1,700 price gaps over Krishak Gold (6-10%) are not backed by any published difference in tread depth or weight. Whether they reflect real differences in rubber and wear cannot be established from public data.
- The radial needs about 41% more life than a bias tyre to break even on tyre cost, so a measured life ratio, not the company's 1.5, is the number that matters.

### Gaps
- NSD (mm), weight, load for every Indian tyre except the BKT items above; Apollo, MRF, Goodyear, CEAT and JK data books were not reached. tractorkarvan.com spec pages returned connection resets.
- Prices for Virat, Virat 23, MRF Shakti Life, JK and BKT in India.
- Whether the 1439 mm outer diameter on the Farm King listing belongs to all sizes (a 420/85 R28 at 1439 mm is plausible; TractorJunction lists 12.4 x 28 at 1284 mm).

## 2. Field evidence on tyre performance in India

### Takeaway
No Indian field trial of radial against bias tractor tyres, and no measured wear (mm per 100 h) or tyre life in hours on Indian farms, was found. The Indian evidence that exists is on ballast and slip. Foreign radial studies show small gains: about 3% fuel and 3.4% efficiency (Turkey, 2005) up to 7% (North Dakota, 1974).

### Inferences
- Taken together, radial fuel gains in the foreign studies range 3-7% at the tractor level, with slip gains of 6.7% (Turkey) to 25% (North Dakota: (13 - 9.75) / 13 = 25%) [Computed]. That spread is wide and none is Indian.
- The Indian papers support the mechanism the case may need (ballast and pressure change slip and wear) but give no hours-of-life number.

### Gaps
- Radial vs bias trials from ICAR-CIAE, PAU, TNAU, in Agricultural Mechanization in Asia, Africa and Latin America or the Journal of Agricultural Engineering were not found by the searches used; the journal sites and KrishiKosh were not searched directly.
- Measured wear rate (mm per 100 h) and tyre life in hours on Indian farms: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Effect of inflation pressure on wear in Indian conditions: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

## 3. Guarantees

### Takeaway
One hours-based tread-wear guarantee on farm tyres exists: Trelleborg's 5,000-hour guarantee (announced 25 Feb 2026), which pays a pro-rata credit for unused hours towards a new tyre. Other farm-tyre promises are time-based defect warranties, with field-hazard cover only at Continental. In India, "unconditional" cover exists on car and two-wheeler tyres but not on farm tyres, and no claim rate has been published for any of them.

- [Computed] Trelleborg credit = (5,000 - hours achieved) / 5,000 x purchase price. A tyre that fails at 0 h returns 100% of price; at 4,000 h, 20%; at 2,500 h, 50%. At Rs 16,000 purchase price (illustrative, using the Krishak Gold portal price) a failure at 2,000 h would carry a credit of 60% = Rs 9,600; at 3,500 h, 30% = Rs 4,800. The credit is only usable on a new tyre of the same model, so the cash cost to the maker is below the face value by its manufacturing margin.

### Inferences
- The only farm-tyre precedent that is expressed in hours is a pro-rata credit, not a refund, and it needs registration and hour-meter proof. That design limits the maker's exposure to the unused fraction of life.
- Indian unconditional warranties found are capped by wear (50%) and time (2 years) and sit on car and two-wheeler tyres.

### Gaps
- Claim rates, uptake or cost of any guarantee: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Terms of the Bridgestone India unconditional warranty, Apollo Amazer page, Zhongce and Bridgestone ag pages were taken from snippets and need a re-read.

**Interest cost of a Rs 16,000 tyre on an amortising loan [Computed].** Interest = price x annual rate x (n + 1) / 24 for n monthly instalments.
- 6 months at 16%: 16,000 x 0.16 x 7/24 = Rs 747; at 30%: 16,000 x 0.30 x 7/24 = Rs 1,400.
- 12 months at 16%: 16,000 x 0.16 x 13/24 = Rs 1,387; at 30%: Rs 2,600.
- Poonawalla fee of up to 5% = Rs 800 on Rs 16,000; with 16% for 12 months the all-in is about 1,387 + 800 = Rs 2,187 (13.7% of the tyre price).
- A "no-cost" 6-month offer at 16% would therefore cost the seller about Rs 747 per Rs 16,000 tyre (4.7%), if the seller absorbed the interest; the sources do not say who pays.
- Poonawalla's Rs 50,000 minimum exceeds a full set at portal prices (2 x 16,000 + 2 x 3,000 = Rs 38,000) and a rear pair (Rs 32,000), so that product would not fit a single-tyre purchase.

### Inferences
- A retread priced Rs 6,000-8,500 is a substitute at roughly 40-55% of new price; the sources do not show how many retread cycles a tractor casing takes or what life a retread delivers.
- The 5-point GST cut on retreads lowers a Rs 5,000 retread bill by Rs 650 (11%) [Computed: 650 / 5,900].
