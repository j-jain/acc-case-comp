## 1. The answer

> **Apollo's tractor tyres are fitted at the factory and lost at the first replacement. Apollo should win that replacement with a guaranteed tyre life and a paid dealer and mechanic, and use the Team India jersey as its mark of trust at the counter. It costs about Rs 11 crore to March 2028, pays for itself at one point of market share, and can be proved before the sponsorship comes up for renewal.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Situation** | Apollo has been Team India's lead sponsor since September 2025. Farm tyres are 6% of its Indian revenue, about Rs 1,200 crore. [Verified fact] |
| **Complication** | The jersey has not visibly moved tractor-tyre demand: Apollo's share of tractor-tyre searches went from 14.0% to 14.8% in fourteen months, and MRF's stayed at 47%. Apollo holds about 9% of tractor-tyre replacement against about 20% in car tyres. Cricket already belongs to MRF, CEAT and BKT. Farm tyres are in short supply. [Outside-in; Computed; Verified fact] |
| **Resolution** | Stop asking cricket to sell the tyre. Sell tyre life with a guarantee, pay the people who advise the owner, and use the jersey for the two things only the sponsor has: standing at the counter and rewards for the trade. [Strategic hypothesis] |

## 2. Three reasons

### 2.1 The prize is real, and Apollo is under-weight in it

- The domestic tractor-tyre market is 7.7 million tyres a year, half of it replacement. [Fact - single source: CRISIL]
- The replacement half is worth about Rs 3,300 crore at manufacturers' prices (Rs 2,500 to 4,000 crore). [Computed]
- About a quarter of Apollo's farm sales are replacement, against half for the industry. That puts Apollo near 9%. [Computed on one broker note]
- One point of share is worth about Rs 33 crore of revenue. Moving from 9% to 15% is worth about Rs 200 crore a year. [Computed]
- Detail: `M02_Value_at_Stake.md`.

### 2.2 Owners buy tyre life on a trusted person's word, and that can be offered everywhere

- Owners judge tyres by how long they last, and they doubt makers' claims. Refused warranty claims are the commonest complaint online. [Outside-in; not a survey]
- Four in ten owners make about two-thirds of purchases. The dealer and the mechanic decide most of the rest. [Computed on one survey; Interpretation]
- **The offer:** a stated number of hours, guaranteed, with the claim paid at the dealer in seven days, at today's price. One tyre maker launched such a guarantee abroad in 2026; none exists in India. [Verified fact]
- **The trade:** Rs 300 to the mechanic and Rs 400 to the dealer for each extra rear tyre, with harvest-timed credit for the buyer. The whole offer costs Rs 1,255 a rear tyre and leaves about Rs 3,000. [Planning assumption; Computed]
- **The jersey:** "Team India's sponsor" on the dealer's board, the guarantee card and the tyre; match tickets and team shirts as rewards for dealers and mechanics. Apollo has already given such rewards to buyers of car and motorcycle tyres. [Fact - single source]
- **First customers:** owners of tractors that left the factory on Apollo tyres.
- Detail: `M04_Customer.md`, `M05_How_to_Win.md`, `M06_Sponsorship.md`.

### 2.3 It pays at one point of share, and a test sizes the rest

- The national offer costs about Rs 7 crore a year and breaks even at one point of share. Most of its cost arises only when a tyre is sold. [Computed on planning assumptions]
- The prize is spread out: the twenty largest districts take 11.5% of tractor registrations. So the offer is national, and a field team goes only to dense tehsils. [Fact - single source]
- A field team must add about five points of local share to pay, and cricket events three more. Those are demanding, so they are tested, not assumed. [Computed]
- A four-arm test in 30 districts starts on 21 January 2027, with India's home Tests against Australia. Gates in August 2027 and January 2028 decide what is funded. [Planning assumption]
- Detail: `M03_Where_to_Play.md`, `M07_Economics.md`, `M08_Delivery.md`.

## 3. The numbers

| | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| What passes its gate | The offer only | The offer and field teams | The offer, field teams and cricket |
| Share of replacement at March 2029 (today about 9%) | About 10.5% | About 12.5% | About 14.5% |
| Extra revenue in FY29 | Rs 33 crore | Rs 82 crore | Rs 132 crore |
| Spending to March 2028 | Rs 11 crore | Rs 11 crore | Rs 11 crore |
| Spending in FY29 | Rs 7.7 crore | Rs 12.2 crore | Rs 15.1 crore |
| Result at March 2028, cumulative | -Rs 9.5 crore | -Rs 7.3 crore | -Rs 3.6 crore |
| Result at March 2029, cumulative | -Rs 9.8 crore | -Rs 1.1 crore | +Rs 10.8 crore |

[Computed on planning assumptions; the share gains are assumptions, not forecasts.]

Read it plainly:

- **It does not pay back by March 2028.** It is a two-year investment that turns positive in FY29 in the base case.
- **The most that can be lost is about Rs 10 crore**, because the gates stop the layers that do not pay.
- **This is a Rs 100 to 200 crore revenue opportunity.** It does not justify the jersey; car and truck tyres must. Farm borrows the jersey's standing and should pay only to use it.

## 6. What we do not know

The whole case turns on one figure: that about a quarter of Apollo's farm sales are replacement. It comes from one broker's note of a meeting with management. Apollo can confirm it from its own books in a week. If Apollo's replacement share is much higher than 9%, the prize is smaller and the programme should be cut to the offer alone.

Also not known: tyre life measured in Indian conditions, real dealer margins, guarantee claim rates, the reach of cricket among tractor owners, and the rights in the contract beyond the logo. For each: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation. The full list, with what would settle each, is in M10 section 5.
