# C10. The three lenses side by side: same, different, and what to present

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the BCG-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** A plain comparison of the three re-analyses of the same case: `..\McKinsey_Lens\M00` to `M10` (6 November 2026), `..\Bain_Lens\B00` to `B11` (7 November 2026) and `C00` to `C09` (7 November 2026). All three are our own work using frameworks the three firms have published; none of the firms had any part in any of them. It is not BCG's work, not Bain's and not McKinsey's. The BCG lens was written from a fresh Day-1 answer (C01 section 2) and reconciled here only at the end, so where the three agree, the agreement is a finding and not an echo. `..\Bain_Lens\B10_Bain_vs_McKinsey.md` compared the first two; this file does not repeat it beyond what the three-way view needs.

**Purpose.** To answer the user's question: is the BCG answer the same as the other two, or different, and what should the team present?

## 1. Where the three lenses agree

| Element | All three say | Why it is robust |
|---|---|---|
| The diagnosis | Apollo's tractor tyres are fitted at the factory and lost at the first replacement; about 9% of replacement against about 20% in car tyres; the jersey has not visibly moved farm demand | Three methods (an issue tree sized from the market; a profit pool and relative share; a portfolio matrix and a TSR decomposition) reached it from different starts |
| The prize | About Rs 3,300 crore of replacement; a point worth about Rs 33 crore of revenue; 15% by FY30 as the aspiration | The BCG lens reuses M02's arithmetic and adds the matrix's growth axis (units +6.9% a year) |
| The offer's core | A guaranteed number of hours, registered at purchase and settled at the dealer; no price cut; the dealer and the mechanic paid per extra tyre; harvest credit; the owners of Apollo-fitted tractors first | All three find the refused claim as the grievance and the adviser as the decider |
| The jersey's role | A trust mark at the counter and rewards for the trade, not the engine; no farm film; no player endorsement; no IPL deal; impressions cannot be priced for a rural audience | McKinsey's five measures, Bain's three steps and BCG's "relationship depth" all fail the impression buy |
| The segments | The first replacement on Apollo-fitted tractors, and the haulier | Found through the decision journey, through leadership economics and the warranty record, and through the Rule of Three and Four's "concentrate" |
| Geography | The prize is spread out; the offer is national; field money only in dense tehsils; thirty first-wave districts in three belts | Unchanged; the BCG lens adds BCG's own tehsil-level targeting principle |
| The test | A controlled, paired-tehsil design in thirty districts with gates in August 2027 and January 2028; no claim of pay-back by March 2028 | Bain's playbook and BCG's measurement pages both insist on test and control |
| Supply | From allocation and off-take, not a new line | The BCG lens adds the experience curve's "experience can be sourced externally" |
| The data request | The same five data sets in the first two weeks, plus one from each later lens (the stocking count; the farm share of the trade pool and the replacement billing growth) | Unchanged |
| Farm cannot justify the jersey | The jersey must be justified by car and truck tyres; farm borrows its standing | All three; the BCG lens puts the TSR arithmetic to it |

In short: the three lenses give the same answer to the brief's question. [Interpretation]

## 2. Where the BCG lens differs, and why

| Element | McKinsey lens | Bain lens | BCG lens | Why the BCG lens differs | Which is better supported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The first question** | How big is the prize? | Where does the profit sit, and is farm an adjacency of the core? | Should Apollo be in farm at all, and in which sector can it lead? | The growth share matrix and the Rule of Three and Four start from the portfolio | The BCG question is the one a board would ask first; its answer (invest, with a cash cap, in two sectors) is the same as the others' programme, so it adds a reason, not a change |
| **The frame** | A share-gain programme with layers and gates | An adjacency of the core with hidden assets | A question mark funded as an experiment with a cash cap, in a classical environment, with the jersey as an adaptive part | The strategy palette says match the approach to the environment | All three; the BCG frame is the only one that names why the counter and the jersey need different processes |
| **Segmentation** | Heavy users by hours and farm size | The episode that makes detractors; hauliers | Seven demand spaces by occasion; the purchase moment as a second axis | Demand-centric growth; BCG's Indian vehicle finding that needs beat demographics | The BCG cut explains the product lines the makers already sell and the failures the record shows; it costs nothing and narrows the cover |
| **The promise** | Hours; cuts excluded | Hours plus a cut-and-burst cover and trolley wear, for all covered tyres (Rs 1,475) | Hours for all; the cover only in the haulage spaces, registered with the load (Rs 1,305 and Rs 1,525) | The space, not the tyre, carries the risk | The BCG scoping answers the Bain evidence at a third of the Bain cost (Rs 0.6 crore against Rs 1.9 crore a year on existing sales); the claim rate by space is the test's to find |
| **Speed** | Settlement in seven days as a term | Settlement in seven days; dealer authority | Two measured clocks (48 hours to stock, 7 days to settle) framed as the advantage, against a market with no clock and a tractor-maker benchmark of 48 hours | Time-based competition | New evidence (the tractor makers' promises; the absence of any farm-tyre clock); the stock promise is working capital, not expense |
| **The jersey** | A slim cricket layer tested as an arm | Experiences with controls | The counter as a registration point (half of buyers in the test, three in five at scale); the moments as experiments (one hospitality window in the core) | BCG's known-fans yardstick, transferred | The registered base is the one farm asset that survives the renewal decision either way; BCG's sport work gives sponsors no method, so the design is ours |
| **Where the sectors are found** | Tractor makers' records; health camps | Mahindra dealers; camps; FPOs and PACS | The mill gate and its purchase centres; the custom-hiring centres; the tractor dealer's service visit | The cane study and the annexure; the tractor makers' service promises | New evidence; the mill gate is the one place hundreds of hauliers stand still |
| **Economics** | Break-even 1.0 point; Rs 7.4 crore a point; Rs 11 crore | Break-even 1.5 points; Rs 7.0 crore a point; Rs 17 crore | Break-even 1.3 points; Rs 7.2 crore a point; Rs 13.7 crore; the 25% contribution case shown | The scoped cover; the clocks, registration, camps and office added | The BCG lens is honest about sitting between the two and about raw materials having moved since M02's 30% |
| **How the money is argued** | New budget in tranches | A reallocation from advertising; no new money | ROCE and option value; a TSR refusal made before a judge makes it; funding from the trade pool (Rs 632 crore of accruals), about Rs 3 crore new | BCG's TSR framing and the auditor's accrual figure | The BCG argument is the one a finance judge will accept; the trade pool is a better source than advertising because the trade spend belongs there |
| **Delivery** | Six initiatives; a steering group; 100 days | Micro-battles; RAPID; a risk dialogue; NPS | The same, scored on DICE (15 to 17) and fixed (10); Smart Simplicity's integrator; a two-person office; the "20 to 40%" prior stated | DICE; Smart Simplicity; BCG's transformation research | The BCG additions cost nothing but commitment; they name the one factor (C1) the other lenses left to chance |
| **Measurement** | Billing against control; the owner survey | Plus NPS, earned growth, the scorecard locked with finance | Plus the Brand Advocacy Index questions, the matched-market check before the draw, the prior, and the 12-to-16-month caution | BCG's four legs and guardrails | Small, cheap and credible to finance; none changes the gates |
| **Method** | One question; an issue tree; sixteen sub-hypotheses | One answer; six hypotheses; three changed | One answer; six hypotheses; all six supported with corrections (section 3); the approach matched to the environment | The environment test | Each route produced the same direction; the BCG route exposed four things the others did not ask (section 6 of C01) |

## 3. What happened to the six Day-1 hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis (C01 section 3) | Outcome | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Farm is a question mark Apollo is already harvesting | **Supported on management's behaviour, corrected on growth:** Indian farm replacement grows at or above the industry's rate (6.9% a year), so it is a question mark and not a pet, and the harvest is by omission, not decision | C02 |
| H2 | Farm replacement is a specialisation business, not a volume one | **Supported through the Rule of Three and Four and the cost position; the "advantage matrix" withdrawn,** because no BCG domain carries it | C02 |
| H3 | Demand spaces explain purchase better than demographics | **Supported, with a second axis:** the purchase moment (planned against the day of failure), which the McKinsey lens had found | C04 |
| H4 | The jersey's main farm use is a known-fan database | **Supported as a design, corrected on its source:** BCG's sport work addresses rights holders and gives sponsors no method; the registered base is our transfer of BCG's yardstick | C06 |
| H5 | A point of farm share is too small to move TSR; justify on option value | **Supported:** 0.17 points a year on the growth leg; the case is ROCE and option value, and the funding is the trade pool | C07 |
| H6 | The programme as designed sits in DICE's "worry" zone | **Supported in direction, corrected in degree:** 15 to 17 on BCG's 2021 bands ("touch and go"), 10 after three changes; the Bain lens's cycles already take two points off | C08 |

All six survived with corrections; none was reversed. The corrections are what the research was for. [Interpretation]

### 4.3 What option D leaves out of the BCG lens, and why

- The seven-space map as a slide of its own: the occasions go onto the customer slide (M09 slide 5) as the cut, with the promise per space on the guarantee slide.
- The environment test and the de-averaged belt map: one line on the test slide ("the counter is planned; the moments are experiments") carries the point.
- The six Indian makers' TSR table and the archetype mapping: backup, because BCG's study names no company and the mapping is ours.
- Smart Simplicity's six rules: inside the delivery slide's notes; the integrator's scorecard is in the ARD's role, not on a slide.

## 6. Limits of the comparison

- All three lenses are our own work from public sources, with no Apollo data, no interviews and no contract. A consulting team of any of the three firms would have all three in its first two weeks.
- The BCG lens uses BCG's published frameworks, its one tyre study and its Indian consumer work; BCG has published nothing on Indian farm tyres, tractor owners, tyre dealers or cricket, so no "BCG view of this industry" exists to borrow, and every application is ours. The Bain lens had the same limit; the McKinsey lens had one farmer survey.
- The economics of all three lenses rest on the same planning assumptions, above all the 25% replacement figure from one broker note and the share gains in the three cases, which are assumptions and not forecasts. The BCG lens adds two of its own: the one-third haulier share and the 2021 net debt in the TSR decomposition.
- The DICE scores, the demand spaces, the archetype mapping and the environment test are interpretations on BCG's published scales, not BCG's judgements of Apollo.
- The outside-in signals (dealer counts, complaint coding, search share, portal prices, store download bands) are signals, not measurements, and are labelled so in every file.
