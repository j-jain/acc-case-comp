# M07. Economics

Prepared 6 November 2026. Part of the McKinsey-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis. It rebuilds the business case in `..\13_Budget_and_Commercial_Model.md` on the market figures in `M02_Value_at_Stake.md`, and sets it out the way a strategy consulting team would: what each layer costs, what it has to deliver, and what management would need to believe. It is not McKinsey's work.

**Purpose.** To show whether the programme pays, by layer and over time, and what budget to release at each decision.

**How to read this file.** Almost every figure here is [Computed] on [Planning assumption] inputs. The inputs are listed in section 2 with their sources. Where a figure comes from a source it is labelled. Nothing here is a forecast of what will happen; it is arithmetic on stated assumptions, to be replaced by Apollo's own numbers.

---

## 1. The answer in seven lines

1. **One point of replacement share is worth about Rs 33 crore of revenue and, after the cost of the offer, about Rs 7.4 crore of contribution a year.**
2. **The national offer pays for itself at one point of share.** It costs about Rs 7 crore a year, and most of its cost arises only when a tyre is sold.
3. **A field team pays only in dense tehsils and only if it adds about five points there.** A point of share in a tehsil of 5,000 tractors is worth about Rs 44,000 a year.
4. **A cricket layer has to add about three points on top.** The first analysis's cricket layer could not have paid on farm tyres; a slimmer one might.
5. **So most of the gain must come from the national offer.** In the base case the offer adds about 1.9 points and field tehsils add 0.6.
6. **The programme does not pay back by March 2028 in any case.** Spending to that date is about Rs 11 crore. The base case earns Rs 6 crore in FY29 and recovers the whole outlay early in FY30.
7. **This is a Rs 100 to 200 crore revenue opportunity.** It does not justify the jersey; car and truck tyres have to do that. It is worth doing because it is cheap, most of its cost is variable, and the gates cap the loss at about Rs 10 crore.

## 5. What you would need to believe

For the base case in FY29: an average gain of 2.5 points of national replacement share. That is Rs 82 crore of revenue and about 97,000 tyres, of which about 24,000 rear pairs.

| # | Belief | In plain terms | Evidence today | How it is settled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The replacement market is about Rs 3,300 crore and Apollo has about 9% of it | – | One CRISIL chart; one broker note | Apollo's own data, in a week |
| 2 | The offer adds about 1.9 points where there is no field team | About 18,500 more rear pairs a year. If 2,000 of Apollo's 6,500 dealerships stock farm tyres, nine or ten each: under one a month | An hours guarantee exists abroad; refused claims are the main grievance online; no Indian result | The test's offer-only arm |
| 3 | In 200 dense tehsils the field engine adds five points more | One more rear pair a fortnight in each tehsil; 0.6 points nationally | None. Analogues from cars and motorcycles moved share with field staff but give no tyre benchmark | The test's engine arm |
| 4 | Guarantee claims stay within 2% of covered sales | One tyre in twenty claims a quarter of its price | No maker publishes a claim rate | Claims in the test, by dealer |
| 5 | About 97,000 more tyres can be supplied | About 3,600 tonnes: 0.5% of Apollo's production | Farm tyres are short; Apollo is not adding farm capacity | Supply decision (`M08_Delivery.md`) |
| 6 | Rivals do not erase the gain within the year | – | No case of retaliation in farm tyres was found | Mystery visits; rivals' schemes |
| 7 | Buyers return | Registered owners buy Apollo again | J.D. Power: repurchase falls to 18% after uneven wear | Repurchase among registered owners, from FY29 |

The number of dealerships stocking farm tyres is not public. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

[Computed]. The share gains are assumptions, not forecasts: no analogue gives a benchmark for a tyre maker (`..\research_notes\N5_analogues_sponsorship_response.md`).

What the table says:

- **No case pays back by March 2028.** The first analysis presented a full year's result for a programme that would have run four to seven months. The honest statement is a run-rate at March 2028 and a result in FY29.
- **The worst case costs about Rs 10 crore.** The gates stop the field layers if they do not pay.
- **The base case recovers its outlay early in FY30** and then earns Rs 12 to 15 crore a year at about 3.5 points.
- **The aspiration in M02 (15% by FY30) is beyond the high case.** It would be worth about Rs 44 crore of net contribution a year against about Rs 15 crore of cost.

## 7. Value not counted

### 7.1 The customer's later purchases

A heavy user working 900 hours a year replaces rear tyres about every three years and front tyres about every two (`M04_Customer.md`). [Computed on survey hours and claimed tyre life]

| | Rs |
|---|---|
| Contribution from the first rear pair, after the offer | 6,010 |
| Contribution from later purchases over nine years, if six in ten owners stay each time: up to two more rear pairs and four front pairs | About 10,000 |
| Field cost of winning the pair, at the engine's break-even | About 7,600 |

A won heavy user is worth about two to three times the first sale. The cases above count only the year's sales. This repeats the reasoning of file 13, section 7.8, on the new figures. The retention rate is an assumption; Apollo's own repurchase data would replace it.

### 7.2 The other tyres the same dealer sells

| Rural replacement tyres | Illustrative value at manufacturers' prices | One point of share: revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Tractor | Rs 3,300 crore | Rs 33 crore |
| Car and utility vehicle | About Rs 4,500 crore | About Rs 45 crore |
| Two-wheeler | About Rs 6,100 crore | About Rs 61 crore |

[Computed] from M02 section 6: retail values less 18% GST and a 10% trade margin. Illustrative only.

- The coordinator who visits a dealer for farm tyres can carry the car and motorcycle ranges at no extra cost. Half a point of rural car-tyre share would add about Rs 7 crore of contribution, more than the field engine costs in 200 tehsils.
- We do not count it. Apollo already holds about 20% of car-tyre replacement and is short of capacity there too.
- It is the strongest reason to run the field engine through Apollo's rural sales organisation, with rural mobile stores and district distributors, and not as a separate farm team.

### 7.3 The fitment business

Three-quarters of Apollo's farm sales go to tractor makers and exports. A tractor maker is more likely to keep fitting a tyre its customers ask for again. Not quantified.
