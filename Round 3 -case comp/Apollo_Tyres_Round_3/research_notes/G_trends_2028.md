## 1. Mechanisation and tractors

### Takeaway
Tractor demand hit a record in FY26 (11.6 lakh wholesale) but every forecaster expects FY27 to be flat to slightly up, and the first half of FY27 data show a slowdown building into the festive season under a weak monsoon. The structural story to 2028-30 (higher hp, replacement of a strong 2018-19 cohort, low hp/ha) comes mainly from OEM and rating-agency commentary; no FY28 volume forecast from CRISIL/ICRA was found.

### (a) Observed now
- FY26 domestic wholesale 11,60,231 (+23.47%); FADA retail registrations 10,50,077 vs 8,82,825 in FY25; M&M 5,05,930 domestic (43.61% wholesale share). About 95% of tractors are bought on credit; RBI cut policy rate by 125 bps in 2025; GST cut (12% to 5%, from 22 Sep 2025) lowered prices by Rs 40,000-60,000. — Autocar Professional analysis, Apr 2026 [S4, opened] [Single source for several items; compiles CRISIL/ICRA quotes]
- Horsepower mix: 41-50 hp rose from under half of sales in FY19 to nearly two-thirds in FY26; 31-40 hp declined; 51 hp+ "a sliver" [S4]. Mahindra independently: 40-50 hp = 64% of FY2025 sales vs 49% five years earlier [S3, company claim]. CRISIL: after TREM-IV buyers shifted to 41-50 hp; 25-75 hp = ~90% of industry volume [S2]. [Verified fact: three sources agree, though they are not fully independent]. Historical reason 51 hp+ stayed small: price gap of ~Rs 1 lakh and limited multipurpose use (CRISIL, Jan 2024) [S5].
- Replacement: CRISIL Jan 2024 gave replacement as 50-60% of domestic sales (70-80% in Punjab/Haryana), ownership life 6-9 years, most replaced at 7-8 years, longer in lower-income states [S5]. The Apr 2026 analysis says typical owner replaces every five to nine years and the cohort now falling due is the strong 2018-19 vintage; replacement was muted in FY24 because the due cohort was small [S4]. (The brief states CRISIL at 60-65% replacement; the 2024 figure was lower, so share has been re-estimated upward.) [Verified fact on cycle range: two CRISIL-derived sources; both ultimately CRISIL].
- Tractor penetration: ~1.5 hp/ha in FY2022 vs 3-4 hp/ha in developed countries and 4.1 hp/ha in China (CRISIL, Jan 2024) [S5]. Mahindra: optimal mechanisation would need up to 17 million tractors at 4 hp/ha or 24 million at 6 hp/ha [S3, company framing].
- Regional shift: west (Maharashtra-led) ~40% of market growing up to 28%; north still >1/3 but moderating [S4].
- Commercial use (haulage, construction): 12-20% of use in normal years per estimates; one anonymous analyst put FY26 at 45-55% [S4] [Unverified secondary claim].
- Current-year momentum (FY27): ICRA: May 2026 wholesale +19.3%, retail +13.6% [S1]; July 2026 wholesale +20.1%, retail +28.3%; Apr-Jul wholesale +19% [S8]. ICRA later called demand "subdued" in August (search summary only). Then slowdown: Mahindra Finance tractor disbursements +13% in Jul-Sep quarter, slowest of its segments; early Navratri data suggest tractors may have declined more than last year; last year's festive base was inflated by pre-GST buying [S6, opened, 22 Oct 2026, CEO Raul Rebello]. Mahindra Oct 2026 domestic dispatches 62,626 (-13% vs 72,071), Apr-Oct 325,498 (~+2%); Mahindra blames Diwali falling in November [S7] [Single source, company]. Escorts Kubota Oct 2026 dispatches reported -10% (headline seen only, not opened) [Unverified secondary claim]. Industry-wide (TMA) Oct 2026: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- A page teaser dated 5 Nov 2026 on autocarpro.in says Mahindra cut its FY27 tractor industry growth outlook from mid-single digits to broadly flat citing weak monsoon and rising costs; I could not open that article [Unverified secondary claim].

### (b) What forecasters say about 2027-2028
- ICRA (17 Jun 2026): FY27 domestic wholesale +1-4% after +23.5%; high base, El Nino risk [S1] [Forecast]. CRISIL Ratings (18 Mar 2026): FY27 +0-2% (~1.2 million) [S2] [Forecast]; CRISIL "very muted, single-digit best case" (S4). YES Securities (May 2026): FY27 roughly flat, citing high base, price hikes, de-stocking, normalisation of Maharashtra/Punjab subsidy demand [S9, search summary only] [Forecast]. Mahindra (Aug 2026, Q1 FY27 commentary): mid-single-digit FY27 industry growth (search summary only) [Forecast, since reportedly cut, see teaser above].
- Mahindra (investor call, reported 20 Nov 2025): industry growth 9% a year to FY2030, installed base 12.2 million units by FY2030, shift to higher hp as one of three growth pillars [S3] [Forecast, OEM, made before the 2026 slowdown; treat as optimistic outlier].
- Emission norms: ICRA says stricter norms for 30-50 hp deferred from April 2026 to April 2028, expected to temper pre-buying [S1]; CRISIL describes draft TREM-V as moving <25 hp and >75 hp from 1 Oct 2026 with 25-75 hp exempt until FY2032 [S2]. The two descriptions differ on the 25-75 hp date; the rule is draft. A pre-buy ahead of April 2028 for 30-50 hp is a possible FY28 effect [Assumption, not found stated by a forecaster].
- FY28 volume or hp-mix forecast: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

### (c) Assumptions / unknowns
- Tractor replacement cycle length in 2026 and the share of replacement buyers who move up in hp: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation (CRISIL says none quantified).
- Growth in implements that load tyres harder (balers, loaders, trolleys, rotavators): no sourced statistic found. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Whether weak 2026 monsoon pushes FY28 replacements later (longer cycle) is an [Assumption] drawn from CRISIL's remark that lower-income states replace later.

## 2. Rural income and farm economics

### Takeaway
Farm incomes were helped in FY26 (good monsoon, MSP, rate cuts) but 2026 indicators have turned: NielsenIQ shows rural FMCG volumes -5% in Apr-Jun 2026, wage growth is disputed and weak in real terms, and a lender flags drought stress heading into half-yearly tractor-loan collections.

### (a) Observed now
- NABARD NAFIS 2021-22 (published Oct 2024): average monthly household income Rs 12,698 for all rural households (Rs 8,059 in 2016-17, +57.6%); agricultural households Rs 13,661; non-agricultural Rs 11,438 (one source says Rs 10,675, conflicting); cultivation ~one-third of agricultural-household income [S32, exam-prep sites relaying NABARD; NABARD PDF not opened] [Single source via secondary]. Newer household income survey: Data unavailable – requires primary research.
- NielsenIQ (reported 28 Sep 2026): Apr-Jun 2026 rural FMCG volume -5%, prices +2.1%, value -2.9% [S33, opened]. Earlier NIQ reports (search summary only): rural volume 7.7% vs urban 3.7% in Q3 2025; Jan-Mar 2026 all-India volume +0.9% [Single source]. [Interpretation: rural moved from out-performing urban (2024-25) to contracting by mid-2026].
- Rural wages: Labour Bureau series showed a jump (6% to 17%) from July 2025 that Systematix (Dhananjay Sinha) attributes to a methodology/sampling change; his adjusted estimate is ~4-4.3% y/y in March 2026, with real rural wages contracting [S34, commentary, Labour Bureau release not seen] [Single source; interpretation contested].
- Credit: Mahindra Finance's CEO said rural growth was ahead of urban in registrations in H1 FY27 and he was "not discounting rural sentiment overall", but 282 districts are drought-prone, three states are being watched (Maharashtra Vidarbha named), some funding was cut in stressed locations, and half-yearly tractor-loan instalments fall due "a couple of months" after October; he "wouldn't be surprised" by delays [S6].
- KCC: Budget 2026 reportedly lifted the interest-subvention KCC limit from Rs 3 lakh to Rs 5 lakh (devdiscourse headline, PIB Mar 2026 doc says the lending limit under modified interest subvention was enhanced in 2025-26; effective rate 4%) [S35, search summary only]; RBI KCC directions apply to loans sanctioned from 1 Jan 2027 [S35]. PM-KISAN instalment amounts, kharif 2026-27 MSP increase: Data unavailable – requires primary research (searches did not return them).
- Monsoon 2026: IMD first-stage forecast 90% +-4% of LPA with El Nino; deficit ~12% on 10 Aug 2026 (from ~30% end June); kharif acreage 2% lower on 7 Aug vs 21% lower late June; 2025-26 foodgrain +3% [S8]. (The brief's "13% below normal" is consistent in direction.) Final 2026-27 farm cash flow estimate: Data unavailable.

### (b) Forecasts
- No forecaster-published farm-income forecast to 2028 found. ICRA/CRISIL both condition FY27 tractor demand on rural cash flows and the monsoon [S1][S2].

### (c) Assumptions
- Weak kharif cash flow reduces ability to meet loan instalments and hence big-ticket purchases through early 2027 [Assumption, extrapolated from S6].

## 3. Custom hiring and rental

### Takeaway
Custom hiring is large on paper (tens of thousands of CHCs) and official subsidy design favours CHCs/FMBs for high-cost machinery, but I could not verify the December 2025 SMAM guideline contents or any current platform scale. The "who buys tyres" implication is an interpretation.

### (a) Observed now
- CHC counts conflict: 75,915 CHCs across India "as of 2025", Punjab leading with 11,148 (Autocar Professional, Apr 2026, no underlying source named) [S4]; versus search-summary figures of 27,554 CHCs + 646 hi-tech hubs + 25,608 farm machinery banks (outlets not opened) and a Lok Sabha-based 43,954 combined [S12] [Unverified secondary claim]. Different definitions likely (SMAM-funded vs all). Crop-residue scheme: >41,900 CHCs and >3.23 lakh machines supplied (Lok Sabha starred Q347, 25 Mar 2025, search summary only) [S13].
- SMAM funds released ~Rs 7,856 crore since inception to Jan 2025 (Lok Sabha Q370, 4 Feb 2025, search summary only) [S12]. SMAM pattern: CHCs 40% assistance up to Rs 250 lakh; FMBs up to 80% for projects up to Rs 30 lakh; high-cost machinery mainly promoted through CHCs/FMBs [S15, secondary]. Individual tractor buyers: 40% for general farmers, 50% for small/marginal/women/SC-ST under SMAM (per S4; subsidy text not verified).
- SMAM Revised Operational Guidelines dated 18 Dec 2025 exist (copy on HP agriculture department site) [S14] but the file (>10 MB) could not be fetched, so the "shift toward CHCs and FPOs" is NOT verified. Data unavailable – requires reading the PDF.
- Platforms: Mahindra Krish-e (since 2020; >100 centres in earlier reports; company says only ~10 million of 120 million tractor "users" own tractors; expanding to all ~1,600 dealerships per Krishi Jagran headline) [S17, company claims, dates unclear]. TAFE JFarm Services: farmer-to-farmer rental app; 2020 free-rental scheme covered 18,000 Massey Ferguson/Eicher customers and ~75,000 implement owners [S16, 2020-21 CSR]. EM3 Agri Services: 2016-17 claims of 8,000-13,000 farmers and 25,000-35,000 acres (inconsistent across sources) [S18, old]. 2026 scale of all three: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- PACS: 79,630 PACS sanctioned for computerisation, 63,707 onboarded; ~1.06 lakh functional PACS; 55,514 PACS providing Common Service Centre facilities (dates unclear) [S43, secondary, totals disagree]; scheme intends PACS-level godowns and CHCs; PACS CHC count: Data unavailable.

### (b) Forecasts
- None found for CHC or rental-platform growth to 2028.

### (c) Interpretation / assumption
- [Interpretation] Because subsidy design channels high-cost equipment to CHCs/FMBs and ~95% of tractor sales are credit-financed [S4], a growing minority of tractors would be operated by hire services that carry fleets of tractors and trolleys. No source quantifies the fleet share of tractor tyre demand. Data unavailable – requires primary research.

## 4. Digital adoption among farmers

### Takeaway
Rural connectivity is broad (95% of rural households have a phone; ~548 million rural internet users per IAMAI 2025) but depth is uneven (internet subscriptions per 100 are about 49 rural vs 129 urban). Farmer-specific behaviour data are only small regional studies. Government digital agri stack is scaling (7.6+ crore farmer IDs).

### (a) Observed now
- NSS 79th round CAMS (Jul 2022-Jun 2023): 94.2% of rural households possess a telephone/mobile (national 95.1%, urban 97.1%); among 15-29 year-olds 73.4% own a mobile phone with active SIM [S19, PIB May 2025 and India Education Diary]. Rural individual smartphone ownership: Data unavailable.
- TRAI, quarter ended June 2026 (released 12 Oct 2026; read via search summary and policy blog, TRAI PDF not opened): rural tele-density 60.89% (60.46% in March); 556.01 million rural telephone subscriptions = 41.24% of total; total internet subscribers 1,114.17 million; internet subscriptions per 100 population 49.07 rural vs 129.03 urban (blog figure) [S20] [Single source for per-100 figures; subscriptions are not users].
- IAMAI-Kantar "Internet in India 2025" (reported Jan 2026): ~958 million active users (+~8%), ~548 million rural (57%), rural growth nearly 4x urban; 588 million (61%) watched short video, rural marginally above urban; 44% used AI features; 18% use someone else's device (nearly 80% of those rural) [S21, via search summary of Free Press Journal/YourStory/Business Today; originals not opened] [Verified fact: multiple outlets agree; primary report not seen]. (Prior edition: 488 million rural users, per brief.)
- Farm-specific: small studies only: Guntur district AP (90 farmers) regular WhatsApp use 96.67%, YouTube 30%; PAU Kisan Club members 93.4% used WhatsApp for farm information, YouTube 76.16% [S24] [Single source each; non-representative]. National farmer WhatsApp/YouTube figure: Data unavailable – requires primary research.
- ASER 2024 (rural, ages 14-16, not farmers): ~90% have smartphone access at home; 82.2% can use one; 31.4% of those who can use have their own [S25, ThePrint].
- AgriStack/Digital Agriculture Mission (govt statement 14 Feb 2026): 7.63 crore farmer IDs created, 23.5 crore crop plots surveyed; Kisan e-Mitra chatbot >93 lakh queries by Dec 2025 (>8,000/day, 11 languages); AI monsoon-onset SMS pilot reached 3.88 crore farmers in 13 states [S22, opened]. Another compilation says 8.48 crore IDs by 4 Feb 2026 with a target of 11 crore by 2026-27 (conflict in as-of dates) [S23]. Bharat-VISTAAR AI helpline (155261) launched 17 Feb 2026 [S23]; usage data after launch: Data unavailable.
- UPI use in rural areas, WhatsApp Business/commerce adoption in India: Data unavailable – requires primary research (not searched within allowance).

### (b) Forecasts
- IAMAI/Kantar 2025 forecasts or any 2027-28 rural internet user projection: Data unavailable (not found). Govt target: 11 crore farmer IDs by 2026-27 [S23, secondary].

### (c) Assumption
- Rural users keep rising at 2025 pace through 2028 [Assumption; no forecaster found].

## 5. Rural e-commerce and tyre retail

### Takeaway
Small-town online shopping is large in platform data, but no source shows agri-input platforms selling tractor tyres or any tyre maker offering doorstep farm-tyre fitment. Apollo itself has just started a rural mobile-van retail pilot.

### (a) Observed now
- Platform datasets (not rural, not representative): Tier-3 cities ~40% of online orders in Republic Day 2026 sale (Unicommerce, 27 million order items); 50.7% of Diwali 2025 shipments (ClickPost, 42.5 million shipments) with cash on delivery 52%; tier 2-3 = 66% of new D2C orders in FY26 (Unicommerce) [S38, search summary only, trade/industry vendors; medium-low reliability]. Rural-only share of e-commerce orders: Data unavailable. Redseer/Bain "How India Shops Online": not found.
- Agri-input platforms: BigHaat markets seeds, crop protection, fertilisers, animal husbandry and farm machinery; AgroStar and DeHaat: nothing found on tyres [S39, CB Insights profile]. No evidence found that any of the three sells tractor tyres. Online tractor marketplaces list tyres and dealers (TractorGuru, Tractorkarvan, TractorGyan, TractorJunction) [S39, listing pages only; no sales data].
- Tyre makers: JK Tyre (CarDekho/AutoBrix) and CEAT (TyresNmore) doorstep fitment exist for cars/two-wheelers [S40, 2021 era]; no farm-tyre equivalent found. Apollo Tyres launched a rural mobile store (first van 20 May 2026, Pandavapura, Mandya, Karnataka), plans 25 vans in 18-24 months; the article does not say farm tyres are sold and says tyre dealerships are sparse or absent in some areas [S11, opened].
- ONDC and tyre sales: Data unavailable – not searched within allowance.
- Price context: raw material costs +15-20% sequential in Q1 FY27; Apollo price rise 6-8%, CEAT ~5%+5%, JK 4-5%, BKT 3-5% (Autocar Professional, 7 Jun 2026) [S10, opened]; CEAT warned demand could moderate after 5-10% hikes [S10].

### (b) / (c)
- No forecaster view on online tractor-tyre sales. [Assumption] none exists; online share of farm tyres is unknown.

## 6. Media change

### Takeaway
TV households are still rising (193 million in 2025, passing 200 million by about 2027) because Free Dish is growing while pay TV falls; connected TV is expanding fast. Sports rights holder JioStar reports record digital reach and regional-language dominance, while Doordarshan/Free Dish carries mandated "national importance" cricket. Rural-specific TV universe data for 2025-26 were not found.

### (a) Observed now
- FICCI-EY M&E Report 2026 (released ~24 Mar 2026): TV households 193 million in 2025 (190m in 2024, 186m in 2023), to cross 200 million within two years; Free TV (essentially all Free Dish) 45m (2023) to 53m (2025); linear pay TV lost ~11m homes to CTV/Free TV; CTV ~68m homes, ~40m weekly active (30m a year earlier); TV advertising -10%+, subscription revenue -8%; M&E industry Rs 2.78 trillion (+9%) [S26: Storyboard18 opened for 11m, 40m weekly CTV, 83m pay TV by 2028, Rs 37,700 crore; the Free Dish 53m and 193m figures are from search summary of e4m/Indian Television] [Verified fact on direction (two outlets); Single source on 53m].
- JioStar: FY26 revenue Rs 36,248 crore; >515 million viewers (linear + digital) on IPL 2026 opening weekend; IPL 2026 CTV reach +27% on IPL 2025 [S27, PTI, opened]; regional languages ~90% of IPL watch time (Exchange4media headline, article not opened) [S28, Unverified secondary claim]. JioHotstar subscriber counts conflict (200m, 280m, 300m at different dates) [Unverified secondary claim].
- Free-to-air cricket: for the 2026 T20 World Cup Prasar Bharati's DD Sports carried all India matches, both semi-finals and the final under the Sports Broadcasting Signals (Mandatory Sharing) Act; not all 55 games [S30, search summary of LatestLY/BW]. IPL on DD: no evidence found.
- Rural TV universe: the latest BARC rural universe figure found is dated (rural TV homes 99 million vs urban 84 million in 2016; a 2017 article put Free Dish at an estimated 74% rural viewing) [S29, old, low relevance]. A Shemaroo CEO remark (June 2025) that Free Dish reaches 41% of rural and 15% of urban TV homes in Hindi markets [S31, single, low reliability]. Current rural TV homes, rural Free Dish homes: Data unavailable – requires BARC.

### (b) Forecasts
- FICCI-EY (Mar 2026): pay-TV subscriptions fall at 3.1% a year to ~83 million by 2028; linear TV ad revenue to fall ~5% a year to 2028; linear + CTV ad revenue Rs 37,700 crore by 2028; TV households >200 million within two years [S26] [Forecast]. Another source puts CTV >90 million homes by 2028 [Unverified secondary claim].

### (c) Unknown
- Whether the post-March 2028 cricket rights and Team India sponsorship landscape change viewing: no source (outside scope).

## 7. Policy and regulation

### Takeaway
Tax and credit policy have been the main recent drivers (GST cut, KCC limit, rate cuts). Waste-tyre EPR is in force but its cost could not be verified; no farm-tyre labelling or mandatory BIS rule was found; electric-tractor scale is negligible in evidence.

### (a) Observed now
- Waste-tyre EPR: under Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules (2022 amendment); producers/importers register on CPCB portal; CPCB directed producers to procure EPR certificates (Business Standard, Feb 2024 headline) [S36, search summary]. NITI Aayog "Enhancing Circular Economy of Waste Tyres in India" (Jan 2026) exists but returned HTTP 403, so EPR targets and costs are unverified. Cost to tyre makers: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.
- Tyre standards: BIS has a specification "Tyres for agricultural vehicles and their trailers" drawing on UNECE R106 and ISO 4251-1; I found nothing showing it is mandatory under a quality control order, and no star-label scheme for farm tyres [S44, search summary]. Data unavailable on whether farm tyres fall under the automotive tyre QCO.
- PACS computerisation and CSC role: see Section 3 [S43]. Agriculture Infrastructure Fund and state tractor subsidies: Maharashtra and Bihar top-ups and Punjab/Maharashtra subsidy-led demand cited as FY26 supports and as normalising in FY27 [S4][S9]; scheme details: Data unavailable.
- Electric tractors: Sonalika Tiger Electric launched Dec 2020 at Rs 5.99 lakh [S37, Autocar Pro]. A low-reliability Aug 2026 blog lists several 2026 e-tractor launches (HAV 55 S1+, AutoNxt X45H4, Cellestial 55 HP, Montra E-27) and says there is no dedicated national e-tractor subsidy (unverified) [S37]. Sales volumes: Data unavailable. CNG/CBG tractors: Data unavailable (not searched).

### (b) Forecasts: none found. TREM-V timeline: see Section 1.

## 9. Tyre and tractor technology

### Takeaway
No current radialisation share or 4WD share was found; the only quantitative radial figure is from 2009 (2-3%). Product launches show makers are pushing farm radials and flotation sizes, and the hp shift implies larger rear tyres, but this is interpretation.

### (a) Observed now
- Farm radial share: ~2-3% of Indian agricultural tyre use in Aug 2009 (Apollo executive, Tire Review/Modern Tire Dealer) [S45, 17 years old]. Current share: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation (Apollo, CEAT, BKT, TAFE, TMA/ATMA).
- Products: Apollo Farm King radial was the first Indian agri OE radial (M&M, 2009) [S45]; TVS Eurogrip Tigertrac R-1W tractor radials showcased Feb 2024 [S46]; CEAT Floatmax flotation radial; BKT Agrimax RT 657 radial sizes [S46, search summaries].
- Farm demand: CEAT (Q4 FY26 call, 29 Apr 2026, preliminary transcript) said farm, two-wheeler and passenger growth was "very strong"; a Jan 2026 brokerage note said farm OEM demand grew double digits on tractor revival [S10b CEAT, search summary only]. BKT FY27 capex Rs 1,500-1,800 crore, Apollo Rs 3,500 crore [S10].
- IF/VF tyres, puncture-resistant compounds, sensor/connected tyres, 4WD share: Data unavailable.

### (b) Forecasts: only vendor market-report claims that radial demand is rising with mechanisation (IMARC, Credence; no percentages) [Unverified secondary claim; vendor press, low reliability].
