**The question.** How can Apollo Tyres use cricket, including its Team India sponsorship, to become the first tyre brand farmers and landowners think of, and turn that recall into tractor and farm tyre sales in rural India, affordably and measurably?

**How to read this file.** This is the summary of 17 supporting files. Labels in square brackets show how firm a statement is: [Verified fact], [Fact - single source], [Proxy evidence], [Interpretation], [Strategic hypothesis], [Planning assumption]. Sources, the fact-check record and every planning assumption are in `17_Source_Register_and_Verification.md`.

---

## 1. The answer

Apollo should not buy more cricket. It should build the last mile that the jersey lacks, run it in a cricket format, and test whether cricket adds anything.

- **The problem is not that tractor owners have not seen the name.** A tractor tyre is bought locally, at short notice, on tyre life, on the word of a dealer, a mechanic and other owners. Apollo has little working for it there, and no record of who buys. [Interpretation]
- **The recommendation is "Apollo XI".** In each chosen tehsil (sub-district), one stocked Apollo farm-tyre dealer captains a team of eleven mechanics. Every registered sale scores for the team. Tyre life is shown on local tractors and at free tyre health camps. The teams back village cricket tournaments that already exist. The best teams go to watch India as guests of the team's sponsor. [Planning assumption; names are working titles]
- **The pilot covers 60 tehsils in two contrasting belts**, around Bathinda and around Bareilly. Twenty get the full programme, twenty get the same trade work without cricket, twenty get nothing new.
- **It costs about Rs 7.4 crore** (range Rs 4.7 to 11.8 crore). Scale-up of Rs 10 to 17 crore is released only if the pilot passes tests agreed in advance. [Planning assumption; the brief states no budget]
- **Cricket has to earn its place.** On our planning inputs, the trade work pays for itself if it wins about four points of the local replacement market. The cricket layer pays only if it adds two to three points more. The three-arm pilot shows whether it does.

## 3. What the research found

### Apollo
- Farm is the smallest named product line in India. Truck and bus tyres are 55% of Indian-entity revenue. [Verified fact, File 01]
- Management has told investors twice that the cricket deal is bringing rural demand, naming car, two-wheeler and farm tyres. No number has been published. [Verified fact, Files 01, 03]
- Management named farm tyres as short of capacity in February 2026. All announced growth capex is for car and truck tyres. [Verified fact, File 01]
- Apollo is building rural reach: a national Head of Rural Sales since December 2025, a "One District, One ARD" strategy, and 25 rural mobile stores planned. [Verified fact, File 01]
- No farmer programme, mechanic programme or farm campaign was found after May 2024. [Verified fact that none was found, File 01]
- Results for the September 2026 quarter, published on 4 November 2026, show revenue up 16.7% and the operating margin down to about 11%. Money is tight. [Fact - single source, File 03]

### The buyer
- No published Indian study covers how farmers choose tractor tyres. [Verified fact that none was found, File 04]
- Tyre wear is the tractor owner's most common and most bothersome problem, and durability drives tyre satisfaction (J.D. Power, 14 states, 3,440 to 4,789 owners). [Fact - single source, File 04]
- Owners who rent the tractor out and haul with it work it about 800 hours a year and replace tyres soonest. [Fact - single source each; small, older district studies, File 04]
- For tractor upkeep, farmers trust mechanics and other owners over shops and advertisements. [Proxy evidence, File 04]
- No data shows how many tractor owners watch India's matches. [Verified fact that none was found, File 02]

### Rivals
- Goodyear supplies six tractor makers, so the owner's first tyres are often Goodyear's. MRF ranked first for tractor tyre satisfaction in 2018, with its largest lead on durability. [Fact - single source each, File 07]
- BKT, a farm-tyre maker, is tyre partner to eight of ten IPL teams. CEAT is an official IPL partner. [Verified fact, File 02]
- No neutral market-share figure exists for farm tyres, and no rival has published the results of a farmer programme. [Verified fact that none was found, File 07]

### The setting
- GST on tractor tyres fell from 18% to 5% in September 2025 and FY26 was a record year for tractor sales. Every brand's farm sales rose, so growth cannot be credited to the sponsorship without a control group. [Verified fact; Interpretation, File 03]
- The 2026 monsoon was about 13% below normal and tyre prices are rising 15 to 16%. Farmers will be short of cash in 2027. [Verified fact, File 03]
- The largest tractor registrations are in Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat. Bareilly and Bathinda are not among the top districts; they are useful as two contrasting kinds of market. [Fact - single source, File 06]

## 4. The diagnosis: five missing links

| Link | The owner's question | State today |
|---|---|---|
| Link | Does "Apollo" mean a tractor tyre to me? | The jersey says Apollo Tyres; the farm tyre sells as Virat or Krishak |
| Proof | Do I believe it lasts? | Rivals hold the reputation |
| People | Do the people I trust recommend it? | No mechanic programme |
| Presence | Can I get it today, near me? | Supply short; dealer cover uncounted |
| Record | Does Apollo know I bought? | No farmer-level record; no farm market-share data |

Cricket can help with the first and third. It cannot supply the others. Detail: `09_Problem_Diagnosis.md`.

## 5. The recommendation

| Part | What it does | Link built |
|---|---|---|
| Stock test | No tehsil gets marketing until a named dealer has the right farm tyres in stock | Presence |
| Apollo XI | Dealer plus eleven mechanics, scored on registered sales, rewarded with trips to India matches | People |
| Lambi Paari tractors | Heavy-use owners' tractors with hours logged and shown | Proof |
| Tyre health camps | Free checks that turn each tractor into a dated lead | Proof, Record |
| Exchange, harvest-timed credit, guaranteed gift | Makes the purchase manageable in a tight year | Presence |
| Scorecard | Buyer registers by missed call or WhatsApp; warranty active at once | Record |
| Apollo Kisan Cup | Teams back existing village tournaments; finals at tehsil, district and season level | Link, People |
| Sponsor's line | "Team India's sponsor, on your tractor", as the BCCI approves | Link |

**What to back in cricket.** The jersey as credential and source of rewards; the home Tests against Australia (January to March 2027) to recruit teams; the 2027 World Cup (October to November 2027) as the main sales window, because it falls at harvest; village tournaments as the gathering point.

**What not to back.** IPL teams, state T20 league titles, a new cricketer for farm tyres, a national farm-tyre television campaign. They build only the first link and cannot be aimed at tractor owners.

Eight alternatives were scored on twelve criteria. Apollo XI scored 52 of 60, the same trade work without cricket 50, and media, a star and a state league 25 to 29 (`12_Strategy_Evaluation_and_Stress_Testing.md`).

## 6. Money, targets and proof

All programme figures are planning assumptions.

| | Pilot | Scale (if Gate 1 is passed) |
|---|---|---|
| Where | 60 tehsils in 12 districts | About 200 tehsils |
| When | March to June 2027; read-out August 2027 | September 2027 to March 2028 |
| Cost | Rs 7.4 crore (Rs 4.7 to 11.8 crore) | Rs 10.2 crore (trade work only) or Rs 17.4 crore (with cricket) |
| Recall target | First mention up 8 to 10 points against control | Held |
| Sales target | 4 or more points of the local market against control | 4 to 7 points |
| Return | Not expected to pay back | About 1.0 per rupee in year one at 4 points (trade work) or 7 points (with cricket) |

The whole programme to March 2028 would cost Rs 17.6 to 24.8 crore: 3 to 4% of the reported jersey fee, and 1.5 to 2% of one year's Indian farm revenue.

**Proof.** A survey of 600 tractor owners per arm before and after, and sales from Apollo's billing and registrations, compared with control areas. Decision rules are fixed before launch. Attendance and views are never counted as results (`14_KPI_and_Impact_Measurement.md`).

## 8. The test the brief sets

Could Apollo's management take this proposal, allocate a budget, run a pilot, measure the results and decide whether to scale?

- **Allocate:** a staged budget with a capped first step.
- **Run:** an owner (the Head of Rural Sales), a calendar tied to the crop year, and a kit built on dealers Apollo already has.
- **Measure:** three arms, a baseline, and decision rules set in advance.
- **Decide:** at Gate 1 in August 2027 and Gate 2 in March 2028.

What Apollo must supply first: farm tyre sales by dealer, its share of replacement sales, margin per tyre, farm tyre supply by depot, and the sponsorship contract's terms on tickets, imagery and designation.
