## 4. What BCG has and has not published (quotable paragraph)

BCG's published India consumer work comes from its Center for Customer Insight and is urban-weighted: the 2019 Ten Trends, the 2021 Retail Resurgence consumer exhibits and most 2022 e-commerce exhibits are explicitly urban only, and the one dedicated rural study, The Rising Connected Consumer in Rural India (August 2016, about 4,000 consumers in 27 villages in 14 states), is a decade old. What BCG has said about rural India is consistent from 2016 to 2026: rural internet users now outnumber urban (52% of users in 2021), rural shoppers are about 30% of online shoppers in 2025 and projected to be 54% by 2030, but the purchase stays physical and local, driven by trust, "test and check", cash, friends and family (57% of auto buyers against 35% citing dealers, 2014) and the known local seller, and rural India's share of household consumption is projected to fall from 47% (2019) to 41% (2030). On farmers, BCG's primary research is from the United States (1,000 growers over 250 acres, 2024) and its agribusiness value-creator reports cover equipment makers' returns, not aftermarket or dealers; its India farmer evidence is client stories (DeHaat in Himachal Pradesh, Jiva) and secondary data (tractor sales in the June 2025 Economic Monitor; 15% of 120 million farmers using mechanical equipment in a 2017 Mahindra sidebar). BCG has published nothing on Indian tractor owners' buying behaviour, tyre dealers, farm-tyre replacement or rural cricket viewership, so any C03 to C05 claim on those must rest on primary research or non-BCG sources, labelled as such.

## 6. Discrepancies corrected before use

1. **"Rural will be 45% of consumer spending by 2030" is not what the report says.** The 45% in How India Spends (p.13) is "India's two highest income brackets ... accounting for approximately 45 percent of India's consumer spending in 2030, up from 33 percent today." Exhibit 4 on the same page gives rural's share of household consumption as 52% (2010), 47% (2019), 41% (2030). Use 41% and cite the exhibit.
2. **Projection versus measurement.** 315 million connected rural consumers by 2020 (row 1), half of internet users rural by 2020 (rows 2, 3), 54% of online shoppers and 24% of online spend rural by 2030 (row 5), 41% rural consumption share by 2030 (row 7) are projections. Measured: 52% of internet users rural in 2021 (row 5); rural 26% to 30% of online shoppers, 2021 to 2025 (row 10); rural online purchase penetration 4% to 8%, 2015 to 2016 (row 1).
3. **The "cash ... lower trust ... test and check" sentence is a quotation, not a survey result.** On the 2023 ConsumerTech slide (row 6) it is attributed to "CXO, ConsumerTech startup". BCG's measured line on the slide is the 57% cash-on-delivery share for rural orders in 2021.
4. **"Test and check" is not in the June 2022 e-commerce report** as the brief implied; the nearest lines are the trust-with-sellers quotation and "consumers like to get second and third opinions before buying" (row 5).
5. **Urban-only samples.** Ten Trends 2019 (row 4): metros and tiers 1 to 3 only. Retail Resurgence 2021 (row 14): every CCI consumer exhibit reads "Data of only urban consumers"; its one rural number (11x packaged staples growth) is Nielsen data relayed by BCG. E-commerce 2022 (row 5): Exhibits 2, 4, 7, 9, 11, 12 and 13 are urban only.
6. **The 1,000-farmer study is US only** (row 17); its trust segmentation can seed a hypothesis for Indian farmers, not a claim about them.
7. **Sample-size mismatch for the same survey.** How India Spends (row 7) says "more than 8,200 consumers" in 2019; Retail Resurgence (row 14) source notes say "N=8,500" for the same 2019 CCI survey. Cite each report's own figure.
8. **Dates and URLs.** The e-commerce PDF is dated June 2022, the article July 06, 2022; the Brands Need Friends PDF is August 2014, the article September 04, 2014; the Decoding Digital Consumers URL in the brief returns 404 (live URL in row 3); the Jiva and JICA pages carry no date.
9. **Jiva's income claim is company-reported** ("up to 25%", row 22) with no independent evaluation, and BCG's own 2021 essay (row 21) calls smallholder income evidence "surprisingly limited". Do not use as a benchmark.
10. **The mechanisation figure is dated and indirect.** "only about 15% of India's 120 million farmers use mechanical equipment" (row 26) is a 2017 case sidebar, not a BCG survey; cross-check against government data before use in C03.
