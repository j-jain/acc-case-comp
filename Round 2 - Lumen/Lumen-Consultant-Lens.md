# Lumen through a McKinsey / BCG lens: the diagnosis a partner would give, and the festivals it produces

Third file in the set. Read after `Lumen-Case-Facts.md` (verified facts) and `Lumen-Round2-Scenarios.md` (the five designs). This file does two things: it re-runs the case the way a strategy partner would run it, and it harvests the surprising festivals, rituals and games that fall out of consulting instruments rather than out of anthropology. Earth references are for the team's reasoning and the Q&A only; none of them goes on the slide.

Written Sunday 1 Nov 2026. The slide is due Monday 2 Nov, noon.

---

## 1. Why this lens matters for this jury

ACC is the Ashoka Consulting Club. The three grading words, creative / authentic / well-structured, are consulting words. "Well-structured" to a consulting-club judge means a governing thought, a MECE breakdown, a chain from fact to recommendation, and a plan with numbers. The five designs in the scenarios file have strong content; this lens supplies the frame they will be graded through, and it generates a different family of ideas because consultants start from the economics and the operations, not from the sky.

The pyramid-principle answer, in one sentence a partner would put at the top of page one:

> Lumen does not have a culture problem; it has a demand problem. Nothing competes with work for 20 waking hours, so the equilibrium is "go home alone". The fix is a portfolio of three products on one flywheel, piloted in the 1,000-seat room, scaled one ward at a time, and measured on two numbers: the fill rate of gatherings and the share of residents who can name something they are looking forward to.

## 2. The diagnosis: eight findings a partner would put on page two

Each is a reading of the case facts that the anthropological designs did not use, or used without saying so.

| # | Finding | Evidence in the case | So what |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The 1,000-cap is not the binding constraint today. Demand is.** | "Community gatherings are close to empty." A venue that is nearly empty is not capacity-constrained. | Designing around the cap (fifty nights, relays) is a Year-10 problem. Year 1 must create want. Manufacture scarcity before the room is full, not after: Singapore's National Day Parade gives away free seats by ballot and the ballot makes a state ritual feel like a prize. |
| 2 | **Culture's competitor is work, and work is winning 100% of share.** | "They choose to occupy their time with work due to the absence of meaningful shared experiences." | Treat culture as a product entering a market with one incumbent. Two strategies: bundle (put the ritual inside the workday) or create a category the incumbent cannot enter (the dark hours). Do not fight head-on for the light hours. |
| 3 | **The dark hours are an idle asset of 104 million person-hours a year.** | 15 dark hours, at least 10 asleep → up to 5 waking dark hours; 50,000 × 5 × 416 = 104,000,000. | Any night-based product has zero opportunity cost. A consultant would call this the cheapest capacity on the planet. |
| 4 | **The elders are culture's labour force, and they are idle.** | Work ends at about 250; life ends at 300 → about 8,300 people with 20 free waking hours each: 166,000 free hours a day. | Every design needs stewards, hosts, judges, keepers. Lumen already has a volunteer corps the size of a town. On Earth, retirees run the clubs; on Lumen they can run the culture. |
| 5 | **Helium release is a token burn: a festival that spends gas makes every remaining holder richer.** | Precious gases are the currency; helium, once released, leaves a planet for good. | A public release of currency is monetary tightening. The festival that "wastes" gas has a positive return for the participants' balance sheets. De Beers built a century of value on controlling supply; Lumen can ritualise it. |
| 6 | **"Nothing to look forward to" is literally a forecasting deficit.** | The case names the symptom in those words. | Institutionalise looking forward: sealed predictions, dated promotions, a pipeline of announced events. The culture's product is anticipation, and anticipation can be manufactured on a schedule. |
| 7 | **The secret Head is a mystique asset. Do not resolve it; productise it.** | "Reporting to a secret Head of Government." | Satoshi, Banksy, the Buffett letter. An unseen author who writes once a year is worth more than a visible one who speaks daily. Every design must also work if the Head never writes (key-person risk). |
| 8 | **"No inherited traditions" can be made the tradition.** | "No history, no religion and no inherited traditions." | Zero-based culture: nothing is inherited; every tradition must be re-adopted by the generation that did not make it. Sunset clauses turn the case's constraint into Lumen's constitution. |

Two frames a partner would draw:

- **7-S.** Lumen has the hard S's (structure: Council and Head; systems: the gas economy; staff and skills: all adults work) and is missing exactly two soft ones: shared values and style. The festival is the shared value; the ritual is the style. Strategy is the Council's brief.
- **The ritual portfolio as a BCG matrix.** Axes: frequency × emotional intensity. Rare and intense = **Peaks** (the festival). Frequent and mild = **Habits** (the ritual). Frequent and intense = **Burnout** (do not build). Rare and mild = **Forgettable** (do not build). The brief asks for one Peak, one Habit, and a third product, entertainment, which is the **Pipeline** that carries anticipation between them. Harvey Whitehouse's research says the same thing in anthropology: rare high-arousal rites fuse small groups; frequent low-arousal rites hold large populations. A strong answer has one of each and a pipeline.

## 3. The surprising festivals a consultant would propose

Each one is generated by a consulting instrument, not by a myth. For each: the mechanics, why it is surprising, the Earth precedent (for reasoning only), the copy-risk as an Indian judge would see it, and which case facts it uses.

### F1. THE SUNSET (zero-based culture)
**Instrument:** zero-based budgeting; sunset clauses; portfolio pruning.
**Mechanics.** Every tradition on Lumen expires after one Ring (50 years) unless the generation that did not create it votes to keep it. Each year, the traditions whose fiftieth anniversary falls that year come up for renewal, so every tradition is reviewed once per Ring and a few are on trial every year. On the last night of the year, in the room, each tradition's keepers make a five-minute case; the vote belongs to those under 50, who inherited nothing. A tradition that fails is performed by the whole city **one last time** that night (the Last Time), then its objects go into the Hall of the Retired: the only museum on Lumen, and therefore the planet's history, written as the list of things it chose to stop doing. The Sunset is the one tradition exempt from itself.
**Why surprising.** The anchor festival is the ritual death of rituals. A culture whose central act is choosing, every year, what not to inherit.
**Earth precedent.** Ise Grand Shrine is torn down and rebuilt every 20 years and has been since 690 CE; Puri's idols are carved anew every 12 to 19 years. Both renew the object; Lumen renews the practice itself. Sunset clauses in legislation. The "last episode" effect in television.
**Copy-risk:** low. No Earth festival is a referendum on festivals.
**Uses:** fact 10 (no inherited tradition), fact 11 (the Ring), fact 3 (the room as parliament).

### F2. THE ACCOUNT (the planetary annual general meeting)
**Instrument:** the annual report; the shareholder meeting; the chairman's letter.
**Mechanics.** Every Lumenite is a shareholder of Lumen. On the first day of the year the planet's accounts are read aloud: births, deaths, hours worked, gas mined and gas lost, the sky's events, the names given, the fill rate of gatherings. For the last moon before it, the Council disappears into the Lock-in to prepare the Account and is not seen (the anticipation is the absence). The secret Head's annual letter is read by a citizen drawn by lot that minute; if the Head wrote nothing, the empty page is shown. Then questions drawn by lot are answered by the Council in public. 1,000 in the room by ballot; the rest hear it in the open and at street tables.
**Why surprising.** The festival is an annual report. It sounds like the sterile thing Lumen already is, which is the joke the design is in on: on Earth, the one AGM that became a festival is called Woodstock.
**Earth precedent.** Berkshire Hathaway's annual meeting in Omaha draws about 40,000 shareholders (Guinness lists 2017's estimated 42,000 as the largest AGM ever) and is nicknamed "Woodstock for Capitalists"; the Buffett letter is read by millions who own no shares. In India, Budget Day is watched like a festival, and the Finance Ministry's halwa ceremony locks sixty-odd officials into the North Block basement for about ten days before it: a ritual of secrecy that creates public anticipation.
**Copy-risk:** low to medium (a judge may say "Budget Day").
**Uses:** fact 5 (the Head's letter), fact 4 (gas accounts), fact 7 (hours worked as a public number), fact 10 (the Account is the history).

### F3. THE WALKOUT (the city emptied for one night)
**Instrument:** the off-site; "you cannot see the system from inside it"; unused-asset analysis; business-continuity drill.
**Mechanics.** Lumen's 50,000 live in one concentrated area on a small planet; the rest of the planet is empty, and a consultant notices an unused asset. Once a year, at dusk, the whole population walks out of the settlement by fifty roads, one per ward, to the plain beyond, and camps the night in fifty camps of a thousand under the open sky, with the city dark behind them (nobody home, nobody spending: on Lumen a dark city is a city with its money in its pocket). At hour 25 the camps turn to face the city (the Turn): the only time Lumenites ever see the place they live from outside. At dawn the wards race back (the Return) and re-light their wards one by one, so the city switches on in sequence, visible from the plain; the order of return is the year's standing. Then everyone sleeps the first ten light hours.
**Why surprising.** A festival whose venue is "not the city". It needs no building, inverts the venue cap (fifty camps of a thousand reproduce the room outdoors), and gives a people who "have never known any other world" one night a year outside the only place they know.
**Earth precedent.** Hajj's Mina tent city holds up to three million; Kumbh builds a 4,000-hectare city for 45 days; Burning Man builds a city to leave it (Lumen leaves a city to see it); the kibbutz Omer festival walks the whole community out to the first field. It is also, quietly, a full evacuation rehearsal: the Council gets a disaster drill disguised as a holiday.
**Copy-risk:** medium ("a camping trip"; "exodus"). Defence: no destination, no shrine, no tents sold; the point is the Turn.
**Uses:** fact 3 (single area; the cap reproduced as camps), fact 1 and 8 (the sky), fact 4 (the dark city), fact 9 (the shifted sleep).

### F4. THE FORECAST (the sealed predictions)
**Instrument:** scenario planning; forecast calibration; prediction markets.
**Mechanics.** On the first night of the year every household seals thirteen predictions for the year: sky events, the Fix of the gases, births, which name the Naming will choose, whether a tradition survives the Sunset. On the last night they are opened and scored; the best households are the year's Seers and get the first seats at the next Account. A moon-by-moon version runs as a game (see E2). A people with no past builds foresight instead.
**Why surprising.** The case's complaint is "nothing to look forward to"; the consultant's literal answer is to institutionalise looking forward. The festival is anticipation itself, scored.
**Earth precedent.** At Ugadi, the Telugu new year, temple scholars read the new almanac's predictions for the year aloud (Panchanga Sravanam) to the assembled public; Sarhul's priest reads the water left overnight in two pots to forecast the monsoon. Superforecasting tournaments and Brier scores.
**Copy-risk:** low to medium (the public almanac reading is a recognisable form; the sealed, scored, household version is not).
**Uses:** fact 8 (sky events are the predictable unpredictable), fact 10, fact 4.

### F5. THE SHIFT (the whole society promoted on one day)
**Instrument:** succession planning; synchronised hiring cycles.
**Mechanics.** On one day a year every role on the planet moves one step: the cohort turning 250 leaves its posts, every post below is handed down the chain in a single wave, and the cohort turning 50 enters at the bottom. The handover is the Two Lessons: each person teaches their successor one thing and receives one from their predecessor, in person, that day. The whole org chart of a planet moves in one synchronised motion.
**Why surprising.** "Nothing to look forward to" is fixed structurally: everyone's next step has a date, and the whole city takes it together. Lumen's biggest shared experience is a promotion.
**Earth precedent.** Japan starts the working year on 1 April, when roughly 900,000 people report to new jobs on the same morning; the Japanese fiscal year, school year and hiring cycle all turn on that date. Japan's Coming of Age Day gathers every twenty-year-old in a town in one hall.
**Copy-risk:** low (no festival on Earth is a promotion wave; the risk is "convocation").
**Uses:** fact 7 (all adults work), fact 11 (the 50/250 thresholds and a 200-year working life).

### F6. THE ROTATION (planet-wide job swap for a day)
**Instrument:** cross-functional rotation; job shadowing.
**Mechanics.** One day a year, every one of the roughly 33,000 workers does someone else's job, assigned by lot within safety classes (no one flies anything they have not flown). The festival is a workday made strange. The stories it produces fill the street tables for a moon.
**Why surprising.** The festival takes place at work. For a people who fill their time with work, the one thing they cannot imagine is work being unfamiliar.
**Earth precedent.** None at planetary scale; corporate rotations and "take your child to work" days are the nearest.
**Copy-risk:** low. **Risk:** safety and output; a consultant mitigates with role classes and one day only.
**Uses:** fact 7 directly.

### F7. THE WINDOW (a festival with a season but no date)
**Instrument:** variable-ratio reinforcement; the product "drop".
**Mechanics.** The major festival has a fixed three-moon window and an unannounced day inside it. The trigger is drawn by lot in the room at the start of the window (sealed) or set by the Council; the city gets 30 hours' notice. For three moons the whole settlement is in "festival readiness": the bag is packed, the ward knows its role. Anticipation is spread across 96 days instead of concentrated on one.
**Why surprising.** The opposite of a calendar festival: one nobody can schedule, in a society that schedules everything.
**Earth precedent.** Eid's date is confirmed the evening before by moon-sighting (a one-day uncertainty; this is a hundred-day one); snow days; limited drops. Tension to state honestly: cross-cultural data say dense societies prefer calendar-fixed rites, so the window is fixed even if the day is not.
**Copy-risk:** medium (moon-sighting mechanism).
**Uses:** fact 8 (a sky event may also trigger it), fact 3.

### F8. THE DISCLOSURE (wealth made visible for one night)
**Instrument:** radical transparency.
**Mechanics.** One night a year, every household's gas holdings are declared in public, read ward by ward in the room and posted at every door, in the fixed unit of breaths. Nothing is lit, nothing is spent: the city simply knows, for one night, what everyone has. The next day the numbers come down.
**Why surprising.** The most private fact in a capitalist economy is the festival's content.
**Earth precedent.** Finland publishes every citizen's taxable income on the first working day of November; foreign media call it National Jealousy Day. Norway and Sweden do similar.
**Copy-risk:** low (not a festival anywhere). **Risk:** judges may find it dystopian; a consultant would present it as a pricing of status that replaces display with disclosure. Treat as a wildcard.
**Uses:** fact 4.

### F9. THE WAITLIST (the festival nobody is allowed to attend yet)
**Instrument:** manufactured scarcity; waitlist marketing; staged rollout.
**Mechanics.** Year 1: the festival runs for one ward of a thousand, chosen by lot; the other 49 wards watch from outside and are told their year. Year 2: thirteen wards, one per moon. By Year 4 every ward has been inside once; from Year 5 it is the full cycle. The first four years are the launch.
**Why surprising.** A culture that opens by excluding 98% of its people, on purpose, and tells them when their turn is.
**Earth precedent.** Bayreuth's opera tickets have a waiting list of five to ten years; Singapore's ballot; Oberammergau plays once a decade.
**Copy-risk:** low (a launch plan, not a festival).
**Uses:** fact 3 and finding 1. This is the consultant's answer to Auroville, which planned for 50,000 and reached 3,300: a plan with no pull. A waitlist is pull.

## 4. The surprising rituals and the pipeline products

### Rituals (the Habit)

**R1. THE SPILL (a daily ten-minute fire drill as culture).** At hour 10 of the light, every workplace empties into the street for ten minutes. Nothing is organised; no words are required; for ten minutes the entire city is outside at once. Cost: about 5,600 work-hours a day, under 2% of work time, for the cheapest synchrony on the planet. Earth precedent: Israel's two-minute siren on its memorial day stops every car on every highway; India's 2020 five o'clock clap from every balcony. A consultant's minimum viable ritual. Copy-risk medium (the minute's silence) if it is silent; make it a spill, not a silence.

**R2. THE FILL (the number the city chases).** On the first night of each moon, two numbers are read in the room and posted at every door: the fill rate of the moon's gatherings (seats filled over seats offered) and the alone rate (share of dark hours spent alone, from a one-line diary). The best street is named. A planet that cheers its own attendance figure. Goodhart's law applies and the design says so: the numbers are reported, never targeted by pay. Copy-risk low.

**R3. THE TWO LESSONS (the handover).** Part of F5, usable alone: whenever a role changes hands, the leaver teaches one thing and learns one thing from the joiner, in person, before the chair is empty. Copy-risk low.

**R4. THE LOCK-IN (the Council vanishes).** For the last moon before the Account the Council is sequestered and unseen; the absence is the countdown. Precedent: the halwa ceremony's ten-day lock-in before India's Budget. Copy-risk low to medium.

**R5. THE EDGE (the street walks to the edge).** On every rest pair, each street of about fifty walks to the edge of the settlement and back together. The weekly reminder that the city has an outside, and the rehearsal of the Walkout's roads. Copy-risk low.

### Pipeline products (the entertainment)

**E1. THE CASE (the planet's sport is its own problems).** Once a moon the Council sets a real problem of the city; teams of ten have the five dark hours in the room to solve it; the room votes; the winning answer is implemented within the moon, and the Sunset reviews it fifty years later. For a consulting-club jury this is a wink, and it is also native: a people with no stories has only its own problems to play with. Earth precedent: case competitions, hackathons, Durga Puja's juried theme pandals. Copy-risk low.

**E2. THE FORECAST GAME (the moon-by-moon prediction market).** Every moon, households stake a capped measure of gas on next moon's sky and the Fix; the pot is released into the sky at the Account, so no one profits in gas, only in standing. Precedent: Diwali-night card games are India's licensed, bounded gambling; prediction markets. Copy-risk medium (gambling splits judges).

**E3. THE RETURN (fifty roads, fifty relays).** Weekly relay races of fifty on the fifty roads out of the city, the rehearsal for the Walkout's dawn return; the league decides who re-lights first. Precedent: Vallam Kali's synchrony, the Olympic relay. Copy-risk low to medium.

## 5. Three complete consultant-grade answers

Each is a Peak, a Habit and a Pipeline product that interlock, with the two numbers a partner would track, the pilot, and the risk a judge will raise.

### Answer A: ZERO-BASED LUMEN (a culture that re-chooses itself)
- **Festival:** the Sunset (F1).
- **Ritual:** the Spill (R1).
- **Entertainment:** the Case (E1).
- **Interlock:** the Case produces the candidate traditions; the Sunset decides which survive; the Spill is the first tradition ever put on trial, in Year 50, and the design says the city may kill it. The Hall of the Retired is the archive.
- **Through-line for the slide:** "Lumen inherited nothing. So nothing on Lumen is inherited: every tradition is re-chosen by the people who did not make it."
- **KPIs:** renewal rate (share of traditions re-adopted per year; a healthy culture retires some), participation in the Last Time.
- **Pilot:** Year 1 has nothing to retire, so Year 1 is the first Case, and the first Sunset is the night the Case's winners are adopted as traditions with a fifty-year expiry date stamped on each.
- **Judge's question:** "A referendum is not a festival." Answer: the Last Time is. Fifty thousand people doing a thing together for the last time is the most emotional night a city can have, and grief rituals measurably work (Norton & Gino 2014).
- **Risk:** cerebral; needs the Last Time to carry the feeling.

### Answer B: LUMEN INC. (the planet reports to itself)
- **Festival:** the Account (F2), preceded by the Lock-in (R4).
- **Ritual:** the Fill (R2), monthly.
- **Entertainment:** the Forecast (F4 as the yearly frame, E2 as the monthly game); the Seers are named at the Account.
- **Interlock:** the Fill supplies the numbers the Account reads; the Forecast is scored against the Account; the Head's letter is the only thing the secret Head ever gives the city, and it is read at the Account by someone drawn by lot.
- **Through-line:** "Lumen has a secret Head and a public ledger. Once a year, the ledger speaks and the Head writes."
- **KPIs:** fill rate; share of households that sealed a Forecast (participation in anticipation).
- **Pilot:** the first Account is read to one ward in the room and to the rest by street table; the first Forecast is sealed that night.
- **Judge's question:** "You cured 'functional but empty' with an annual report?" Answer: the one AGM on Earth that became a festival draws 40,000 people to Omaha and is called Woodstock; what makes it a festival is the letter and the questions, not the accounts. And the Lock-in makes the Council vanish for a moon, which is the most anticipation a government can manufacture.
- **Risk:** reads corporate; the joke must be visible on the slide or it reads as a mistake.

### Answer C: THE WALKOUT (see the system from outside)
- **Festival:** the Walkout (F3).
- **Ritual:** the Edge (R5), weekly, by street.
- **Entertainment:** the Return (E3), weekly relays on the fifty roads.
- **Interlock:** the Edge teaches every street its road; the Return's league fixes the order the wards re-light the city; the Walkout is the one night the whole plan runs at once. Fifty camps of a thousand reproduce the 1,000 cap outdoors, so the venue constraint is honoured on open ground.
- **Through-line:** "Fifty thousand people live in one place on a small planet and have never seen it from outside. One night a year, they leave it, turn around, and look."
- **KPIs:** participation (target: all 50,000; the elders ride); the alone rate on rest pairs (the Edge is by street).
- **Pilot:** Year 1 walks one ward out as a rehearsal of the roads; Year 2 all fifty.
- **Judge's question:** "Isn't this a camping trip?" Answer: there is no destination and nothing is sold; the festival is the Turn, the moment the city is seen dark from outside, and the Return, when it is switched on ward by ward. It is also the only design in which the city's money is visibly all in its pocket at once.
- **Risk:** weather ("natural phenomena are common"), elders, fifty thousand people outdoors; mitigated by fifty stewarded camps and the weekly Edge as rehearsal.

## 6. The consultant's overlay for the Fifty Nights (if the base design stays)

Three moves from this lens strengthen the scenarios file's recommended design without changing it:

1. **The launch is a waitlist (F9).** Year 1: thirteen wards, one night per moon, the rest told their year; Year 4: all fifty have been inside; Year 5: the full fifty nights. This answers finding 1 (demand first) and the Auroville risk (no pull).
2. **The Breath is a token burn (finding 5).** The slide can say, in one line, that the only night Lumen spends its money in public is the night every remaining holder becomes richer. For a jury that may include De Beers, that line lands.
3. **Two numbers and a control group.** The Table of Ten starts in 25 wards in Year 1 and the other 25 in Year 2, so the first year of Lumen's culture is a randomised trial of itself, measured on the fill rate and the alone rate. India is where this method lives: the Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab ran its first randomised trials here and its founders shared the 2019 Nobel for it.

## 7. The slide, structured the way this jury reads

Pyramid order, left to right:

1. **Governing thought** (the headline): the one sentence that is also the answer.
2. **Diagnosis** (three lines): symptom → cause → the fact that makes Lumen's answer different from Earth's.
3. **Design principles** (three or four): demand before capacity; synchronised rest; cost creates meaning; nothing inherited.
4. **The three products as one flywheel**: festival (Peak), ritual (Habit), entertainment (Pipeline), each with its generating fact and one line on why only Lumen could have it.
5. **Launch and measurement**: pilot in the room, scale by ward, two numbers, a control group.
6. **Key assumptions** (the brief asks for them): 32-day moon, 8-day week with two shared rest days, stable population, a fixed "breath" of gas, open air uncapped, light runs on the currency gases.
7. **Risks and the answer to each** (two or three).

No Earth festival, country, company or religion is named anywhere on the slide. The references in this file are for the team's reasoning and the Q&A.
