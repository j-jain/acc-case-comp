# C06. The jersey as a known-buyer asset, and how the programme is measured

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the BCG-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using BCG's published work on sport ("Beyond Media Rights", Schmidt and others, 5 February 2026; the Olympics episode of BCG's podcast, July 2024) and on marketing measurement ("Marketing Measurement Done Right", September 2019; "Six Steps to More Effective Marketing Measurement", June 2025; the four-legged approach, August 2024; the geo-testing and incrementality pieces of 2026). It is not BCG's work. BCG has published nothing on cricket, the IPL, the BCCI or Indian sport, and nothing in its sport work is addressed to a sponsor; every transfer to Apollo's jersey below is ours and is labelled so. Sources: `..\research_notes\S6_sponsorship_known_fans_measurement.md`. The cricket facts (reach, calendar, rights, rivals' assets) are in `..\McKinsey_Lens\M06_Sponsorship.md`, `..\Bain_Lens\B06_Sponsorship_Zero_Click_Test.md` and `..\research_notes\Q7`, and are not repeated.

**Purpose.** To say what the jersey is for in farm when read through BCG's sport work (a relationship with known people, not reach), what a known farm buyer base is and how it is built, which cricket uses are always-on and which are moments, and how the whole programme is measured so that a chief financial officer believes it.

Where nothing reliable exists: Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **BCG's sport work is about turning followers into known people.** "Today, most sports organizations convert only a small fraction of their fan base, including their most devoted "core" fans, into known and contactable fans"; conversion "range[s] from as low as 1% to more than 60%"; value comes from "how effectively teams convert fan attention into measurable partner value"; the shift is "from audience reach to relationship depth". It is written for teams. Nothing in it tells a sponsor what to do. [BCG-published; Verified fact]
2. **Apollo's farm business has no known buyers at all.** A warranty number is issued by a toll-free call or by the dealer in a dealer-only app; no count is published; CEAT alone pays for registration (six extra months of cover). The one Indian durable programme at scale, Hero's GoodLife, enrolled 45 million members by having the dealer sign the buyer up on the day of sale. [Verified fact; Benchmark - external]
3. **So the jersey's first farm use is to make the counter the place where every buyer is registered,** with the guarantee as the reason, the dealer as the registrar and "Team India's sponsor" on the card. The target is a share of farm-tyre buyers registered, set inside BCG's published range, not at a figure no tyre maker has reported: half in the test, three in five at scale. [Strategic hypothesis]
4. **BCG's one sponsor-side line sorts the cricket uses:** "activations that are on 52 weeks a year" against "individual moments that are truly special". The always-on uses (the mark at the counter, the card, the trade's rewards) are the programme; the moments (hospitality at Tests, match days, tournaments) are experiments with controls, as both earlier lenses concluded by other routes. [BCG-published; Interpretation]
5. **Measurement follows BCG's four legs, laid on the test the McKinsey lens designed:** modelling on 24 months of billing, incrementality experiments (the four arms), customer insights (the owner survey, with BAI and NPS), execution metrics (registrations, stock days, claim days). BCG's own pages supply the guardrails: matched markets checked to a 95% correlation before the draw, a stated prior that "20% to 40%" of programmes "deliver marginal to negative lift", experiments as "an investment in information, not a cost to revenue", and a budget for them of "5% to 10%". [BCG-published; Strategic hypothesis]
6. **At the 2028 renewal, farm prices the asset on known buyers, not reach.** The one thing BCG's sport work says a rights holder sells is a registered base; the one thing Apollo's farm business will have by January 2028 that it has never had is one. [Interpretation]

Reading [Interpretation]: BCG's sport work is a rights holder's playbook. Read from the sponsor's side it says one thing clearly: the asset that compounds is a list of known people with a relationship, and the metric that will replace reach is relationship depth. Apollo's farm business, fourteen months into a Rs 579 crore sponsorship, has neither for farm. Both earlier lenses reached the counter as the jersey's place (M06 section 5; B06 section 3); the BCG reading adds why the counter matters beyond trust: it is the only place the buyer can be registered.

### 3.2 The design

[Strategic hypothesis; targets are planning assumptions set inside BCG's published range]

| Element | Design |
|---|---|
| Who registers | The dealer, at the counter, by QR scan on one app, with a photo of the hour meter; the rural van and the mill-gate camp as second registrars |
| Why the owner agrees | The hours promise and the cover exist only for registered tyres (M05); the card carries "Team India's sponsor" and the referral code; an SMS confirms the number |
| Why the dealer does it | Rs 100 a registration (M07 budgeted Rs 50), paid with the per-tyre bonus; settlement authority only on registered tyres; the registration count on his scorecard |
| What is recorded | Name, phone, village, tractor make and model and year, tyre size and date, hour-meter reading, the occasion ticked (`C04`), the trolley's load where registered at a camp; consent for contact about the guarantee and the next replacement |
| The number | The share of Apollo farm-tyre buyers in the tehsil registered at purchase, from registrations against billing; BCG's range for known fans is 1% to more than 60% |
| Targets | Half of buyers in the test tehsils by December 2027; three in five nationally by March 2029; the guarantee makes the covered rear tyre nearly always registered, so the measure is of all farm tyres, fronts and uncovered sizes included |
| What the base is used for | The reminder before the next season; the first-replacement call at years three to five for Apollo-fitted tractors; the referral; the haulier's pressure check; the hospitality and reward lists; the operational NPS and BAI calls; the renewal case |
| What it is not used for | Anything outside the guarantee and the next purchase; the consent says so |
| Cost | Rs 100 a registration on extra and existing sales (about Rs 0.9 crore a year at scale on 88,000 rear tyres); the app and the SMS inside M07's system line |

Why this is the jersey's use and not only the guarantee's [Interpretation]: the registration is what "Team India's sponsor" is printed on. The sponsorship pays for its standing at the counter with a list the farm business keeps after March 2028, whether or not the jersey is renewed.

## 5. Measurement: BCG's four legs on the four-arm test

### 5.1 What BCG's pages say

|---|---|---|

### 5.2 The programme's measurement, leg by leg

The test is M08's: 30 districts; four tehsils a district drawn by lot, one per arm; Arm 0 nothing, Arm 1 the offer, Arm 2 the offer and the field engine, Arm 3 all of that and the cricket moments; the primary metric is Apollo's farm replacement billing by tehsil against 24 months of history; gates in August 2027 and January 2028. [Planning assumption: M08 section 3] BCG's legs laid on it [Strategic hypothesis; the BCG rule in each row]:

| Leg | What it is here | Source of the data | Read-out | The BCG rule applied |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Modelling | A simple model of each tehsil's monthly farm billing on 24 months of history, season and district, used to set the baseline and to check that a district's four tehsils track each other | Apollo's billing | Before the draw (December 2026); monthly after | "matched-market creation"; the 95% correlation guardrail applied as a check: a district whose tehsils do not track is swapped before launch |
| Incrementality experiments | The four arms; within Arm 3, each moment against its own control (hospitality dealers against the matched set; tickets against cash) | Billing; registrations | Gate 1 (Arm 1 against Arm 0); Gate 2 (Arm 2 against 1; Arm 3 against 2) | "randomly applying a marketing activity to one (test) group while withholding it from the other"; the prior stated in the gate paper: "20% to 40%" of programmes "deliver marginal to negative lift", so a null result on the cricket arm is the expected case, not a failure of the test |
| Customer insights | The owner survey (40 owners a tehsil, three waves) with first mention, the belief that Apollo lasts as long as MRF, who is credited with the jersey, the NPS question (Bain) and the BAI behaviour questions (`C04`) | Survey | January, July, December 2027 | "Customer Insights" as a leg that explains, not decides; the gate is sales |
| Execution metrics | Registrations a week and the share of buyers registered; farm-stocking dealers; days from order to delivery; days from claim to settlement; claims per 100 covered tyres by demand space; active mechanics | The dealer app; shipment tracking; the claims system | Weekly to the steering group | "Execution metrics"; the two clocks (`C05`) and the registration share are the leading indicators |

Three guardrails from BCG's pages, added to M08 [Strategic hypothesis]:

- **State the prior.** The gate paper opens with BCG's "20% to 40%" finding and treats Arm 0's forgone sales as "an investment in information, not a cost to revenue". That is how a chief financial officer who, if he is like the 58% in Bain's survey, distrusts marketing metrics, is told what the test is for.
- **Fund the experiments explicitly.** BCG's "5% to 10%" of marketing spend for experiments: the measurement lines in `C07` (surveys, independent review, the billing model) are about Rs 2 crore of a Rs 14 crore programme, about 14%, which is above BCG's range because the programme is itself an experiment.
- **Do not over-read a season.** BCG's OEM needed "12 to16 months to show a smooth trend". Gate 1 reads one buying season and is checked for pull-forward (M08); Gate 2 reads two. The renewal decision in early 2028 will have the sales read; it will not have a settled measurement system, and the deck should say so.

### 5.3 What is not BCG's

The first-mention survey, the misattribution questions and the brand-lift read rest on the cricket evidence in Q7 and the McKinsey measures in M06, not on BCG, which publishes no brand-lift method. The matched-market guardrail is BCG's; drawing by lot within districts is M08's, kept because it removes district effects. [Verified fact; Interpretation]
