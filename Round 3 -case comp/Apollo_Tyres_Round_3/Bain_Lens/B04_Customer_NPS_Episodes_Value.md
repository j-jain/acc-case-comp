# B04. The customer: promoters, detractors, the episodes that make them, and the elements of value

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the Bain-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using four tools Bain has published: the Net Promoter System (Reichheld, HBR December 2003) and Earned Growth (HBR November-December 2021), customer episodes (Bradley and Hatherall, 2015), the Elements of Value (B2C, HBR September 2016; B2B, HBR March-April 2018) and Bain's B2B loyalty work with its Tata Steel Wire Division case (2014). It is not Bain's work. Sources: `..\research_notes\Q6_nps_outside_in_and_episodes.md`, `Q2`, `Q5`; the decision-journey analysis in `..\McKinsey_Lens\M04_Customer.md`, which this file builds on rather than repeats.

**Purpose.** To say who Apollo's promoters and detractors are and what makes them, which episode to redesign first, what the owner and the dealer value, and how owners can be made to recruit owners.

**Labels.** [Verified fact], [Fact - single source], [Reported by press], [Outside-in count], [Computed], [Planning assumption], [Interpretation], [Strategic hypothesis], [Bain-published], [Not found]. No Net Promoter Score for any Indian tyre brand is published; every NPS statement here is a proxy and says so.

---

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **Detractors are made by early physical failure and by silence.** Of 39 tractor-tyre complaints coded on the main complaint board, 23 are cuts, cracks or bursts within days or a few hundred hours, 13 are early wear, and 10 are claims refused, delayed or unanswered. Apollo's replies are templates asking for a phone number. [Outside-in count]
2. **The claim episode is slow and adversarial, for every brand.** The dealer takes the tyre in, a company engineer decides, the decision is "final and binding", and no maker publishes a settlement time; one MRF claim took two months and was rejected. The commonest real cause of early rear wear, pulling a trolley, is excluded by Goodyear in writing and was MRF's ground of rejection. [Verified fact]
3. **A tyre problem costs more satisfaction than anything else on the tractor.** Any tyre problem cuts satisfaction by 114 points on a 1,000-point scale, and only 18% of owners with uneven wear would buy the brand again. Bain's B2B research puts a promoter's lifetime value at 3 to 12 times a detractor's. [Verified fact; Bain-published]
4. **The promise owners want is risk reduction, not a slogan.** On the elements of value the owner ranks highest (quality, reduces risk, reduces cost, saves time), Apollo can lead on "reduces risk" and "responsiveness" with a cover paid at the counter, where it cannot out-reputation MRF. Bain's own Indian precedent is Tata Steel Wire's "Farming Gold": a 15-year warranty at a 25% premium, after 1,100 farmer and 200 trade interviews, which Bain reported as early results showing it among its most profitable lines. [Interpretation; Bain-published]
5. **Owners recruit owners.** In five Indo-Gangetic states (Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal), progressive farmers are the source of advice for 33% of farm households and input dealers for 34%; formal advice on machinery is sought by only 1.5%, so the tyre is chosen in conversation. Mahindra already pays tractor owners for referrals. The Bain lens adds an owner-to-owner referral and measures it as earned growth. [Verified fact; Strategic hypothesis]
6. **So H3 is supported with a correction:** the episode to fix first is the refused or unanswered claim, as the McKinsey lens says, but the cover must include cuts and bursts in the first season and trolley use, because that is what detractors actually suffer, and CEAT already offers a three-year non-defect cover that Apollo must at least match. [Interpretation]

### 2.1 What a Net Promoter view would show

No maker publishes an NPS. The proxies, with their reliability:

| Proxy | What it shows | Label |
|---|---|---|

| YouTube coding (131 brand comments, N3): Goodyear 29 positive and 7 negative; MRF 20 and 7; Apollo 19 and 5, but 13 of Apollo's 19 give no reason; CEAT 6 and 12 | Apollo has fewer reasoned promoters than Goodyear or MRF | [Outside-in count] |
| Complaint-board coding (39 tractor items, Q6): every brand draws the same complaints; no praise anywhere | Detractor drivers are shared across brands | [Outside-in count] |

Reading: Apollo in farm is a brand with few detractors because it has few replacement customers, and few promoters because nobody has asked its owners to speak. The McKinsey lens's phrase holds: fitted, then not sought, not vouched for and not bought. [Interpretation]

## 3. The episodes

Bain's definition: episodes "begin when the customer expresses a need" and "do not end until the need is met"; fix the worst and highest-value episodes first. [Bain-published] The three episodes of a tractor tyre, as the evidence shows them today:

### 3.1 "A tyre failed in the season" (urgent)

| Step | Today | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | A cut on stubble or stones, a burst under a loaded trolley, a bald tyre | Portal lists of causes; complaint timings |
| First call | The mechanic or puncture shop; a tube patch if the cut is central and under 10 mm; otherwise a retread or a new tyre from the nearest shop with stock | "Many farmers rely on mechanics for tyre repairs"; a maker's repair guide |
| Who chooses the brand | The mechanic and the shop, from what is in stock | M04 section 3 |
| Apollo today | No mechanic programme; stocking unknown; no record of the buyer | Q4; B03 section 7 |

### 3.2 "I want a claim" (the detractor factory)

| Step | Today, by the policies and the court records | Target state [Strategic hypothesis] |
|---|---|---|
| Register | By phone to Apollo Direct or through the dealer; a warranty number issued; owners and dealers sometimes do not register, and the claim is later rejected for it | The dealer registers at the counter by QR scan with a photo of the hour meter; the owner gets an SMS with the number; no registration, no sale |
| Intake | The tyre is deposited at the dealer with invoice and papers | The same, plus a photo sent from the dealer's app |
| Decision | "Company's authorized personnel"; the dealer tells the farmer "the Tyre Company will come and inspect"; two months in the MRF case; no published time | The dealer settles on his own authority up to a limit within seven days for the hours promise and the first-season cover; the engineer reviews a sample and anything above the limit |
| Outcome | Pro-rata credit on tread depth for a manufacturing defect; cuts, bursts and trolley wear excluded or rejected | Pro-rata credit for hours; a replacement or credit for a cut or burst in the first season; trolley wear covered where pressure was checked at a camp |
| After | Complaints left open; template replies | An SMS at each step; an operational NPS question after settlement; a call back on every detractor |

The McKinsey lens designed the hours promise (M05 section 3). The Bain lens keeps it and adds the first-season cut-and-burst cover and the trolley clause, because the complaint evidence says that is where detractors are made, and because CEAT's "Aayushmaan" already covers non-defect damage for three years or 50% wear, which Apollo must at least match to be chosen on the promise. [Interpretation; Verified fact for CEAT]

What it costs [Planning assumption]: the McKinsey lens reserved 2% of covered sales for the hours promise. A first-season cut-and-burst cover with claims at 3% of covered tyres and an average credit of half the price adds about 1.5%. The reserve per rear tyre rises from Rs 280 to about Rs 500, and the offer's cost from Rs 1,255 to about Rs 1,475 a rear tyre. `B07` carries this through. No maker publishes a claim rate; the test measures it. Data unavailable – requires primary research or company confirmation.

### 3.3 "I am buying before the season" (planned)

| Step | Today | Target state [Strategic hypothesis] |
|---|---|---|
| Consideration | The tyre on the tractor, MRF, what the shop stocks | Apollo on the shelf and on the board as "Team India's sponsor"; the owner's neighbour on Apollo under the guarantee |
| Evaluation | Other owners, the mechanic, pair prices, videos | A guarantee card with the hours and the cover in plain words; a mechanic who is paid to recommend; a referral credit from a neighbour |
| Closure | Pair price, warranty years, credit | Same price; hours, cover, seven-day settlement, harvest credit |
| After | Nothing | Registration; a reminder before the next season; a referral code |

## 4. The elements of value

### 4.1 The owner (consumer elements; Bain names 30 in four categories) [Interpretation; scores are our judgement on the evidence, not a survey]

| Element the owner ranks high | Why it matters here | Apollo today | MRF today | Apollo's target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quality (tyre life) | The first factor in J.D. Power; the commonest reason for praise | At par in price per hour; unproven in owners' minds | Rated highest | Prove with the hours promise and a university trial |
| Reduces risk | The commonest complaints are failures and refused claims | Seven years on defects only | Six years on defects | Lead: first-season cut-and-burst cover, trolley wear covered, settled at the dealer in seven days |
| Reduces cost | Cost per hour; Apollo lists 6 to 10% below MRF | Below MRF on price; not framed per hour | Premium | Hold price; sell hours |
| Saves time, avoids hassle | A claim today means waiting for the engineer | Same as rivals | Same | Lead on settlement time |
| Variety (one tyre for field and road) | A comment asking for it drew 141 likes | Virat is sold as farm and haulage | Shakti range | Lead the haulier segment |
| Affiliation and belonging; badge value | The jersey | Team India's sponsor, unused for farm | Bats of the leading batters since 1987 | Use at the counter and as the trade's reward; do not expect it to carry the sale |
| Provides access (credit) | Harvest-timed cash | Not offered | Not offered | Harvest credit on the entry tyre (M05) |

### 4.2 The dealer (B2B elements; Bain names 40 in five categories) [Interpretation]

| Element | What the dealer wants | Apollo today | Apollo's target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Table stakes: acceptable price, specifications, regulatory compliance | A tyre he can sell at the local price | Yes | Yes |
| Improved margin, cost reduction | Thin margin, slow stock | 10 to 16% gross, eaten by stock | A per-tyre bonus above baseline; harvest credit on stock |
| Reduced risk, stability | Not being caught between the farmer and the engineer | The engineer decides; the dealer carries the farmer's anger | Settlement authority up to a limit; a clock |
| Time savings, responsiveness | Claims and schemes without paperwork | Phone registration; four dealer apps of mixed rating | QR registration; one app; rewards paid in seven days |
| Fun and perks; recognition | Something money does not buy | Tickets and shirts went to car and bike buyers | Tickets, shirts and hospitality for the trade (B06) |
| Social responsibility, hope | Standing in his town | – | The dealer as the Team India sponsor's farm counter; health camps |

### 4.3 The precedent Bain documented

Tata Steel Wire Division sold through distributors and treated end users as one group. After "1,100 end users and more than 200 retailers, contractors and other influencers" were interviewed, it learnt that farmers prized durability, launched "Farming Gold" wire "with a 15-year warranty" at "a price 25% higher than the existing product", and "early results show Farming Gold to be one of the most profitable wire products" in its range (Bain, 2014). [Bain-published] The parallel is exact in shape: a farmer-facing durable, sold through a trade, where the promise, not the price, carried the sale. The difference is that Apollo already sits below MRF on price, so the promise can be sold at today's price without a premium. [Interpretation]

## 5. Earned growth: owners recruiting owners

### 5.1 The evidence that owners talk

| Finding | Figure | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Source of farm advice, five Indo-Gangetic states (Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal), 2018-19 (14,782 households) | Input dealers 34%; progressive farmers 33%; radio and television 8%; farmers' organisations 2.2% (FPOs 0.3%); apps under 1% | [Verified fact: NSSO 77th round, in a 2024 paper] |
| By state, progressive farmers | Uttar Pradesh 38.5%; Haryana 35.8%; Bihar 30.2%; Punjab 13.2% | [Verified fact] |
| Advice on farm machinery sought formally | 1.48% of 14,046 crop-growing households | [Verified fact] |
| Bain's car-buyer finding | 44% follow recommendations from their private circle; only 26% call the dealer their most trusted channel | [Bain-published] |
| Referral in tractors | Mahindra Shree: an invitation-only tier that pays points for "successful referrals of Mahindra Tractors", redeemable at the dealership; no uptake figures | [Verified fact: company page] |

Reading: nobody asks an expert about tyres; they ask the farmer whose tractor works most. That owner, the heavy user in `M04`, is the promoter Apollo needs, and the referral mechanism exists in the category. [Interpretation]

### 5.2 The design [Strategic hypothesis; costs are planning assumptions]

| Element | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Who can refer | Any registered owner of a guaranteed Apollo rear pair |
| How | A referral code on the guarantee card and by SMS; the new buyer gives it at registration |
| Reward | Rs 300 credit to the referrer against his next Apollo purchase, per rear pair registered; the same sum the mechanic earns, so the two do not compete |
| Cost | Paid only on a registered sale; as a credit, its cash cost is below face value and it brings the referrer back |
| Measure | Earned growth rate: the share of farm replacement revenue that comes from repeat buyers and referred buyers, which Bain treats as the accounting counterpart of NPS (HBR, November-December 2021). Baseline today: zero, because Apollo holds no record of its buyers |
| Why it is Bain's addition | The McKinsey lens registers buyers for the guarantee; this makes the registered owner a recruiter and gives management one number that says whether loyalty is earned or bought |

## 6. The Net Promoter cadence

Bain's three layers for B2B (November 2025): a strategic NPS every one to two years including against competitors; a relationship NPS yearly for key accounts; an operational NPS continuously after each episode. [Bain-published] Applied:

| Layer | Who | When | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic | 40 owners a tehsil in the test areas, with the brand of their last purchase; dealers in the same areas | A baseline wave in January 2027, then yearly | The owner survey the McKinsey lens already designed (M08 section 3.2), with the NPS question and "who do you ask about tyres" added |
| Relationship | The 300 largest farm dealers and the mechanics in the programme | Yearly, in person | The dealer programme's health |
| Operational | Every owner after a claim or a camp; every dealer after a scheme payout | Continuous, by SMS and call-back | The closed loop: a call to every detractor within a week |

Detail of the closed loop and who owns it: `B08_Delivery_Micro_battles_RAPID_Results.md`.

| Belief | Evidence today | What settles it |
