# B08. Delivery: micro-battles, decision rights, the risk of change, and the closed loop

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the Bain-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using four tools Bain has published: the micro-battles system (Allen, May 2017), RAPID decision rights (Rogers and Blenko, HBR January 2006), the Results Delivery approach to the risk of change (Bain's change-management pages), and the Net Promoter System's three-layer cadence (November 2025). It is not Bain's work. It keeps the test design, gates and supply decision of `..\McKinsey_Lens\M08_Delivery.md` and says where it changes them.

**Purpose.** To let the rural sales organisation start in December 2026, to name who decides what, to say which risks of change could sink the programme, and to make the measurement credible to a chief financial officer who, if he is like 58% of those Bain surveyed, does not trust marketing metrics.

**Labels.** [Verified fact], [Fact - single source], [Planning assumption], [Interpretation], [Strategic hypothesis], [Bain-published]. Dates, teams and thresholds are [Planning assumption] unless labelled.

---

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **Run the build as three micro-battles, not as a project plan.** Bain's definition: "discrete time-boxed initiatives that rapidly bring strategic choices to action", run by "small, cross-functional teams of seven to nine people" that "test and learn on three- to four-week cycles" and must be "masters of winning and scaling". The counter; the first replacement; the adviser and the haulier. [Bain-published; Strategic hypothesis]
2. **Keep the controlled test for the read-out.** Bain's own sponsorship playbook says that without test-and-control the result "is merely an opinion", so the four-arm, paired-tehsil design of M08 stays; the micro-battles prototype what the arms will run, from December 2026, and the test launches with the Australia Tests on 21 January 2027. H6 is therefore only half right. [Bain-published; Interpretation]
3. **Name the "D" for each decision.** Six decisions, one person with the D each, in a RAPID chart; the head of rural sales recommends, the CFO and legal agree, the micro-battle leads perform. [Bain-published; Planning assumption]
4. **Hold the risk dialogue before the first rupee.** Ten risks of change, of which three can stop the programme: competition law (Apollo was fined Rs 425.53 crore for cartel conduct in this industry; the Supreme Court dismissed the appeals in January 2022 and the penalty went back to the Commission for review that December), the sponsorship contract's rights, and a farm business that is 6% of revenue and nobody's main job. [Verified fact; Interpretation]
5. **Close the loop with the customer.** A strategic owner and dealer NPS wave in January 2027, a relationship wave for the 300 largest farm dealers, and an operational question after every claim and camp, with a call back to every detractor within a week and a monthly review of the themes. [Bain-published; Strategic hypothesis]
6. **Lock the scorecard with finance, then start on Monday.** The first 100 days begin on 9 November 2026 with the data pull, the freeze of the farm advertising allocation, the contract and competition reviews, and the three teams named. [Strategic hypothesis]

## 2. Three micro-battles

Bain's structure: a Win-Scale team runs each battle (prototype, then build the repeatable model, then deploy); leadership runs an "Amplify" loop that removes obstacles and spreads what works. Teams are "preferably dedicated full time"; here, for a Rs 300 crore business, half-time with one full-time lead. [Bain-published; Planning assumption]

| | Micro-battle 1: The counter | Micro-battle 2: The first replacement | Micro-battle 3: The adviser and the haulier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic choice it tests | Being stocked and shown, with a promise settled at the shop, moves replacement sales | Apollo can find and keep the owners whose tractors came on its tyres | A paid, verified mechanic and a covered haulier move sales in dense tehsils |
| Plays (B05) | Play 1, with the guarantee and cover | Play 2 | Plays 3 and 4 |
| Where | Ten districts: four in the cotton belt, three in Rohilkhand, three in Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh; the treated tehsils of M08's design, never the control tehsils | Ten Mahindra dealerships in the same districts | The dense tehsils of the same ten districts; the cotton belt first for hauliers |
| Team of eight | Lead: a zonal rural sales manager (full time); a warranty and claims engineer; a finance analyst; a marketing and sponsorship person; an app and systems person; a district ARD; one dealer; one farmer or mechanic as the voice of the front line | Lead: the Mahindra key-account manager; a rural sales manager; a service-data analyst; a claims engineer; a finance analyst; a Mahindra dealer principal; a district ARD; an owner | Lead: a district ARD; a farm product manager; the mechanic-programme vendor's lead; a finance analyst; a legal or compliance person; a dealer; a mechanic; a haulier |
| Cycle 1 (1 to 24 December 2026) | Kit the first 100 dealers: board, stand, two pairs on credit, guarantee cards; dealer match days during the Sri Lanka series; count what each dealer stocked on day one | Pull the fitment list by model and year; agree the service-record protocol with Mahindra; list the first 500 tractors | KYC and enrol the first 110 mechanics by IVR; design the camp; draft the cover's trolley clause with legal |
| Cycle 2 (26 December to 22 January) | QR registration live on one dealer app; settlement authority of Rs 5,000 a claim set; first claims settled and timed | Returning-owner cards issued at the first camps; retention baseline from the first 500 | First pressure-and-load camps; the first hauliers registered; the first referrals paid within 24 hours |
| Cycle 3 (23 January to 19 February) | Dealer audit: stock, display, registrations a week; hospitality list for Nagpur drawn and matched controls set | Mahindra dealer principals at Nagpur; the first replacements recorded | District contest round one; cover claims by cause read weekly |
| Cycle 4 (20 February to 19 March) | The repeatable model written: the kit, the rules, the numbers, the cost per dealer | The model written: how to find the owner, what to offer, who pays | The model written: enrolment, payout, cover terms, camp calendar |
| Scale (April to July 2027) | All 30 first-wave districts; the first buying season | 100 Mahindra dealers | All dense tehsils in the 30 districts |
| Deploy (from October 2027) | National, on Gate 1 | National, with other tractor makers if they share records | Where Gate 2 funds hubs |
| The one number | Farm-stocking dealers; registrations a week | Share of fitted owners who return | Active mechanics; registered hauliers; claims per 100 covered tyres by use |
| Amplify loop | A monthly steering meeting chaired by the India business head; obstacles removed in the week they are raised | Same | Same |

**How this fits M08's four arms** [Interpretation]: the micro-battles prototype the treatments in the treated tehsils of ten districts between December and March; the controls are untouched; the test launches on 21 January 2027 in all 30 districts with the prototyped version, as M08 planned, and the gates in August 2027 and January 2028 read the arms. Arm 1 is the counter and the promise; Arm 2 adds the hubs, the adviser and the haulier; Arm 3 adds the experiences of `B06`. The difference from M08 is that the first six weeks of learning happen before the launch, on a weekly cadence, and that what scales is a written repeatable model, not a plan.

## 3. RAPID: who has the D

Bain's roles: Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide; the D is "the single point of accountability who commits the organization to action"; "A good decision executed well often beats a brilliant one implemented poorly." [Bain-published] Roles below are by title; Apollo would put names to them. [Planning assumption]

| Decision | Recommend | Agree | Perform | Input | **Decide** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Approve the programme, the scorecard and the reallocation of Rs 17 crore within advertising and promotion | Head of rural sales | CFO; legal | The three micro-battle leads | Group head of marketing; head of supply chain; key-account head | **President, India business (or the vice-chairman and managing director)** |
| 2. The guarantee's hours, the cut-and-burst cover, the trolley clause and the reserve | Micro-battle 1 lead, with farm product | CFO (reserve); legal (terms) | Claims engineers; dealers | The university trial; mechanics | **Head of farm product** |
| 3. Dealer settlement authority and the per-tyre bonus | Micro-battle 1 lead | Finance (controls); legal (competition law) | Zonal sales; dealers | AITDF-type trade feedback through the relationship NPS | **Head of rural sales** |
| 4. Uses of the sponsorship: marks at the shop, hospitality list, tournaments, player days, the cash-against-tickets split | Micro-battle leads | Legal (the BCCI contract); CFO | Sponsorship team; district ARDs | BCCI liaison; broadcaster for screenings | **Head of brand and sponsorship** |
| 5. Supply: tyres ring-fenced for the test and FY29 volume from allocation and off-take | Head of farm product | CFO | Plants; off-take partners | Rural sales | **Head of supply chain** |
| 6. The gates: what scales in October 2027 and FY29 | The test office with the independent reviewer | CFO | Micro-battle leads | Dealers; Mahindra | **The steering group's chair (the India business head)** |

Two of Bain's four bottlenecks apply here: function against function (marketing holds the jersey, rural sales holds the dealers) and centre against business unit (farm is a line inside a company run by truck and car). The chart puts the D for money and gates with the business head, and the D for the jersey's farm uses with brand, so that neither can block the other without saying so. [Interpretation]

## 4. The risk of change

Bain: "More than 80% of major change efforts fall short of their goals"; its Results Delivery approach "predicts, measures, and manages the risk of change from day one" with a "proprietary risk dialogue". [Bain-published] The dialogue, as we would run it, without Bain's tools. The "why it is real here" column cites facts carrying the labels of the files named (Q3, Q4, Q6, Q7, M02); the guards and the stop verdicts are [Planning assumption] and [Interpretation]:

| # | Risk | Why it is real here | Guard | Owner | Can it stop the programme? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Competition law** | The CCI's 2012 record of this trade carries the makers' own submissions on dealer margins and a dissenting finding of tie-ins, exclusive supply and resale price maintenance; the CCI fined Apollo Rs 425.53 crore for cartel conduct (2018; appeals dismissed by the Supreme Court in January 2022; penalty remanded for review in December 2022) | No minimum resale price; no tie-in of tubes or fronts to the guaranteed rear; no exclusivity condition; no exchange of scheme terms with rivals; written legal clearance before tranche 1 | Legal | **Yes** |
| 2 | **The sponsorship contract** | The rights package is not public; marks at the shop, ticket allocations, hospitality seats, player days and screenings are assumed | Contract review in weeks 1 to 6; the offer works without any of them | Head of brand and sponsorship | **Yes, for the cricket uses only** |
| 3 | **Farm is nobody's main job** | 6% of domestic revenue; the 2024 rural plan did not name farm; the Farm Zone was dropped after two outlets | A full-time lead per micro-battle; the D for money with the business head; the one number per battle in the monthly review | India business head | **Yes** |
| 4 | **Dealer resentment** | The dealers' federation says benefits go to the "creamy layer"; a 2007 allegation that Apollo favoured 200 to 300 dealers | Per-tyre rewards open to every dealer who stocks farm; hospitality earned on farm sales and said so; the relationship NPS as the early warning | Head of rural sales | No, but it erodes the result |
| 5 | **Claims abuse and the cover's cost** | No claim rate is published; tampered hour meters; loose settlement | A cap per dealer; photo at registration and claim; audit of a sample; the narrowing rule: if cover claims exceed the reserve for two months, the cover narrows or pauses before the national launch | Head of farm product | No, if the rule is kept |
| 6 | **Supply** | India plants at 92% utilisation; farm short in 2026; no farm capacity being added; 20 to 25% of farm volume to be outsourced | Ring-fence the test volume now; no tyre under the guarantee unless Apollo made it and holds its wear data | Head of supply chain | No, at the test's scale |
| 7 | **Too many tools at the counter** | Four dealer apps today, one rated 2.5 stars | One app for registration, claims and rewards; IVR for the mechanic | Systems lead | No |
| 8 | **Measurement not believed** | A new CFO; 58% of CFOs distrust marketing metrics (Bain, 2026) | The scorecard locked with finance before tranche 1; an independent reviewer at each gate; sell-out from billing, not survey, as the gate measure | CFO's nominee on the test office | No, if locked |
| 9 | **A weak farm year** | The 2026 monsoon 13% short; tyre prices up 15%; owners will retread (GST on retreads now 5%) | Paired tehsils share the year; read run-rates, not totals; the harvest credit | Test office | No |
| 10 | **Rivals copy** | CEAT already offers a non-defect cover and is pushing off-highway; MRF can match any scheme | Settlement speed at the dealer and the record of buyers are the parts that take a year to copy; do not follow on price | Head of farm product | No |

A tenth of the budget is held as contingency for the guards (B07). [Planning assumption]

## 5. The closed loop

Bain's three Net Promoter layers for B2B: strategic (every one to two years, against competitors), relationship (yearly, key accounts), operational and customer-journey (continuous). [Bain-published]

| Layer | Who is asked | When | The question added to the McKinsey-lens survey | Who closes the loop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic | 40 owners a tehsil in the test areas; dealers in the same areas | January 2027 baseline; July and December 2027 | "How likely are you to recommend the brand of your last rear tyres?" and "Whom do you ask about tyres?" | The test office; themes to the steering group |
| Relationship | The 300 largest farm dealers; the hospitality dealers and their matched set; enrolled mechanics | Yearly, in person; a short wave after each season | "How likely are you to recommend stocking Apollo farm tyres to another dealer?" and the three things Apollo should change | The zonal sales manager calls every detractor dealer within a week |
| Operational | Every owner after a claim, a camp or a registration; every mechanic after a payout | Continuous, by SMS and a call back | "Was your claim settled within seven days?"; "Would you recommend the Apollo tyre?" | The dealer, then the district ARD, within a week; the monthly review changes a term or a step when a theme repeats |

The loop is the farm business's first record of what its buyers think. Today it has none. [Interpretation]

**Status of H6.** Partly supported. Micro-battles are the right way to build and scale (weekly cadence, a written repeatable model, a front-line voice on the team), and they bring the start forward to December 2026. But Bain's own sponsorship playbook demands test-and-control, so the four-arm, paired design is kept for the read-out; the Day-1 answer's "instead of" becomes "in front of".
