# C08. Delivery: the odds of the programme, scored and improved

Prepared 7 November 2026. Part of the BCG-lens re-analysis of the Apollo Round 3 case.

**What this file is.** Our own analysis using three things BCG has published on making change happen: the DICE assessment of change programmes (BCG's live tool at dice.bcg.com; the 2021 report "How the Best Set Up Their Program Journey"; the 2005 Harvard Business Review origin as bcg.com cites it), Smart Simplicity's six rules (Morieux and Tollman, 2014 and 2016), and BCG's published figures on why transformations and pilots fail. It is not BCG's work; DICE and Smart Simplicity are BCG's registered marks, used here only to score and improve our own programme. Sources: `..\research_notes\S7_delivery_dice_simplicity.md`. The test design, gates, supply decision, micro-battles, RAPID chart, risk dialogue and closed loop are in `..\McKinsey_Lens\M08_Delivery.md` and `..\Bain_Lens\B08_Delivery_Micro_battles_RAPID_Results.md`; this file keeps them and says what changes.

**Purpose.** To score the programme the two earlier lenses designed on BCG's own scale, say what moves it from "touch and go" to "set up for success", apply the six rules to the people who will run it, size the office that runs it, and say what the first 100 days look like with that done.

**Labels.** [Verified fact], [Planning assumption], [Interpretation], [Strategic hypothesis], [BCG-published]. Dates, teams and scores are [Planning assumption] unless labelled.

---

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **BCG's DICE score for the programme as designed is 15 to 17: "touch and go", at the edge of "in danger of failing".** Seven months between the launch and the first gate (D = 3), a part-time team (I = 2 to 3), a farm business that is "nobody's main job" (C1 = 3), dealers and mechanics willing but company engineers reluctant to lose claim authority (C2 = 2), and extra effort loaded on the counter (E = 2). The formula is BCG's: "DICE = D + 2I + 2C1 + C2 + E"; lower is better. [BCG-published; Interpretation]
2. **Three changes take it to about 10, "set up for success":** learning milestones every three to four weeks (the Bain lens's micro-battle cycles, kept, so D = 1); the India business head's visible, stated commitment with the "D" for money and gates (C1 = 1); and a counter that asks nothing extra of the dealer beyond one QR scan and an app that settles (E = 1). Each step on commitment is worth two points; nothing else is worth as much. [BCG-published; Strategic hypothesis]
3. **Smart Simplicity's six rules say who must cooperate and what makes them.** The integrator is the district ARD, who sits between the dealer, the mechanic, the claims engineer and the rural van; he needs power (settlement authority to delegate, stock to allocate) and an interest (his scorecard on registered sales and days-to-deliver). "Reward those who cooperate" is the per-tyre bonus paid to the dealer who registers; "Extend the shadow of the future" is the call-back to every detractor and the renewal in 2028. [BCG-published; Strategic hypothesis]
4. **Run it with a two-person transformation office, not a committee.** BCG's largest study ("more than 3,000 transformations") finds "over 70% of transformations fail" and that a dedicated office brings "a 50% increase in value realization"; its five stage gates map onto M08's two gates and the prototype cycles. For a Rs 300 crore business the office is two people to March 2028, then one. [BCG-published; Planning assumption]
5. **Expect one arm to fail.** BCG: "Even in a well-run program, 20% of initiatives won't achieve their desired impact, and even more initiatives (30%) will typically be at risk." The gate paper says so in advance, so that a null result on the cricket arm or the field engine is read as the test working, not the programme failing. [BCG-published; Interpretation]
6. **Measure the test in tyres sold and claims paid, not in activity.** BCG's 2026 warning on pilots that never scale is about AI, but its diagnosis transfers: pilots fail when firms "measure results in terms of activity (hours saved, tasks automated)" and spread themselves thin; the cure is "going deeper on a smaller number of initiatives". Thirty districts done fully, not three hundred done thinly. [BCG-published, AI context; Interpretation]

## 4. Smart Simplicity: who must cooperate, and what makes them

### 4.1 The six rules at source

The six rules, as headed in "Stop Trying to Control People or Make Them Happy" (Morieux and Tollman, 23 April 2014): 1 "Understand what your people do"; 2 "Reinforce integrators"; 3 "Increase the total quantity of power"; 4 "Increase reciprocity"; 5 "Extend the shadow of the future"; 6 "Reward those who cooperate". From the BCG booklet "Introducing Six Simple Rules of Smart Simplicity" (Morieux and Tollman; undated; the book is 2014): the integrator is "the people and units who bring others together and drive processes"; "An effective integrator has both an interest in making others cooperate and the power to impel them to do so."; in "the top 20 percent of complicated organizations" managers spend "More than 40 percent of their time writing reports" and "Between 30 and 60 percent of their total work hours in coordination meetings"; "the number of procedures goes up 6.7 percent a year". From BCG's Smart Simplicity capability page: complicatedness is "the number of procedures, vertical layers, interface structures, coordination bodies, decision approvals, and other add-ons". [BCG-published]

### 4.2 Applied to the farm counter

[Strategic hypothesis throughout]

| Rule | Who and what | What the programme does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Understand what your people do | The dealer sells six brands, holds Rs 3 to 8 lakh of stock, carries the farmer's anger when a claim stalls, and uses four Apollo apps; the claims engineer decides claims he did not see; the rural officer is measured on calls | Forty dealer interviews and the DICE workshop before the design is frozen (the first 100 days); one app; the engineer's role rewritten as sample reviewer |
| 2 Reinforce integrators | The district ARD is the integrator: he alone touches the dealer, the mechanic, the camp, the van and the claims review. Today he has a target and no power | Give him the power: settlement authority to delegate to dealers up to the limit, stock allocation in the buying seasons, the camp calendar; and the interest: his scorecard on registered sales, days-to-deliver and claims settled in seven days |
| 3 Increase the total quantity of power | Settling claims is zero-sum today: the engineer decides, the dealer waits | Create new power rather than move it: the dealer settles small claims; the engineer reviews the sample and decides the large ones and the pattern; both gain |
| 4 Increase reciprocity | The dealer wants stock and fast settlement; Apollo wants registration and display | Each depends on the other: no registration, no settlement authority; no display, no first stock on credit; the 48-hour promise is Apollo's side of the bargain |
| 5 Extend the shadow of the future | A refused claim has no consequence for anyone at Apollo today; the owner simply goes elsewhere | The call-back to every detractor within a week (B08), the operational NPS and BAI, the dealer NPS, the hospitality list earned on farm sales, and the 2028 renewal, which farm's registered base feeds |
| 6 Reward those who cooperate | Dealers are paid on volume; engineers on claims refused; officers on calls | The per-tyre bonus to the dealer who registers; the officer paid on registered sales and days-to-deliver; the engineer measured on settlement days and claim accuracy on the sample |

What this removes [Interpretation]: the steering group of M08 and the RAPID chart of B08 stay, but the programme adds no layer, no new approval and no new report. BCG's finding that "the number of procedures goes up 6.7 percent a year" (the Smart Simplicity booklet) is the reason the office (section 5) is two people and the review is an hour.
